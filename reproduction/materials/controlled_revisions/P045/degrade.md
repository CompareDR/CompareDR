# Relocation Report: Leaving San Francisco on a $120K Remote Salary — Ranked Top 3 Cities

## TL;DR

- **Pittsburgh, PA is the #1 pick**, followed by **#2 Minneapolis, MN** and **#3 St. Louis, MO**. All three clear the user's four hard filters: 40%+ cheaper than SF including rent, genuine fiber/gigabit broadband, walkable neighborhoods scoring 78–93 on Walk Score with real trail/park access, and a major airport within ~30 minutes with nonstop flights to SFO.
- **The biggest savings is rent, not taxes.** A 1-bedroom in a walkable SF neighborhood now runs ~$4,300 at the city median (Zumper), and a 2-bedroom just crossed $6,020 for the first time on record (Zumper via NBC Bay Area, July 2026); comparable walkable neighborhoods in Pittsburgh (Shadyside), Minneapolis (Uptown) and St. Louis (Central West End) run ~$1,400–$2,050 — saving roughly $22,000–$34,000/year on housing alone. State/local income-tax savings on $120K are real but modest (roughly +$1,400 to +$3,200/year of take-home vs. California).
- **Trade-offs are mostly weather and, for Pittsburgh/St. Louis, diversity/scale.** Expect real Midwestern/Appalachian winters (snow, gray skies), tornado/heat risk in St. Louis, and a smaller-city feel than the Bay Area. The single biggest financial risk is **employer geo-based pay adjustment** — confirm your salary is protected before you move.

## Key Findings

1. 1. **The $120K goes dramatically further.** Per Numbeo, you'd need ~$10,388 in San Francisco to match the lifestyle of ~$6,100 in Pittsburgh (rent included) [Numbeo][ref-01] — roughly a 41% cost reduction. St. Louis is ~41.5% cheaper including rent (Numbeo). Minneapolis is cheaper on housing (~15–18% below the U.S. average [Apartments.com][ref-02] per Apartments.com/C2ER) though it carries higher taxes. All three easily beat the 30%-cheaper threshold.
2. 2. **Rent is the headline.** SF's 1BR median is now $4,300, up 25.7% year-over-year and "just $200 behind New York City's $4,500" (Zumper National Rent Report); the SF 2BR median hit $6,020 in July 2026 — the first time Zumper has recorded SF above $6,000. The recommended walkable neighborhoods elsewhere: Shadyside/Squirrel Hill Pittsburgh 1BR ~$1,400–$2,200; Minneapolis Uptown 1BR ~$1,400–$1,900; St. Louis Central West End 1BR ~$1,494 and 2BR ~$1,911 (Apartments.com).
3. 3. **Healthcare cost varies by state and matters.** 2026 ACA benchmark (2nd-lowest silver, age 40), per KFF via Becker's Payer Issues (Nov 20, 2025): Pennsylvania $572/mo (rank 31), Missouri $605/mo (rank 28), Minnesota $448/mo (rank 49) — against a U.S. average of $625 and CA at $570. **Minnesota's $448 is the 3rd-lowest of all 51 jurisdictions, behind only Maryland ($414) and New Hampshire ($401).**
4. 4. **Broadband is genuinely strong in all three**, and elite in the wildcard (Chattanooga). Pittsburgh has ~93.3% fiber availability (Verizon Fios + Xfinity gig cable); St. Louis has AT&T Fiber (symmetrical up to 5 Gbps, from ~$35/mo) + Spectrum; Minneapolis has widespread fiber/cable competition.
5. 5. **Take-home pay improves modestly.** A single filer on $120K nets ~$84,518 in California [Snappaycalc][ref-03] (federal+state+FICA, incl. CA SDI). In a no-income-tax state that same person nets ~$93,250. [Snappaycalc][ref-03] Pittsburgh, Minneapolis and St. Louis all sit between those poles because each levies state (and, in PA/MO cities, local) income tax — netting an estimated ~$1,400–$3,200 more per year than SF, not the full ~$8,700 gap.

## Methodology

Candidate cities from the user's list (and beyond) were screened against the four **hard filters**, in order:

1. 1. **Cost of living ≥30% below SF, weighted to rent and healthcare.** Cities failing the 30% rent-inclusive test (Denver, Austin, Salt Lake City, Boise, Portland OR, etc.) were cut.
2. 2. **High-speed, reliable internet.** Cities without meaningful fiber competition or gig availability were penalized.
3. 3. **Walkability + nature.** At least one neighborhood with Walk Score ≥60 AND named trail/park access was required. Cities with citywide Walk Score in the 20s–40s and no walkable core were cut even if cheap.
4. 4. **Major airport ≤90 minutes with nonstop SFO/OAK/SJC service.** Verified via FlightConnections/airline route data and IATA hub status.

Screening information was drawn from the following separate sources:

- BroadbandNow city pages: fiber availability %, provider count, and gig availability; provider footprints.
- Numbeo SF-vs-city comparisons including rent; C2ER/Salary.com composite indices.
- Walk Score verification on walkscore.com.
- Zumper/Apartments.com/RentCafe neighborhood rents; KFF 2026 ACA benchmark premiums.

Survivors were then ranked on the total package — magnitude of savings, quality/number of qualifying walkable neighborhoods, breadth of nature access, airport connectivity, healthcare quality, tech-job depth (downside protection), and honestly-reported weather/diversity trade-offs. Assumptions per the brief: single person or couple, no kids, renting initially, optimizing to preserve the $120K.

## Details

### #1 — Pittsburgh, PA

**Why it wins:** Pittsburgh is the rare city that clears all four filters comfortably *and* offers Bay-Area-transplant amenities (a real food/coffee scene, major universities, a growing AI/robotics tech cluster) at genuinely low cost. Numbeo pegs it ~41% cheaper than SF including rent.

- **Cost & rent:** Salary.com/C2ER puts Pittsburgh's composite index around 88 (~12% below U.S. average, with housing far below that). Walkable **Shadyside (Walk Score 91)** and **Squirrel Hill (88)** offer 1BRs from ~$1,400–$2,200; [Ultrarix][ref-05] the Strip District and Downtown score 95+ (Walker's Paradise) at somewhat higher rents. Against SF's $4,300 1BR median, that's roughly $2,500/month less — on the order of $30,000/year in rent savings.
- **Internet:** ~93.3% fiber availability (BroadbandNow). **Verizon Fios** (100% fiber, near-symmetrical, ranked #1 J.D. Power East region 13 straight years) competes with **Xfinity** gig cable (up to 2 Gbps); Fios covers ~95–97% of the city [FindBetterInternet][ref-06] per FindBetterInternet. Strong competition, real gig, good reliability.
- **Walkability + nature:** Beyond the high Walk Scores, Pittsburgh is unusually green — **Frick Park (644 acres, Tranquil/Falls Ravine trails), Schenley Park (456 acres, Panther Hollow), Emerald View Park** on Mt. Washington, plus **North Park (~13 mi)** and the **Rachel Carson Trail (35+ mi)**. Ohiopyle State Park and the Laurel Highlands are ~1.5 hrs for bigger hikes.
- **Airport:** **Pittsburgh International (PIT)**, ~25 minutes from the East End neighborhoods, serves ~64 destinations. [FlightsFrom][ref-07] **United flies nonstop PIT–SFO** (~11–14 weekly, ~5h25m); [Direct Flights][ref-08] many connecting options on Southwest, Alaska, JetBlue. [Google Flights][ref-09]
- **Taxes:** PA flat **3.07%** income tax, but Pittsburgh adds a **3% local earned-income tax** (1% city + 2% school) — an important caveat. Sales tax 7% (Allegheny County); groceries and clothing exempt. Net take-home on $120K ≈ $85,900, only ~$1,400/year above SF because of that local tax. **The win is rent and daily costs, not taxes.**
- **Weather (the real version):** Humid continental/subtropical. July highs ~85°F with 70%+ humidity; January highs ~39°F, lows ~23°F; **~44" of snow** and notably **gray/cloudy winters** (cloud cover up to ~70% in winter). [Weather Atlas][ref-10] Late spring through early fall is lovely. Seasonal-affective risk is real given winter cloudiness. Low tornado/hurricane risk; occasional river flooding.
- **Diversity & social climate:** This is Pittsburgh's weak spot. The metro is one of the less racially diverse and lower foreign-born (~mid-single-digit %) large metros, and a widely cited 2019 city equity report found poor outcomes for Black residents, especially Black women. The city proper is progressive and LGBTQ-friendly; the surrounding region is more mixed politically. A Bay Area transplant will notice less international diversity and a more insular, neighborhood-rooted social culture.
- **Amenities & jobs:** Strong food scene (Lawrenceville, the Strip), excellent coffee culture, museums (Warhol, Carnegie), pro sports, and — importantly for downside protection — a real tech/robotics/AI job market anchored by Carnegie Mellon, Pitt, UPMC, Google, Duolingo, and Aurora. Healthcare access is excellent (UPMC and Allegheny Health Network; UPMC is nationally ranked).
- **Locals complain about:** gray winters, hilly/aggressive driving and confusing roads, the 3% local wage tax, and slow population growth.

### #2 — Minneapolis, MN

**Why #2:** The best "big-city amenities + walkability + outdoors" package on the list, with the **cheapest healthcare** of the three and elite airport connectivity — held back only by brutal winters and higher taxes.

- **Cost & rent:** Housing runs ~15–18% below the U.S. average (Apartments.com/C2ER). **Uptown (Walk Score 93)**, **Downtown/North Loop (85–95)**, and **Northeast** offer walkable, transit-served living; Uptown 1BRs ~$1,400–$1,900, 2BRs ~$1,900–$2,500. [Movewithmint][ref-11] Citywide average rent ~$1,391–$1,641 depending on source.
- **Internet:** Widespread fiber and cable competition (Xfinity, CenturyLink/Quantum Fiber, US Internet fiber in parts of the metro) with gig availability; a reliable remote-work market.
- **Walkability + nature:** Arguably the best urban nature of the three — the **Chain of Lakes (Bde Maka Ska, Lake Harriet), the Grand Rounds Scenic Byway, Minnehaha Falls/Regional Park, and the Mississippi River Gorge trails** are all in-city. Minneapolis consistently ranks among the most bike-friendly U.S. cities. Bigger wilderness (North Shore, Boundary Waters) is a few hours north.
- **Airport:** **MSP (Minneapolis–St. Paul International)**, ~20 minutes via light rail (Blue Line) or car, is **Delta's 2nd/3rd-largest hub** with ~120–168 nonstop destinations. **~51 weekly nonstops to SFO** (Delta, United, Sun Country) — the best Bay Area connectivity on the list.
- **Taxes:** Minnesota is the highest-tax of the three — progressive brackets up to 9.85%, with $120K landing in the 7.85% bracket (no local income tax). Estimated take-home ~$85,500, roughly break-even-to-slightly-positive vs. SF. **You move here for lifestyle and amenities, not tax savings** — though the cheapest ACA premiums in the group ($448/mo benchmark) claw some of that back if you buy your own coverage.
- **Weather (the real version):** This is the defining trade-off. Hot-summer humid continental: July highs ~83°F (humid), but **~52" of snow/year and winters running November–March** with subzero stretches (January mean daily max ~24°F). Upside: the Twin Cities get more mid-winter sunshine than most northern cities, and the culture fully embraces winter (skiing, skating, the skyway system downtown). SAD risk is real; strong indoor/third-place culture compensates.
- **Diversity & social climate:** More diverse than Pittsburgh, with significant East African (Somali), Hmong, and Latino communities and higher foreign-born share. Minnesota is among the most **LGBTQ-friendly** states (strong legal protections; Loring Park, Northeast, Uptown as hubs). Politically blue/progressive city in a purple-trending state. Note: the metro drew national attention around 2020 policing/racial-justice events — worth understanding the local context.
- **Amenities & jobs:** Excellent — top-tier theater (Guthrie), music legacy (First Avenue), acclaimed food, and a deep corporate/tech job base (Target, UnitedHealth, 3M, Best Buy, U.S. Bancorp, plus Mayo Clinic in the region). Strong healthcare access and quality.
- **Locals complain about:** winter length, high taxes, mosquitoes in summer, and post-2020 downtown recovery.

### #3 — St. Louis, MO

**Why #3:** The **cheapest of the three and the best take-home pay**, with a genuinely walkable, park-adjacent showcase neighborhood (Central West End) and nonstop SFO service — ranked third mainly because of milder-but-riskier weather (tornadoes), a smaller tech scene, and crime perception that requires neighborhood-level nuance.

- **Cost & rent:** ~41.5% cheaper than SF including rent (Numbeo). **Central West End (Walk Score 78; individual addresses up to 88)** offers 1BRs ~$1,494 [Lizzy Dooley][ref-12] and 2BRs ~$1,911 (Apartments.com) [Apartments.com][ref-04] — walkable to Forest Park, restaurants, and the Barnes-Jewish/WashU medical campus. **Lafayette Square (Walk Score 82)**, **The Hill (79)**, and **Shaw (68)** are other strong walkable options. Citywide, housing is ~46% below the national average. [Lizzy Dooley][ref-12]
- **Internet:** **AT&T Fiber** offers fully symmetrical plans from 300 Mbps up to **5 Gbps** (from ~$35/mo; [Attfiber-internet][ref-13] 1 Gig ~$50/mo), competing with **Spectrum** cable (~100% coverage, up to 2 Gbps). i3 Broadband reaches up to 8 Gbps symmetrical in parts of the metro. [businesswire][ref-14] Fiber is strong in CWE/Clayton; weaker in north city.
- **Walkability + nature:** **Forest Park (~1,326 acres — larger than Central Park)** sits on CWE's doorstep with a free zoo/art museum, trails, and cycling loops. **Tower Grove Park (289 acres)** [Tower Grove Park][ref-15] and the adjacent **Missouri Botanical Garden** anchor the south side; **Castlewood State Park (~1,818 acres)** on the Meramec River [Wikipedia][ref-16] is ~20–25 miles southwest for real hiking/mountain biking.
- **Airport:** **St. Louis Lambert International (STL)**, ~20 minutes downtown (MetroLink light rail connects), serves ~65 nonstop destinations. [FlightConnections][ref-17] **Southwest (its largest carrier) and United both fly nonstop STL–SFO** (~4h15m).
- **Taxes:** Best of the three. Missouri top rate **4.7%** (2025), [remoteworklaws.com][ref-18] effective ~4% on $120K, plus the **1% St. Louis city earnings tax** [TakeHomeTax][ref-19] [City of St. Louis][ref-20] (~$1,200/yr). Estimated take-home ~$87,700 — about **$3,200/year more than SF**. Missouri also newly allows a 100% capital-gains subtraction from state AGI (2025). [remoteworklaws.com][ref-18] Sales tax base 4.225% + local add-ons.
- **Healthcare:** MO 2026 ACA benchmark $605/mo [Becker's Payer Issues][ref-21] (age 40, mid-pack). Quality is a genuine strength: **Barnes-Jewish Hospital / Washington University** is #1 in Missouri, nationally ranked in ~11 specialties [BJC HealthCare][ref-22] and a frequent U.S. News Honor Roll hospital — located right in Central West End.
- **Weather (the real version):** Humid continental. Summers are **hot and muggy (July highs ~89°F)**; [Weather Spark][ref-23] winters milder than the northern picks (Jan lows ~23°F, only **~16" snow**), [BestPlaces][ref-24] ~202 sunny days. [BestPlaces][ref-24] **Key risk: tornadoes** (a destructive tornado hit the city in 2025), severe spring storms, extreme summer heat, and river flooding.
- **Diversity & safety:** One of the most even Black/White splits of any large U.S. city (~44% White, [Missouri Demograph…][ref-25] ~42–43% Black, ~5% Hispanic, ~3.5% Asian; foreign-born ~6.8%). [Data USA][ref-26] **Crime nuance is essential:** St. Louis City is an independent city separate from the much-safer, larger county, so per-capita city crime rates overstate metro risk. The city recorded **141 homicides in 2025, a 12-year low** — a 46.8% drop from the 263 recorded in 2020 (SLMPD year-end CompStat), with overall Part I crime down 16% versus 2024. Crime is concentrated in specific north-city areas; CWE, Clayton, and the Grove are far safer. Politically the city is blue; Missouri statewide is red, which affects some policy (e.g., abortion, LGBTQ legislation) — a real culture-shift consideration from the Bay Area.
- **Amenities & jobs:** Strong, underrated food scene, Forest Park's free institutions, robust brewery/music culture, and low cost of entry for third places. Tech scene is smaller than Pittsburgh's but growing (Square/Block, Boeing, Centene, WashU/Cortex innovation district). Best value for anyone optimizing pure dollars.
- **Locals complain about:** crime headlines/reputation, hot humid summers, tornado season, sprawl outside the walkable core, and civic fragmentation.

## Honorable Mentions (narrowly missed)

- **Chicago, IL** — World-class walkability (Wicker Park 96, [ApartmentList][ref-27] Logan Square, West Loop), superb transit (L trains), O'Hare/Midway with abundant SFO nonstops, Illinois **flat 4.95% tax and no city income tax**. Missed the top 3 because it only marginally clears the 30%-cheaper test — 1BRs average ~$1,940–$2,050 [Apartments.com][ref-28] and COL is ~14% *above* the national average, the priciest survivor. Excellent if walkability/amenities outrank maximum savings.
- **Chattanooga, TN** — The user's fiber flag is justified: **EPB municipal fiber delivers symmetrical service to 95.3% of the city at up to 25 Gbps** (it launched the nation's first community-wide 25 Gbps service in 2022; residential plans run $57.99 for 300 Mbps up to $1,500/mo for 25 Gbps) and **Tennessee has no income tax**. But it fails the walkability filter as a whole — citywide Walk Score is **29 ("car-dependent")**, the most walkable ZIP (37403) is only 72, [Walk Score][ref-29] transit is minimal, and the airport is small with no SFO nonstop. Great for a car-owning outdoors lover; wrong for a car-free requirement.
- **Buffalo/Rochester, NY** — Very cheap, walkable pockets, cheap housing; held back by lake-effect snow, smaller airports with limited/no SFO nonstops, and slower job markets.
- **Cincinnati / Cleveland / Columbus, OH** — Affordable and walkable in spots, but Ohio cities layer **municipal income taxes (~2–2.5%)** that erode the take-home advantage, and SFO connectivity is thinner.
- **Richmond, VA / Philadelphia, PA** — Both walkable and reasonably connected; Philly is cheaper-than-SF but carries a ~3.75% city wage tax and higher crime perception, while Richmond's airport SFO connectivity and overall savings edge slightly below the top 3.

## Cities to Rule Out (and why)

- **Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins CO; Austin TX; Salt Lake City UT; Boise ID; Portland OR; Asheville NC; Nashville TN** — These popular remote-work destinations **fail the 30%-cheaper-than-SF test on a rent-inclusive basis** (Denver, Austin, SLC, Boise, Portland are only ~15–25% cheaper and rising). Nashville rents have actually dropped (~$1,520 1BR) [Zumper][ref-30] but its citywide walkability and transit are weak and it's car-dependent.
- **San Antonio, Dallas, Houston, Phoenix/Tucson, Albuquerque, and similar Sun Belt metros** — Cheap enough on paper, but **car-dependent sprawl** fails the Walk Score ≥60 + car-free requirement outside tiny cores; extreme heat and (TX) grid/water concerns.
- **Knoxville, Greenville SC, Sioux Falls, Des Moines, Omaha** — Affordable but **fail walkability/transit** citywide and/or have limited airport connectivity to the Bay Area. (Chattanooga is covered above as an honorable-mention broadband standout that still fails walkability.)
- **Providence, Portland ME, Burlington VT, New Haven, Hartford** — Walkable and pleasant but **New England costs and taxes** shrink the savings, and SFO nonstops are limited.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Protect the salary first (do this before anything else).** Confirm in writing whether your employer uses **geo-based pay bands**. Some tech companies cut pay when you relocate from a Tier-1 metro to a lower-COL one; if yours does, model the new number *before* committing, because a 10–15% cut changes the math (though all three cities still win on rent). Benchmark that would change the recommendation: if a pay cut pushes your net below ~$95K, lean toward the **highest-take-home, lowest-rent option (St. Louis)**.

**Stage 2 — Pick based on your winter tolerance and diversity priorities:**

- Want the best all-around balance and a real tech safety net → **Pittsburgh**.
- Want big-city amenities, the best airport, cheapest health insurance, and can embrace hard winters → **Minneapolis**.
- Want maximum dollars saved, mild-ish winters, and top hospitals, and are comfortable doing neighborhood-level due diligence on safety → **St. Louis**.

**Stage 3 — Scout remotely, then visit.** Use Walk Score's map view to test exact addresses (aim ≥70), pull address-level fiber availability on BroadbandNow/provider sites (don't trust city averages — confirm your building), and check crime at the neighborhood level (especially in St. Louis). Spend 4–7 days on the ground in **late winter (Feb/March)** — if you like the city in its worst month, you'll love it the rest of the year.

**Stage 4 — Time the lease.** Rents in all three dip in **winter (Nov–Feb)**; that's the cheapest, least competitive window to sign. Negotiate a 13–14 month lease to avoid re-listing at peak summer.

**Questions to ask before committing:** (1) Confirmed fiber (not just cable/5G) at the specific unit? (2) What's the actual winter commute/errand experience without a car? (3) Employer's remote-work permanence and any pay adjustment? (4) In-network hospital for your ACA/employer plan? (5) Neighborhood-level crime and lighting, walked at night? (6) State policy climate (reproductive/LGBTQ law) if that affects you — most relevant in Missouri.

## Caveats

- **Rent figures vary by source and month.** SF's 1BR median is quoted from $3,160 (Zumper, Aug 2025) [KTVU][ref-31] up to $4,300 (latest Zumper National Rent Report), and the 2BR median just hit $6,020; neighborhood rents elsewhere similarly range by methodology. Treat all rent numbers as current-market estimates, not contracts.
- **Take-home estimates are modeled** (single filer, standard deduction, 2025–2026 federal brackets, state + local income tax) and exclude pre-tax 401(k)/HSA contributions and property tax (you're renting). Your actual number depends on filing status and deductions.
- **ACA premiums are pre-subsidy benchmark figures** for a 40-year-old; enhanced federal subsidies were set to expire Dec 31, 2025, which pushed 2026 premiums up in many states — if you buy on the marketplace, run your own ZIP/age/income through healthcare.gov. If you keep employer coverage, the state ACA differences are less relevant.
- **C2ER composite scores** for St. Louis and some cities were corroborated via secondary aggregators (Salary.com, Numbeo) rather than a primary C2ER release; the rent-inclusive Numbeo comparisons are the most decision-relevant and all show >30% savings.
- **Weather and diversity are reported as honest trade-offs, not filters**, per the brief. The user should weight winter severity (Minneapolis > Pittsburgh > St. Louis) and social/diversity fit (Minneapolis most diverse; Pittsburgh least) according to personal priorities.

# Berkeley Student Group End-of-Year Banquet Plan for About 30 Attendees

## Executive Summary

For a future late-spring/early-summer banquet, the strongest overall combination is **the Tamalpais Room at the David Brower Center in downtown Berkeley, paired with Gather, the Center’s on-site catering partner**. Because today is August 17, 2026, I interpret “late spring/early summer” as **roughly May–June 2027**; if 2026 was intended, that window has already passed. No venue can meaningfully confirm availability without an exact date, and the Brower Center’s inquiry form requires an event date. Its events team reports an average response time of one to two business days. [[1]](https://browercenter.org/rentals/rental-inquiry-form/)

The Tamalpais Room is significantly larger than needed but is unusually good for this assignment: it has an 80-person banquet capacity, 60-inch round banquet tables and padded chairs included, and a current $425 standard AV package that explicitly includes a **projector, laptop, PA system, up to four wireless microphones, cabling, and three hours of AV staff time**. The current college/university quarter-day room rate is $590 for up to four hours. [[2]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/) Gather is physically located in the Brower Center and is listed by the venue as its in-house partner for full service, drop-off, and pickup catering, avoiding the Center’s potential outside-caterer approval fee of up to $300. [[3]](https://browercenter.org/rentals/catering-event-partners/)

For the food, I recommend a deliberately **plant-based menu**, with all mushroom sandwiches ordered **without pesto unless Gather provides written confirmation that the pesto and preparation process are safe for the nut-allergic guests**. Gather expressly labels its Roasted Mushroom Sandwich and Local Lettuces as vegan, which eliminates egg and dairy as intentional ingredients in those dishes and also covers the vegetarian attendee. Its catering page, however, does **not** publish a comprehensive egg/peanut/tree-nut/shellfish cross-contact guarantee, so the booking should be contingent on a written allergen response from the catering manager. [[4]](https://gatherberkeley.com/catering) Cross-contact is important here: FARE notes that self-service buffets create relatively high cross-contact risk, while CDC research recommends dedicated equipment or preparation areas for allergy-safe food. [[5]](https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/avoiding-cross-contact)

A realistic planning package is:

| --- | --- |
| Tamalpais Room, college/university quarter day | $590.00 |
| Standard AV/projector package | $425.00 |
| Gather food for 30 | $870.00 |
| Catering service/setup allowance, **not to exceed** | $350.00 |
| Berkeley sales-tax reserve, 10.25% applied conservatively to food + service allowance | $125.05 |
| Discretionary gratuity reserve, 15% of food | $130.50 |
| Transportation | **$0 — excluded** |
| **Planning total** | **$2,490.55** |
| Preferred-budget headroom | **$9.45** |
| Hard-cap headroom | **$509.45** |

Berkeley’s current sales-and-use tax rate is 10.25%. The $125.05 above is deliberately a conservative planning reserve rather than a statement about the caterer’s final taxable base. [[6]](https://cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/rates.aspx)

**Budget conclusion:** the recommended **weekday, four-hour** configuration fits the $2,500 preferred budget at **$2,490.55** and is $509.45 below the absolute $3,000 ceiling. The caveat is that Gather does not publish its special-event service/setup charge; therefore the $350 line must become a **written not-to-exceed amount in the catering quote** before the total can be considered contractually locked. If Gather’s service/setup quote is higher, there is still substantial room before the $3,000 hard cap, but the preferred $2,500 target would be exceeded.

I would avoid a weekend booking unless the venue confirms all staffing charges first. Brower adds a $100-per-floor weekend facility charge, and some evening/weekend events may also be assigned $700 security and/or janitorial staffing at $55/hour with a three-hour minimum. A weekend facility fee alone would make the same plan about **$2,590.55**; mandatory $700 security would push it above the hard cap. [[7]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)

Gather's published box lunch consists of a half sandwich/wrap, side and cookie and has an eight-box minimum. For the eight special boxes, ask for **Roasted Mushroom Sandwich without pesto + Local Lettuces**, and **remove the standard cookie unless Gather explicitly confirms an appropriate egg-, dairy-, peanut- and tree-nut-safe substitute and its cross-contact procedure**. [[19]](https://gatherberkeley.com/catering) The two extra boxes are intentional backups in case a meal is damaged, mislabeled or another attendee discloses a restriction shortly before service.

The six requested accommodations should be transmitted to Gather as a written matrix, assuming they are six distinct people:

| Meal code | Restriction | Requested preparation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A1–A2 | Egg allergy | Vegan special box; no standard cookie; written egg and cross-contact confirmation |
| P1–P2 | Peanut allergy | Special box prepared and packaged separately; written peanut/cross-contact confirmation |
| M1 | Dairy + nuts + shellfish allergy | Vegan special box, pesto omitted; explicit confirmation covering dairy, peanuts/tree nuts and shellfish |
| V1 | Vegetarian | Vegan special box |
| B1–B2 | Backup | Same specification as the most restrictive M1 meal |

“Vegan” helps with intentional egg and dairy ingredients, but it **does not mean nut-safe or cross-contact-safe**. FDA identifies milk, egg, crustacean shellfish, tree nuts and peanuts among the major food allergens, and FARE defines cross-contact as unintended transfer of an allergen into otherwise safe food. [[26]](https://www.fda.gov/food/nutrition-food-labeling-and-critical-foods/food-allergies)

Because buffet self-service increases the chance that serving utensils migrate between dishes, the special meals should **never be placed on the public buffet**. FARE specifically warns that buffets carry increased accidental-exposure/cross-contact risk, and CDC notes that dedicated equipment or preparation areas can reduce that risk. [[27]](https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/dining-out)

### What would break the budget

**Venue staffing is the main risk, rather than food.** The usual weekday configuration fits the preferred budget. With Brower's $100 weekend facility fee, the same plan would be about **$2,590.55**, still comfortably below the $3,000 ceiling. Adding the published $700 security charge, if required by the Events Director, would take the unchanged package to roughly **$3,290.55**, exceeding the hard cap. Some evening/weekend bookings may also require janitorial staffing at $55/hour, with a three-hour minimum. [[7]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)

Include the following explicit question in the venue inquiry:

“For a 30-person student banquet with no alcohol, approximately four hours on a weekday, please confirm in writing whether any security, janitorial, building/facility, or other mandatory staffing fee applies beyond the quoted room and AV charges.”

### Cost-reduction fallback options

The base recommendation comes in below $2,500, but two alternatives are useful if the chosen date or actual quote adds fees.

**Fallback A — Use boxed/drop-off Gather service.** Thirty box lunches at the published $20 price total $600. Add two fruit platters at $70 each for a $740 food subtotal: $130 less than the recommended $870 food package, before any associated setup/service labor reduction. [[19]](https://gatherberkeley.com/catering) Where Gather can safely accommodate it, order a common allergy-aware specification, with allergy meals prepared separately and labeled. The format feels less like a banquet, but is simpler to operate and reduces buffet cross-contact.

**Fallback B — Adjust the date/time before switching venues.** If a weekend booking adds $100 facility, $700 security or janitorial charges, seek a weekday/daytime or lower-staffing period and have Brower confirm that those fees are unnecessary. Avoiding a possible $700 security charge offers far more savings than realistic food cuts. [[7]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/) If a weekday is not possible, seek a fresh quote for UC Berkeley's Tilden Room once its 2026/27 rates are released, with Berkeley Catering as the caterer. Tilden's 40 banquet seats make it almost exactly the right size for this group. [[17]](https://eventservices.berkeley.edu/venues/indoor-venues/mlk-building/tilden/)

One other potential saving should **not** be assumed in advance: La Méditerranée's $18–$21.50/person prix-fixe pricing is cheaper than Gather's proposed $29/person food subtotal, but using a caterer outside Brower's approved partner list can require prior approval and an outside-catering fee of up to $300 per day. [[28]](https://cafelamedberkeley.com/berkeley-elmwood-la-mediterranee-berkeley-food-menu)

## Booking, Contract, and Insurance

### Venue booking sequence

Start with **three candidate weekday dates in May–June 2027**, ideally ranking them first/second/third rather than asking for “any date.” Submit one through Brower's official event inquiry process for Tamalpais, specifying 30 attendees, banquet seating, a four-hour rental, college/student-group status, Gather catering, no alcohol, and the standard projector package. The form requires a date and start/end time, and Brower reports a typical one-to-two-business-day response. [[1]](https://browercenter.org/rentals/rental-inquiry-form/)

When Brower responds, obtain a written quote that contains all of the following before paying a deposit: room rate; AV package; whether the group receives the college/university or nonprofit category; whether any security or janitorial charge applies; four-hour load-in/event/load-out window; included round tables/chairs; and confirmation that Gather is being treated as the in-house catering partner.

This matters because **all vendor delivery, setup, event activity and load-out must occur within the contractual rental time unless Brower approves otherwise**. [[25]](https://browercenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DBC-FB-Policies-2024.pdf) Four hours therefore cannot mean “four hours of guest program plus setup.” The run-of-show below treats setup and cleanup as part of those four hours.

After the quote is accepted, Brower's published terms call for a **50% booking deposit when the contract is signed and the remaining 50% one month before the event**. If cancelled more than 90 days before the first event date, the booking deposit is refunded; at 30–90 days the deposit is retained, and within 30 days the full rental fee is retained. [[16]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/) Major post-contract changes such as adding AV, changing space or changing occupancy time can incur a $100 change fee plus applicable rental increases, so include the projector package from the initial contract rather than adding it later. [[7]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)

### Catering contract and allergy conditions

Contact Gather's special-event/on-site catering channel rather than placing this allergy-sensitive order solely through ordinary online ordering. Gather explicitly directs customers with on-site catering or special requests to its event inquiry process. [[29]](https://gatherberkeley.com/catering)

The catering quote should contain five written confirmations:

- Exact ingredients or allergen declarations for the mushroom sandwich bread, mushroom filling, pesto if retained, vinaigrette and any substituted dessert.

- Whether **peanuts or tree nuts are handled on the same preparation surfaces/equipment**, and what cross-contact controls can be applied.

- Whether shellfish is prepared in the same kitchen/preparation area and how the M1 meal will be protected.

- Eight individually packaged special meals prepared separately from general buffet handling and held away from guest self-service.

- Total service/setup/serviceware charge of **no more than $350** for the recommended preferred-budget configuration.

FDA and FARE guidance make the distinction important: removing an ingredient does not eliminate the possibility of unintended allergen transfer, and cooking does not make cross-contact safe. [[30]](https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/avoiding-cross-contact)

If Gather says it **cannot safely accommodate one of the severe allergies**, do not attempt to solve that through signage alone. Ask the affected attendee what sealed commercially prepared or personally supplied safe meal they prefer, subject to venue approval. The budget has sufficient margin to handle a small separate meal expense.

### Insurance and facility conditions

Brower's public rental materials do not state a universal general-liability COI requirement for every ordinary student banquet, so ask the events manager whether the group's legal/organizational status triggers one rather than purchasing insurance unnecessarily. Alcohol is different: Brower explicitly requires prior approval for alcohol service and requires alcohol-liability coverage, with other permits and licensing depending on the event. [[25]](https://browercenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DBC-FB-Policies-2024.pdf) **The recommended plan contains no alcohol**, which simplifies both insurance and student-event risk management.

The Magnes alternative is materially more demanding: its current venue information requires a Certificate of Insurance naming the Regents of the University of California as additional insured and at least $1 million in general liability; subcontractors including caterers also need appropriate coverage. [[18]](https://magnes.berkeley.edu/about/venue-rental/) This is one reason I rank it third for a $2,500 student event.

At Brower, also put the sustainability rules into the catering order. The Center does not process ordinary landfill waste/plastics onsite and requires event materials to be reusable or compatible with its compost/recycling system. Outside caterers require approval and may face an outside-catering fee, while Gather is already an approved on-site partner. [[31]](https://browercenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DBC-FB-Policies-2024.pdf) The Center provides no linens, so the budget assumes **no rented tablecloths**—use the venue's tables as-is or organization-owned reusable cloths. [[32]](https://browercenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DBC-FB-Policies-2024.pdf)

### Signage and staffing

Use three types of signs. The general buffet should have ingredient/allergen cards supplied or approved by Gather rather than labels improvised by student organizers. The special-meal station should say **“Reserved special meals — please see catering/event lead; do not self-serve or exchange boxes.”** Individual boxes can use private codes such as A1, P1, M1 and V1 instead of publicly displaying someone's medical condition.

Do **not** write “allergen-free” on a sign unless the catering provider affirmatively makes that claim. FDA notes that allergen advisory language and cross-contact circumstances require careful interpretation, and FARE emphasizes that an otherwise safe recipe can become unsafe through cross-contact. [[35]](https://www.fda.gov/food/conversations-experts-food-topics/current-food-allergen-landscape)

Assign four student leads: one overall event lead who communicates only with the Brower house manager; one AV/program lead; one food/allergy lead who alone releases special meals; and one check-in/guest lead. Brower itself supplies a house manager and includes venue furniture setup/takedown, but the house manager is not a substitute for the group's food-service or event staff. [[16]](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)

Finally, ask Brower for its waste-sorting signage or place venue-approved reusable signs by each disposal station. The Center asks clients to manage bussing and attendee waste sorting, and it can provide additional sorting signage on request; excessive food waste can trigger a fee of up to $300, so order to the confirmed headcount and arrange leftovers before the event. [[32]](https://browercenter.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/02/DBC-FB-Policies-2024.pdf)

**Bottom line:** reserve a **weekday Tamalpais Room + standard projector package + Gather catering**, target a May–June 2027 date, and make the reservation conditional on two written statements: **no mandatory Brower security/janitorial surcharge** and a **Gather service/setup charge of no more than $350 together with an acceptable written allergen/cross-contact plan**. Under those conditions, the researched package comes to approximately **$2,490.55 for 30 people, or $83.02 per attendee all-in**, excluding transportation, and remains within both the preferred $2,500 target and the $3,000 hard ceiling.

## Executive verdict

For your particular combination of **meaningfully lower costs, strong home internet, a genuinely car-light neighborhood, nearby nature, and a major airport**, my ranking is:

| Rank | City | Why it makes the cut | Biggest drawback |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Minneapolis, Minnesota** | Best overall combination of cost savings, exceptional parks, very strong broadband options, walkable neighborhoods, and rail access to a major hub airport | The winter is a serious lifestyle change; Minnesota taxes are not especially low |
| **2** | **Philadelphia, Pennsylvania** | By far the strongest option for truly car-light big-city living; excellent airport access, diversity, amenities, and fiber coverage | Less cheap than the other two; Philadelphia's city wage tax eats into Pennsylvania's low state-tax advantage |
| **3** | **Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania** | Excellent housing value, strong fiber availability, outstanding urban nature, and lower taxes than California | Citywide walkability/transit are only borderline for your requirement, and PIT is substantially smaller than MSP or PHL |

There is one important caveat to your “**at least 30% cheaper than San Francisco**” requirement. Using the **C2ER Cost of Living Index–based city data** published by RentCafe, all three pass: Minneapolis is about **44% cheaper**, Pittsburgh about **40% cheaper**, and Philadelphia about **35% cheaper** than San Francisco on the overall index. Rent savings are even larger—roughly **49% to 57%**. [[1]](https://www.c2er.org/products-and-services/)

However, the federal Bureau of Economic Analysis's broader **Regional Price Parities** tell a less dramatic story. Its latest metro-wide all-item figures put San Francisco at 115.6 versus roughly 104.8 for Minneapolis, 102.6 for Philadelphia, and 94.7 for Pittsburgh, equivalent to only about **9%, 11%, and 18% below San Francisco**, respectively. [[2]](https://www.bea.gov/data/prices-inflation/regional-price-parities-state-and-metro-area)

That difference is not an error. The measures answer somewhat different questions. C2ER is an urban consumer-basket index with explicit housing, healthcare, groceries, utilities and transportation components and is therefore much more sensitive to the expenses you specifically want to escape; BEA's RPP is a broad metro-wide measure of prices across the economy. [[3]](https://www.coli.org/)

**My recommendation therefore assumes that your 30% rule refers to the practical urban household budget represented by C2ER, especially housing.** If instead you require a city to be 30% cheaper under the federal BEA all-item measure as well, I would not claim that any of these three passes.

There is a second nuance: **none of the finalists has a healthcare component fully 30% below San Francisco** in the same C2ER dataset. Minneapolis comes closest at about 27% lower, Pittsburgh about 23%, and Philadelphia about 16%. Your *overall* cost requirement passes and rent passes by a mile, but if you intended “rent and healthcare must each individually be ≥30% cheaper,” these three do not satisfy that stricter interpretation. [[4]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/ca/san-francisco/)

For a remote engineer, I would also treat broadband as a **building-level rather than city-level requirement**. FCC's National Broadband Map lets you inspect the technologies and advertised maximum speeds available at an individual address. That matters because “fiber is available in Minneapolis” does not mean the particular apartment you rent has fiber. [[8]](https://broadbandmap.fcc.gov/)

I therefore used two things as proxies for remote-work resilience: **high-speed coverage and ISP redundancy**. A building where fiber and a separate cable provider are both available is preferable to one with a single nominally fast connection. BroadbandNow's current city datasets are built from FCC availability records and its own provider/speed data, making them useful for this screening, although they are not a guarantee of uptime at any one address. ## Minneapolis — the strongest overall match

**Why it ranks first.** Minneapolis is the rare candidate that does well on virtually every one of your hard constraints rather than dominating one category and compromising badly elsewhere. Its C2ER-style cost level is about **44% below San Francisco**, its average apartment rent is **$1,710 versus $3,964**, and the healthcare component is roughly **27% lower** than SF's. That makes it the best healthcare result of the three and the largest rental saving. [[10]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/mn/minneapolis/)

At the published city-average rents, the difference is about **$2,254 per month, or $27,048 per year**. That is before considering any state-tax differences and obviously is not a promise that the particular one-bedroom you choose will cost the citywide average. [[10]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/mn/minneapolis/)

**Car-light living.** Minneapolis has a citywide **Walk Score of 71**, comfortably clearing your 60 threshold. This is also a case where neighborhood selection materially improves the experience: Uptown scores **93 for walking and 94 for biking**, while the North Loop scores **84 for walking, 81 for transit and 92 for biking**. [[11]](https://www.walkscore.com/score/north-central-university-minneapolis-mn)

For you, I would lean toward **Uptown/Lyn-Lake if everyday access to lakes and outdoor recreation wins**, or **North Loop if airport/transit convenience, restaurants and downtown access win**. Minneapolis is particularly unusual among lower-cost U.S. cities because cycling can genuinely substitute for many car trips rather than merely supplement them. The city's park system includes 7,059 acres of parkland and water, 22 lakes, and more than 100 miles of Grand Rounds biking and walking paths. [[12]](https://www.minneapolisparks.org/golf/courses/theodore_wirth_golf_club/)

The green-space data are exceptionally strong. Trust for Public Land ranks Minneapolis **third among the 100 largest U.S. cities in the 2026 ParkScore**, with near-maximal park-access and amenities scores. Theodore Wirth offers wooded and hilly trails, off-road cycling and winter snowshoeing, while the wider system connects lakes, the Mississippi River and Minnehaha Creek through trails and parkways. [[13]](https://www.tpl.org/city/minneapolis-minnesota)

That makes Minneapolis my best answer to your phrase **“easy access to green spaces or nature for hiking.”** It is not a western mountain city—you are not getting Sierra or Cascades elevation—but you can get from an urban apartment to substantial woods, lakes and trails without structuring your life around a car. [[14]](https://www.minneapolisparks.org/parks-destinations/parks-lakes/theodore_wirth_regional_park/)

**Internet.** This is also arguably the strongest remote-work market of the finalists. Xfinity reaches about **99.3%** of Minneapolis with plans advertised up to **2 Gbps**; Quantum Fiber advertises up to **8 Gbps** with roughly **55.6%** availability, and local provider US Internet advertises speeds up to **10 Gbps** with roughly **31.6%** availability. [[15]](https://broadbandnow.com/Minnesota/Minneapolis)

The percentages overlap rather than add, but that overlap is actually what you want: at the right address you can potentially have a fiber provider plus cable backup. Before paying a deposit, I would require the exact unit/building to show a wired fiber option or, at minimum, gigabit-class cable plus an alternative provider on the FCC address map. [[16]](https://help.bdc.fcc.gov/hc/en-us/articles/10467446103579-How-to-Use-the-FCC-s-National-Broadband-Map)

**Airport.** MSP is a real major airport rather than merely an airport that happens to have “International” in its name. It handled more than **36 million passengers in 2025**, and Metro Transit's Blue Line normally connects the terminals with downtown Minneapolis in **under 30 minutes**, with trains generally every 15 minutes. [[17]](https://www.mspairport.com/about-msp)

There is a very temporary current wrinkle: as of **August 19, 2026**, construction has buses replacing Blue Line trains through August 20. That is a short-term service interruption, not a reason to downgrade Minneapolis as a relocation choice. [[18]](https://www.metrotransit.org/route/blue)

**Taxes.** Minnesota is the weak point compared with the popular “leave California and slash my state taxes” narrative. For 2026, Minnesota's single-filer brackets are **5.35% up to $33,310, 6.8% from $33,311 to $109,430, 7.85% from $109,431 to $203,150, and 9.85% above that**. At a $120,000 salary, the top portion of your taxable income can therefore reach the **7.85% marginal bracket**, although your effective rate is lower because the system is graduated and taxable income is not the same as gross salary. [[19]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/press-release/2025-12-16/minnesota-income-tax-brackets-standard-deduction-and-dependent-exemption)

So Minneapolis is primarily a **housing-and-lifestyle arbitrage**, not a tax arbitrage. Your housing saving is so large that this can still be a very favorable move, but someone relocating solely to minimize state income tax would rank it lower. [[20]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/mn/minneapolis/)

For California specifically, moving is not merely about changing a mailing address. California taxes residents broadly and continues to tax nonresidents on California-source income; the FTB identifies compensation for services physically performed in California as California-source compensation. Ordinary wages for work you perform after genuinely relocating and working outside California are therefore materially different from continuing to perform services in California, while stock compensation or other income tied to California service periods can require more careful sourcing. [[21]](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-588-instructions.html)

**Diversity and amenities.** Minneapolis is clearly more diverse than the stereotype of an Upper Midwest city sometimes implies, though not as diverse as Philadelphia. Census QuickFacts puts the foreign-born share at **13.7%**, Hispanic/Latino residents at **10.1%**, and non-Hispanic White residents at **58.8%**. [[22]](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/minneapoliscityminnesota/PST045225)

The lifestyle advantage is less about sheer big-city scale than about an unusually high quality of everyday urban recreation: lakes, trails, cycling, neighborhood restaurants, arts and a substantial metro economy. The trade-off is that Philadelphia offers more East Coast urban density and Pittsburgh has a more compact topographic character. Minneapolis wins because it gives you the best balance rather than the most of any one urban amenity. [[23]](https://www.tpl.org/city/minneapolis-minnesota)

**The weather, honestly.** This is the reason not to move there. The Twin Cities have a real northern-continental winter, not a San Francisco resident's idea of “cold.” Snow, ice and extended subfreezing weather affect walking and biking for months. The park system itself maintains extensive winter recreation and snowshoeing infrastructure—a benefit if you embrace winter, but also a good indicator that winter is an actual season around which residents organize life. [[24]](https://www.minneapolisparks.org/parks-destinations/parks-lakes/minnehaha_regional_park/)

My read is straightforward: **do not choose Minneapolis without first spending at least several days there in January or February**. If you discover you can tolerate the winter, it is the strongest match in the country from this shortlist. If winter materially reduces your willingness to walk, its on-paper car-light advantage will matter much less.

## Philadelphia — the best choice if avoiding a car matters most

Philadelphia is second overall, but I would actually put it **first for a person whose dominant requirement is “I never want to feel like I need a car.”**

Its citywide **Walk Score is 75**, versus 71 for Minneapolis and 62 for Pittsburgh. In its best central neighborhoods, the difference is much more pronounced: Center City West reaches a **99 Walk Score and 100 Transit Score**; Fitler Square is roughly **97/98/98 for walk/transit/bike**; and Northern Liberties–Fishtown scores **94 for walking**. [[25]](https://www.walkscore.com/score/39.9548419-.dash.75.13980750000002-14/lat%3D39.9548419/lng%3D-75.13980750000002/)

That makes Philadelphia the only finalist where I would be comfortable telling a remote engineer, **“you can design your normal weekly life around walking and transit first and treat a rental car as an occasional tool.”** Minneapolis can also work well without a car in the right neighborhoods; Pittsburgh requires more careful neighborhood selection. [[26]](https://www.walkscore.com/score/39.9548419-.dash.75.13980750000002-14/lat%3D39.9548419/lng%3D-75.13980750000002/)

**Costs.** Philadelphia is the weakest of the three on pure savings but still clears your C2ER-based 30% gate: its overall cost level works out to approximately **34.8% below San Francisco**. Its average apartment rent of **$2,038** is almost **49% below SF's $3,964**, or roughly **$1,926 a month / $23,112 a year less** at those city averages. [[27]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/pa/philadelphia/)

Healthcare is the concern. Philadelphia's healthcare component is about **16% below San Francisco**, materially cheaper but not remotely a 30% reduction. Of your three priorities inside the cost basket, **Philadelphia is excellent for rent but least compelling for healthcare costs**. [[27]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/pa/philadelphia/)

**Nature.** Philadelphia is much greener than the mental image of a dense East Coast rowhouse city suggests. The city Parks & Recreation system manages more than **10,200 acres of public land and waterways** and an extensive trail network. Wissahickon Valley Park alone encompasses roughly **1,800 acres of woodland, meadows and streams with more than 50 miles of scenic trails**. [[28]](https://www.phila.gov/departments/philadelphia-parks-recreation/)

Trust for Public Land ranks Philadelphia **27th in its 2026 ParkScore**, with a particularly strong **96/100 access score**. That ranking is below Minneapolis and Pittsburgh, but the qualitative advantage is that you can have genuine wooded hiking inside the city while still living in one of America's most walkable large urban environments. [[29]](https://www.tpl.org/city/philadelphia-pennsylvania)

For your priorities, I would investigate **Fitler Square / western Center City** if day-to-day walkability and the Schuylkill River Trail matter most, or **Fairmount/Art Museum** if you want a little more breathing room and green-space orientation. If immediate access to wooded trails outranks central-city nightlife, moving farther northwest toward Wissahickon becomes attractive, though you should check the exact address's Walk Score because neighborhood walkability becomes less uniform. [[30]](https://www.walkscore.com/PA/Philadelphia/Fitler_Square)

**Internet.** Philadelphia is excellent for remote work. BroadbandNow's FCC-based dataset shows Xfinity reaching about **99.8%** of the city, while Verizon fiber is available to roughly **85.7%**; both Verizon Fios and Xfinity offer plans reaching about **2 Gbps** in parts of the city. Gigabit-class service is available across almost the entire city through at least one provider. [[31]](https://broadbandnow.com/Pennsylvania/Philadelphia)

That fiber-coverage percentage is one of Philadelphia's major advantages: it gives you a better chance of finding both **high walkability and true fiber in the same apartment search**. Still, verify the precise unit in the FCC Broadband Map before signing. [[32]](https://help.bdc.fcc.gov/hc/en-us/articles/10467446103579-How-to-Use-the-FCC-s-National-Broadband-Map)

**Airport.** Philadelphia wins this category outright. SEPTA's Airport Line goes directly to the airport terminals and takes about **25 minutes from Center City**. PHL reports **134 nonstop destinations—95 domestic and 39 international—and about 400 average daily departures** based on its December 2025 fast facts, with additional routes launching in 2026. [[33]](https://www.phl.org/about/news/fast-facts)

For a remote engineer who may need to fly to a headquarters, client meetings or conferences, that is a large practical advantage over Pittsburgh. You do not have to budget for a car or a long airport ride every time you travel. [[33]](https://www.phl.org/about/news/fast-facts)

**Taxes.** Pennsylvania's state income-tax rate is a flat **3.07%**, dramatically simpler than Minnesota's progressive structure. [[34]](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/personal-income-tax)

But Philadelphia has the catch: as of July 1, 2026, the city resident Wage/Earnings Tax is **3.735%**. On gross wage compensation, the headline combination of the Pennsylvania state rate and Philadelphia resident wage rate is therefore about **6.805 percentage points**, before getting into differences between each tax's base, deductions, credits or your individual circumstances. [[35]](https://www.phila.gov/services/payments-assistance-taxes/taxes/business-taxes/business-taxes-by-type/wage-tax-employers/)

At $120,000, simply multiplying those headline rates gives roughly **$8,166** associated with those two wage-tax layers if the full salary is subject to both. That is an illustrative calculation, not a personal tax return estimate. The important conclusion is that **Philadelphia does not give you the full “Pennsylvania is a 3.07% state” tax benefit because the city takes another sizeable cut**. [[35]](https://www.phila.gov/services/payments-assistance-taxes/taxes/business-taxes/business-taxes-by-type/wage-tax-employers/)

**Diversity and urban amenities.** Philadelphia is comfortably the diversity winner. Census QuickFacts reports roughly **39.4% Black, 33.2% non-Hispanic White, 15.6% Hispanic/Latino, and 8.0% Asian**, with **15.2% foreign-born**. [[36]](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/philadelphiacitypennsylvania/PST040225)

It also has the strongest “big city” amenity package of the three: a much denser transit-oriented core, far stronger nonstop aviation than Pittsburgh, and large-scale cultural and commercial districts that can be accessed without driving. Those characteristics are reflected directly in its Walk Score and airport network rather than being based merely on subjective city reputation. [[37]](https://www.walkscore.com/score/39.9548419-.dash.75.13980750000002-14/lat%3D39.9548419/lng%3D-75.13980750000002/)

**The weather.** Philadelphia's downside is summer rather than winter. At PHL, the 1991–2020 normal July high is **87.8°F** and the normal low **69.6°F**; summers are therefore substantially hotter than what you are accustomed to in San Francisco. Winters are real but much milder than Minneapolis. [[38]](https://www.weather.gov/phi/clinormalsKPHL)

For a former San Franciscan, the biggest adjustment will probably be **heat plus humidity**, particularly because a walkable lifestyle means you are outside more. Air conditioning should be a hard requirement in any apartment search.

## Pittsburgh — excellent value and nature, with the most neighborhood dependence

Pittsburgh is the value-oriented choice. On the C2ER-based measure it is approximately **40% cheaper than San Francisco overall**, while its average apartment rent of **$1,850** is about **53% lower**. The difference from SF's published average is approximately **$2,114 a month or $25,368 a year**. [[39]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/pa/pittsburgh/)

Its healthcare component is about **23% lower than San Francisco**, second only to Minneapolis in this shortlist. So if your concern about healthcare reflects premiums, medical services and routine out-of-pocket costs rather than just rent, Pittsburgh is materially stronger than Philadelphia on the underlying C2ER category. [[39]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/pa/pittsburgh/)

**Why it is only third.** Pittsburgh's citywide Walk Score is **62**—technically above your stated 60 target, but not by much. Its Transit Score is around **55**. This is precisely why I would not simply say “Pittsburgh is walkable” without specifying where. [[40]](https://www.walkscore.com/PA/Pittsburgh)

The good news is that the right neighborhoods are dramatically better. **Shadyside has a Walk Score around 91 and Transit Score around 71**; North Oakland is similarly around **91/70**; Squirrel Hill South scores around **74**, and Regent Square around **74**. [[41]](https://www.walkscore.com/PA/Pittsburgh/Shadyside)

For you, **Shadyside** is probably the safest first apartment search because it gives you a much more credible car-light daily life while keeping restaurants, groceries, universities and transit nearby. **Squirrel Hill/Regent Square** become more attractive if your number-one lifestyle priority is getting onto wooded trails quickly. [[42]](https://www.walkscore.com/PA/Pittsburgh/Shadyside)

Pittsburgh's topography is part of the trade-off. A Walk Score does not communicate how steep some walks are. A grocery store a half mile away up a significant hill is not the same experience as a half-mile walk in Center City Philadelphia. That is one reason I would personally walk candidate routes from apartment → grocery store → coffee shop → transit stop before leasing rather than relying solely on the score.

**Nature is outstanding for a city this affordable.** Trust for Public Land ranks Pittsburgh **16th in the 2026 ParkScore**, ahead of Philadelphia. Frick Park, the city's largest park, is **644 acres**, and the Nine Mile Run/Frick system offers extensive walking paths of varying difficulty plus mountain-bike trails. [[43]](https://www.tpl.org/city/pittsburgh-pennsylvania)

That is a particularly good match for your request because it is not merely “a grass lawn within walking distance.” In the eastern neighborhoods, you can live in an urban environment and have legitimate wooded, hilly trail terrain close by. [[44]](https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/Business-Development/City-Planning/City-Planning-Programs/Our-Greenways)

**Internet.** Pittsburgh punches above its weight. BroadbandNow reports Xfinity availability around **99.6%** and Verizon Fios fiber around **91.4%**, with both offering service up to about **2 Gbps** in the city. Gigabit-class internet is effectively universally available through at least one wired provider in its dataset. [[45]](https://broadbandnow.com/Pennsylvania/Pittsburgh)

For a remote engineer, that **91%+ fiber footprint plus cable alternative** is arguably more attractive than the city's modest national profile would suggest. Again, check the building rather than trusting the ZIP code. [[46]](https://help.bdc.fcc.gov/hc/en-us/articles/10467446103579-How-to-Use-the-FCC-s-National-Broadband-Map)

**Airport.** PIT easily satisfies your “within roughly 90 minutes” geographic criterion from the central/eastern neighborhoods, and public transit includes the 28X Airport Flyer. The airport handled about **9.84 million passengers in 2025** and advertises roughly **62 nonstop destinations**. [[47]](https://www.rideprt.org/services/airport-service/)

But airport *quality for a frequent traveler* is a real distinction between this and the top two. PIT's passenger volume and nonstop network are much smaller than Philadelphia's 134-destination network or MSP's major-hub scale, so more trips will involve a connection. [[48]](https://www.phl.org/about/news/fast-facts)

**Taxes.** Pennsylvania again charges **3.07%** at the state level. Pittsburgh residents also face a local earned-income tax of approximately **3%**, putting the simple headline state-plus-local rate on earned income at about **6.07%**. [[49]](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/personal-income-tax)

At $120,000, 6.07% of gross compensation is about **$7,284** as a rough rate-multiplication illustration. The actual taxable bases and return mechanics matter, so this is not individualized tax advice. The useful comparison is that Pittsburgh avoids Philadelphia's somewhat higher **3.735% city wage rate**, while both benefit from Pennsylvania's low flat state rate. [[50]](https://www.phila.gov/services/payments-assistance-taxes/taxes/business-taxes/business-taxes-by-type/wage-tax-employers/)

**Diversity.** Pittsburgh is the least diverse of the three by several Census measures. QuickFacts puts the city at approximately **61.9% non-Hispanic White, 22.3% Black, 6.2% Asian and 4.5% Hispanic/Latino**, with **9.5% foreign-born**. [[51]](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/pittsburghcitypennsylvania/PST045225)

For comparison, Philadelphia's foreign-born share is 15.2% and Minneapolis's is 13.7%, so Pittsburgh has a noticeably smaller immigrant presence. [[52]](https://www.census.gov/quickfacts/fact/table/philadelphiacitypennsylvania/PST040225)

**The weather.** Pittsburgh has genuine four-season weather. Winters are colder and snowier than the Bay Area, and the city's hills can make snow and ice disproportionately relevant when your plan is to walk rather than drive. Summers are warm rather than San Francisco-cool. The National Weather Service's Pittsburgh climate products use 1991–2020 normals and show the seasonal range expected of an inland northeastern city. [[53]](https://forecast.weather.gov/product.php?format=CI&glossary=0&issuedby=PIT&product=CLI&site=NWS&version=41)

In practice I consider its climate **easier than Minneapolis but less pleasant than Philadelphia in winter for someone highly sensitive to gloomy/cold conditions**. The topography is the bigger car-free concern than the temperature itself.

## Taxes, remote work, and the California exit

At your salary, the tax story is important enough that it should not be reduced to “California high, everywhere else low.”

| Location | Relevant 2026 wage-income structure | Practical read for $120K remote salary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **California / SF today** | Progressive state income tax; California also has detailed residency/source rules | Properly terminating CA residency can matter substantially, but California-source income may remain taxable |
| **Minneapolis** | Minnesota: 5.35%, 6.8%, 7.85%, 9.85% brackets | **Least attractive of the three for pure tax savings**; some of a $120K single filer's taxable income can fall into 7.85% bracket |
| **Philadelphia** | PA 3.07% + Philadelphia resident wage tax 3.735% | Headline wage-tax layers ≈ **6.805%** |
| **Pittsburgh** | PA 3.07% + Pittsburgh resident EIT ≈3% | Headline wage-income layers ≈ **6.07%** |

Minnesota rates: [[19]](https://www.revenue.state.mn.us/press-release/2025-12-16/minnesota-income-tax-brackets-standard-deduction-and-dependent-exemption) Pennsylvania rate: [[34]](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/revenue/resources/tax-types-and-information/personal-income-tax) Philadelphia rate: [[54]](https://www.phila.gov/services/payments-assistance-taxes/taxes/business-taxes/business-taxes-by-type/wage-tax-employers/) Pittsburgh local tax: [[55]](https://www.pittsburghpa.gov/City-Government/Finance-Budget/Taxes) California residency/source framework: [[56]](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-540-es-instructions.html)

There are two remote-work issues I would check with your employer **before** moving.

First, confirm that the company is registered and willing to employ you in Minnesota or Pennsylvania. A remote employee can create state payroll, unemployment-insurance and other employer obligations, and not every company permits every state even when a job is advertised as “remote.”

Second, make the California residency transition real rather than cosmetic. The FTB distinguishes residents, part-year residents and nonresidents and continues taxing California-source income after a move. Compensation associated with services physically performed in California and some equity compensation can raise sourcing questions even after you have moved. [[56]](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2026/2026-540-es-instructions.html)

For ordinary salary going forward, the financial logic of relocating remains powerful because **your largest saving here is housing, not merely income tax**. At the citywide rental averages, you are looking at an apples-to-apples annual rent difference of roughly **$23,000–$27,000**, dwarfing relatively small differences between, say, a 6% and 8% state/local effective tax burden. [[4]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/ca/san-francisco/)

## Final recommendation

For **your exact profile**, I would move to **Minneapolis** unless you already know that severe winters make you miserable.

It has the strongest quantitative package: roughly **44% lower C2ER-style living costs than San Francisco, 57% lower average rent, the lowest healthcare cost of the three, a 71 citywide Walk Score, the nation's #3 ParkScore, multiple multigigabit fiber/cable options, and a sub-30-minute rail connection from downtown to a major airport**. [[57]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/mn/minneapolis/)

My first apartment search would be **Uptown/Lyn-Lake**, followed by **North Loop**. Uptown is stronger for the daily “walk outside and be around lakes/trails” lifestyle; North Loop is stronger for airport/transit access and dense urban amenities. [[58]](https://www.walkscore.com/MN/Minneapolis/Uptown)

Choose **Philadelphia instead** when the sentence “I really don't want to be stuck in a car” is the overriding requirement. It is the most convincing place on this list for an SF-style habit of walking to groceries, restaurants and daily errands; it also gives you genuine rapid rail access to a much larger international flight network and substantially more demographic diversity. You pay for that advantage through somewhat higher living costs, the Philadelphia wage tax, and smaller healthcare savings. [[59]](https://www.walkscore.com/score/39.9548419-.dash.75.13980750000002-14/lat%3D39.9548419/lng%3D-75.13980750000002/)

Choose **Pittsburgh instead** when you want to maximize the combination of **cheap housing + fiber + wooded urban nature** and are willing to be extremely selective about neighborhood. Shadyside or the Squirrel Hill/Frick Park side of town can satisfy your lifestyle; the city as a whole is not nearly as reliably car-light as Philadelphia or the best parts of Minneapolis. [[60]](https://www.rentcafe.com/cost-of-living-calculator/us/pa/pittsburgh/)

The most consequential pre-lease check in all three cities is therefore not another city ranking. It is an **address-level screen**: take every apartment you are seriously considering and verify its Walk Score, actual walking route to groceries/transit, fiber availability on the FCC map, and realistic public-transit route to the airport. City averages identify the right metro; those four checks determine whether the individual apartment actually delivers the car-light remote-engineer lifestyle you are moving for.

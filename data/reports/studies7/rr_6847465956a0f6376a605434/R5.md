# LiDAR vs. Cameras: Why Autonomous Vehicle Systems Diverge on Perception

*Technical report — September 2026*

## Executive summary

The autonomous-vehicle industry has split into two durable camps. **LiDAR-first systems** (Waymo, Zoox, Aurora, Baidu Apollo, Mobileye's Chauffeur) fuse cameras with active laser and radar sensing to obtain redundant, directly measured 3D geometry, arguing that superhuman safety cannot be validated on any single passive modality. **Vision-first systems** (Tesla, Xpeng, and increasingly other Chinese OEMs) rely on cameras plus large neural networks, arguing that end-to-end learning on massive fleet data can close the perception gap at a fraction of the hardware cost and scale to any road without pre-mapping. The divergence is rooted less in disagreement about physics than in different optimization targets: **provable safety redundancy per vehicle** versus **cost, scalability, and data compounding across millions of vehicles**. Recent evidence cuts both ways — LiDAR prices have collapsed ~99.7% since 2017 while neural-network perception has absorbed tasks once thought to require active sensing — and the two architectures are visibly converging.

## 1. The physics of the split: active versus passive sensing

The sensor choice is fundamentally a bet about where to place the hard problem: in the hardware or in the software. A camera is a **passive** sensor — it records a dense, high-resolution 2D projection of ambient light. That signal carries the semantic content driving actually requires: traffic-light states, brake lights, signage text, lane markings, hand gestures. But a single 2D projection discards metric depth, which must then be *inferred* by a neural network, and monocular depth-estimation error grows exponentially for targets beyond roughly 50 m ([Semantic Scholar](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/d316/f2cba9ad7e6ca891fb327916dcfbe808153b.pdf)). Image quality also collapses under strong backlight, darkness, fog, or lens occlusion — the exact conditions implicated in NHTSA's investigation of Tesla FSD, which found the system failed to detect reduced-visibility conditions and did not warn drivers until just before impact ([Land Line](https://landline.media/magazine/nhtsa-investigates-teslas-full-self-driving-feature/)).

LiDAR is an **active** sensor: it emits laser pulses and times their return, producing a sparse but metrically exact 3D point cloud regardless of ambient lighting. It cannot read a traffic light or a sign, and its proponents concede it is blind to color and text — but it physically detects the *presence* of an object whether or not a classifier recognizes it, a distinction that matters because camera-based crashes have repeatedly involved objects outside the detection ontology ([Think Autonomous](https://www.thinkautonomous.ai/blog/occupancy-networks/)). Radar occupies a third niche: it penetrates fog, rain, and dust and measures velocity directly via Doppler shift, which is why most LiDAR-first stacks keep it ([Not a Tesla App](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/4545/waymo-explains-their-multimodal-approach-to-autonomy-video)).

| Property | Camera (passive vision) | LiDAR (active ranging) | Radar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Output | Dense 2D image, color, texture | Sparse metric 3D point cloud | Range + Doppler velocity |
| Depth/distance | Inferred by NN; error grows >50 m ([Semantic Scholar](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/d316/f2cba9ad7e6ca891fb327916dcfbe808153b.pdf)) | Direct measurement, cm-level accuracy | Direct, coarse angular resolution |
| Semantics (signs, lights, color) | Excellent | None ([VŠB-TU Ostrava](https://mrl.cs.vsb.cz/data/vyuka/zao/2025/ZAO-LE-08.pdf)) | None |
| Darkness / glare | Degrades | Unaffected | Unaffected |
| Fog / heavy rain / dust | Degrades | Partially degraded (noise points) ([CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/05/21/xpeng-defends-pure-vision-strategy-says-lidar-no-longer-necessary/)) | Best penetration |
| Unit cost (2025) | ~$50 per car in an 8-camera suite (Contrary estimate: $400 total) | $200–500 solid-state ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/us-autonomous-car-market)) | $50–300 ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/4998828.pdf?abstractid=4998828&mirid=1)) |

Because each modality fails differently, the fusion literature treats redundancy and fault tolerance as the central functional-safety argument: when one sensor degrades, others compensate, and fused systems can hold decision latency under 100 ms ([Sensors review, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13074927/)). Everything else in the industry debate is downstream of this asymmetry.

## 2. The LiDAR-first rationale: buy certainty, then prove safety

The LiDAR-first camp optimizes for the **right tail of the reliability distribution**. Waymo co-CEO Dmitri Dolgov frames it as a ceiling problem: a camera-only "weak perception" system can plausibly reach human-level driving, but its safety curve plateaus too early when the target is performance *far above* human, and each additional "9" of reliability is exponentially harder to validate ([mywheels.my](https://mywheels.my/?p=98510)). Waymo's canonical demonstration — repeated at Google I/O — is a Phoenix dust storm in which cameras captured nothing usable while LiDAR still tracked a pedestrian ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/tesla-waymo-and-the-great-sensor-debate)). The architectural claim is stronger than mere backup: each modality gets its own processing chain, and fusion happens over independent world views, so failures are decorrelated rather than shared ([mywheels.my](https://mywheels.my/20260816/waymo-%E5%BC%80%E5%91%9B-tesla%EF%BC%81-lidar-%E4%B8%8E-ai-%E8%B7%AF%E7%BA%BF%E6%8A%80%E6%9C%AF%E6%AD%A3%E9%9D%A2%E5%BC%80%E6%88%98/)).

Mobileye formalizes the same idea as **"True Redundancy"**: it runs one development AV on cameras alone and another on radar/LiDAR alone, each building a complete world model, so the production system gains a genuinely independent second channel and a higher mean time between failures rather than merely complementary coverage ([Mobileye](https://www.mobileye.com/blog/av-safety-demands-true-redundancy/)). This design philosophy is also about **validation economics**: deterministic 3D geometry and HD-map anchoring make a modular perception–prediction–planning stack easier to test, explain, and defend to regulators and insurers than a monolithic learned policy ([AI Understanding](https://aiunderstanding.org/learn/waymo-autonomous-driving-stack)). Part of the preference is historical path dependence — Google's project chose LiDAR in 2009, before deep learning made camera inference credible — and the safety case accumulated since then reinforces it ([Spatial Intelligence Handbook](https://github.com/sou350121/Spatial-Intelligence-Handbook/blob/main/embodiments/driving/waymo_vs_tesla_doctrinal_split.md)).

The business model tolerates the hardware bill. A geofenced robotaxi runs high utilization with remote assistance and fleet-level maintenance, so a sensor suite once estimated at ~$12,700 per vehicle (5th-generation Waymo Driver, 2024) is amortized over millions of paid miles ([Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/tesla-waymo-and-the-great-sensor-debate)). Autonomous-trucking operators such as Aurora and Kodiak make the same calculation for highway freight, where regulators explicitly favor redundant multi-sensor architectures ([Market Intelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/automotive-lidar-market)).

## 3. The vision + neural-network rationale: trade hardware for data

The vision-first camp makes the opposite bet: perception is fundamentally a **learning problem**, and every dollar spent on extra sensors is a dollar that doesn't buy training data, compute, or fleet scale. Tesla removed radar in 2021 and ultrasonics in 2022, leaving eight cameras and an end-to-end network; FSD v12 replaced a hand-coded stack that had grown to roughly 300,000 lines of C++ with a single network mapping pixels to driving actions, an explicit application of Karpathy's "Software 2.0" thesis that learned weights scale better than hand-written rules for an unbounded long tail of edge cases ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/after-being-called-out-by-elon-musk-teslas-ex-ai-director-andrej-karpathy-says-google-waymo-cannot-do/articleshow/126285839.cms), [vercel.app teardown](https://site-one-liart-13.vercel.app/en/2026-06-18/tesla-fsd-end-to-end-architecture-jun2026)). Occupancy networks — predicting volumetric occupancy and flow directly from multi-camera video at over 100 FPS — were Tesla's answer to LiDAR's core capability: detecting *that* something is there without recognizing *what* it is ([Think Autonomous](https://www.thinkautonomous.ai/blog/occupancy-networks/)).

Three structural arguments carry more weight than Musk's famous jabs ("anyone relying on LiDAR is doomed", 2019; "humans drive without shooting lasers out of their eyes", 2025) ([Fierce Sensors](https://www.fiercesensors.com/sensors-fusion/lidar-robotaxi-debate-continues-updated-waymos-dolgov), [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/report/tesla-waymo-and-the-great-sensor-debate)). **First, cost and manufacturability**: Tesla's camera suite costs roughly $400 per vehicle versus $12.7K for Waymo's fifth-gen suite, and Morgan Stanley pegs Tesla's full FSD hardware near $3,000 against ~$30,000 of sensor-plus-compute hardware on a Waymo vehicle — an existential gap for a consumer car sold at scale ([Longbridge / Morgan Stanley](https://longbridge.com/news/270699542)). **Second, data compounding**: a fleet of millions of customer cars feeds the training loop every mile, an advantage no robotaxi operator can replicate in simulation ([vercel.app benchmark](https://site-one-liart-13.vercel.app/en/2026-06-18/physical-ai-talent-engineering-teams-waymo-google-sdc-tesla-fsd-karpathy-ai-engineers-jun2026)). **Third, infrastructure-free scaling**: a mapless, camera-only stack can light up a new metro in weeks rather than quarters of HD-mapping, which is why Xpeng dropped LiDAR across its line-up from late 2024 and argues that end-to-end vision-language-action models *require* the information density of high-resolution video — far more bits per second than LiDAR returns — while LiDAR itself adds rain/fog noise and eye-safety power limits ([Cubic³](https://www.cubic3.com/blog/the-sensor-debate-that-will-decide-robotaxi-economics/), [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/05/21/xpeng-defends-pure-vision-strategy-says-lidar-no-longer-necessary/)). DBS Bank estimates cameras are roughly 90% cheaper than LiDAR per unit and identifies cost as the main driver of Xpeng's switch ([DBS](https://www.dbs.com/content/article/pdf/AIO/102024/241030_insights_china_hong_kong_industry_focus_regional_auto_sector_smart_tech_drives_into_mass_market_fast_lane.pdf)).

A subtler engineering argument also favors minimalism: fewer modalities mean fewer cross-sensor calibration, cleaning, and fusion-conflict failure modes, and improvements ship as model updates rather than hardware revisions ([Cubic³](https://www.cubic3.com/blog/the-sensor-debate-that-will-decide-robotaxi-economics/)). The philosophical core — the road system, signage, and lighting were all designed for *human vision*, so sufficiently capable vision AI inherits that infrastructure for free — remains the camp's founding premise.

## 4. The economics are converging faster than the rhetoric

The strongest historical argument for vision-only — LiDAR's price — has a short shelf life. Automotive LiDAR has fallen from ~$75,000 per unit in 2017 to solid-state units under $500 in 2025, with China's long-range units reaching roughly $145–200 and Hesai shipping its ATX near $200 against a four-million-unit order book ([dataintelo](https://dataintelo.com/report/global-l4-automatic-vehicle-market), [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/us-autonomous-car-market), [Cubic³](https://www.cubic3.com/blog/the-sensor-debate-that-will-decide-robotaxi-economics/)).

Automotive LiDAR unit price collapse, 2017–2026 (log scale)

Simultaneously, sensor-heavy stacks are thinning. Waymo's sixth-generation Driver cut sensor count 42% — to 13 cameras, 4 LiDARs, 6 radars — at a reported hardware cost under $20,000, while Pony.ai removed ~70% of bill-of-materials cost in its Gen-7 platform ([Cubic³](https://www.cubic3.com/blog/the-sensor-debate-that-will-decide-robotaxi-economics/)). As one analysis puts it, when the sensor approaches the price of a premium headlamp, removing it stops being a strategic decision and becomes an engineering preference — which is why the dispute has migrated from hardware to *where the model's capability ceiling actually sits*.

## 5. What the evidence shows so far

The operational evidence currently favors the multi-sensor camp on its chosen metric. Waymo logged 170.7 million fully driverless rider-only miles through December 2025 and passed 200 million in early 2026 ([arXiv 2606.17451](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.17451v1)); a Swiss Re analysis of 25.3 million autonomous miles found **88% fewer property-damage claims and 92% fewer bodily-injury claims** than human baselines ([Repairer Driven News](https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2025/01/16/waymo-swiss-re-study-finds-waymos-autonomous-driving-safer-than-human-driving/)), and a peer-reviewed study in *Traffic Injury Prevention* over 56.7 million miles found 85% fewer suspected-serious-injury crashes, 92% fewer pedestrian-injury crashes, and 96% fewer intersection-injury crashes ([InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/758604/waymo-robotaxis-better-than-human-drivers/)). Updated figures through March 2026 report −82% for any-injury and −94% for serious-injury crash rates across 220.6 million rider-only miles ([Axis Intelligence](https://axis-intelligence.com/robotaxi-statistics/)).

The vision-only camp's evidence base is thinner precisely where it matters most. NHTSA escalated its probe of FSD's reduced-visibility performance to an Engineering Analysis — the step preceding a recall — covering an estimated 3.2 million vehicles, after finding degradation detection failed to warn drivers when cameras were blinded by glare, fog, or dust ([Motley Fool discussion](https://discussion.fool.com/t/tesla-is-one-step-away-from-having-to-recall-fsd/123866), [Basenor](https://www.basenor.com/blogs/news/nhtsa-escalates-tesla-fsd-probe-what-owners-must-know)). Tesla's Austin robotaxi fleet recorded nine NHTSA-reported crashes over roughly 500,000 supervised miles through November 2025 — about one per 55,000 miles *with a safety monitor aboard*, versus a human police-reported baseline near one per 500,000 miles ([SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/waymo-vs-tesla-autonomy-two-fundamentally-different-approaches-to-risk/)). The comparison is not apples-to-apples — supervised miles with a human backstop measure a different thing than driverless miles — but that asymmetry is itself the point: the LiDAR-first camp has published independently validated driverless safety data, and the vision-first camp has not yet ([SoftwareSeni](https://www.softwareseni.com/waymo-vs-tesla-autonomy-two-fundamentally-different-approaches-to-risk/)).

## 6. Outlook: a dial, not a winner

Both theses are being partially confirmed. Neural networks genuinely have absorbed much of the perception task — Xpeng and Tesla now operate commercial-grade systems on cameras alone, and every new Xpeng model is LiDAR-free ([Gasgoo](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/articles/news/is-lidar-no-longer-necessary-in-the-automotive-sector-2057737516554313729)) — while collapsing LiDAR prices have simultaneously neutralized the cost objection that motivated vision-only in the first place. The industry direction is therefore thinner sensor stacks *everywhere*, with the residual disagreement about where the floor sits: Waymo keeps four LiDARs as decorrelated safety channels, Tesla and Xpeng keep zero and push the burden onto model capability and fleet data.

The most defensible reading of the current evidence is that the sensor set will be **tuned per operating domain** — leaner configurations in benign, well-characterized geographies and consumer vehicles where cost dominates, richer ones where weather, darkness, and liability exposure dominate ([Cubic³](https://www.cubic3.com/blog/the-sensor-debate-that-will-decide-robotaxi-economics/)). The strategic battleground has already shifted from the sensor to the data engine: whichever architecture learns fastest per mile driven will define the floor for everyone else.

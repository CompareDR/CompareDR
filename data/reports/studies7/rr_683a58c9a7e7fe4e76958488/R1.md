# Engram-Augmented Predictive Coding in a Biologically Plausible Spiking Network for Classification

## Executive Summary

This report proposes an **Engram-Augmented Predictive Coding Spiking Network (EPC-SNN)**: a single recurrent architecture in which hierarchical cortical-like circuits perform predictive inference while a hippocampal-like recurrent excitatory/inhibitory circuit forms sparse enram-like memory ensembles. The central computational hypothesis is that the two mechanisms solve complementary problems. **Predictive coding estimates latent causes and computes where current sensory evidence violates expectation; engram dynamics preserve and pattern-complete particular latent causes that have proved behaviorally important.** Memory retrieval then feeds those latent causes back into the predictive hierarchy as priors. This proposal is motivated by evidence that experimentally identified neuronal ensembles can be sufficient for memory retrieval, that neuronal excitability biases which cells enter a memory trace, that memories acquired close in time can occupy overlapping ensembles, and that hippocampal pattern completion is related to predictive signals in sensory cortex. [[1]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028)

The proposed network uses **conductance-based leaky integrate-and-fire neurons with adaptation** rather than purely rate-based units. This is computationally more demanding, but it allows synaptic eligibility traces, spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP), inhibitory homeostasis, neuromodulatory third factors, replay and engram reactivation to be represented directly. LIF neurons with adaptation, AMPA/NMDA-like excitation, GABA-like inhibition, triplet STDP and inhibitory plasticity have already been combined successfully in recent computational work on dynamic engram formation and consolidation. [[2]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

The cortical part contains two predictive-processing levels. At each level, excitatory pyramidal-like representation neurons coexist with **positive and negative prediction-error populations** and local inhibitory interneurons. Signed errors are therefore represented without violating Dale's principle: no neuron needs to change from excitatory to inhibitory output. Top-down predictions are excitatory signals routed through inhibitory comparator circuitry, while residual errors propagate upward. This circuit should be regarded as a concrete hypothesis rather than a settled anatomical interpretation of predictive coding; canonical-microcircuit mappings remain theoretical proposals. [[3]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn0199_79)

The memory module is a sparse recurrent E/I network. Engram membership is **activity- and excitability-biased rather than predetermined by class label**. High-level predictive activity cues the memory network; cells with temporarily elevated intrinsic excitability have a higher probability of entering the active ensemble, following the memory-allocation literature. The proposal adds one specific hypothesis: **unsigned prediction error contributes to allocation**, so that events that are poorly explained by the current generative model are preferentially tagged for episodic storage. This addition is not an established biological fact and is one of the model's principal experimental predictions. CREB/excitability-dependent neuronal competition and later work showing recruitment according to relative pre-training excitability provide the empirical basis for the excitability component. [[4]](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1139438)

Plasticity operates through several interacting local mechanisms rather than backpropagation. Cortical generative connections are adjusted by products of presynaptic activity and locally available dendritic or prediction-error signals. Engram E→E connections use STDP-like eligibility traces, modulated by behaviorally relevant neuromodulatory signals. Homeostatic inhibitory plasticity controls runaway recurrent excitation. A BCM-like sliding activity threshold controls long-term competition, while a synaptic-tagging-and-capture-inspired slow variable determines whether transient changes are consolidated. Experimental and computational work supports each ingredient separately: classical STDP depends on relative spike timing; BCM introduces a moving modification threshold; synaptic tagging can separate transient from protein-synthesis-dependent persistent plasticity; dopamine can retrospectively alter eligible synapses; and local eligibility traces combined with broadcast learning signals can support useful online learning in spiking recurrent networks. [[5]](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/18/24/10464)

For supervised MNIST classification, a correct-label teaching signal activates the corresponding decision population and generates a dopamine-like reinforcement/modulatory pulse. Hidden-layer credit is not explicitly backpropagated. Instead, prediction errors establish local dendritic mismatch signals throughout the hierarchy; recent biologically motivated learning frameworks have shown that local predictive errors, segregated dendritic compartments, or eligibility traces plus top-down signals can approximate useful portions of error-based credit assignment. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28333583/)

The most important feature of the proposal is therefore not any individual plasticity equation but the **closed loop**

$$
\text{sensory evidence} \rightarrow \text{prediction error} \rightarrow \text{latent representation} \rightarrow \text{engram allocation/retrieval} \rightarrow \text{top-down prior} \rightarrow \text{new prediction error}.
$$

This creates a classification system that should behave differently from either a conventional predictive-coding network or a recurrent associative memory alone. The full model is expected to be particularly advantageous when stimuli are **partial, noisy, sequential, few-shot or susceptible to catastrophic interference**; the predictive-only architecture should remain competitive on clean familiar inputs, while the engram-only architecture should pattern-complete stored exemplars but generalize less efficiently. These are **predictions to be tested, not reported simulation results**.

The recommended first implementation is deliberately modest: canonical MNIST, two predictive levels, approximately ${10}^{3}$ excitatory neurons in the memory circuit, one 200–300 ms presentation per image, local spiking plasticity and accelerated replay/consolidation between learning blocks. MNIST originates from the handwritten-digit recognition framework developed by LeCun and colleagues and remains appropriate here because it permits careful mechanistic ablation before moving to temporal or naturalistic tasks. [[7]](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/726791)

**Engrams and allocation.** “Engram” should be used carefully. In modern experimental neuroscience it generally refers to a set of physical cellular and synaptic changes associated with a particular memory, operationally studied through activity-dependent tagging, loss-of-function and gain-of-function manipulations rather than simply by identifying a high-activity cluster. The landmark Liu et al. experiment showed that optogenetic reactivation of dentate-gyrus cells tagged during fear conditioning could elicit freezing, giving a causal rather than merely correlational interpretation to an activity-defined ensemble. [[19]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028)

Allocation research adds a crucial constraint: ensemble membership is not random. Han et al. established neuronal competition during memory formation, while Yiu et al. reported that relative excitability immediately before training predicts recruitment into a subsequent memory trace. Reviews of memory allocation place CREB-associated excitability and competition at the center of this mechanism. [[4]](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1139438) The model therefore should not designate permanent “digit-3 engram neurons.” Instead, memory neurons begin with heterogeneous excitability and acquire temporary excitability changes that bias subsequent recruitment.

Cai et al. further showed that contextual memories acquired relatively close in time can be represented by more-overlapping CA1 ensembles, with altered excitability influencing memory linking. [[20]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17955) This motivates an allocation bias that decays more slowly than the membrane potential: a neuron participating in one episode remains preferentially recruitable for a limited subsequent window. Such a mechanism provides a biologically grounded route to **temporal clustering of related experiences**.

Engrams are also not necessarily static. Tomé and colleagues' recent computational study combined adaptive LIF neurons, conductance-based synapses, triplet STDP, non-Hebbian plasticity, inhibitory plasticity and consolidation dynamics. Their simulated engrams lost and gained neurons during consolidation, while memory representations became more selective. [[21]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w) This argues against a design in which an engram is a permanently frozen sparse vector immediately after one training presentation.

**Predictive coding and hierarchical predictive processing.** Rao and Ballard's visual-cortex model formalized the idea that higher levels generate predictions of lower-level activity and that residual discrepancies carry the information not already explained by those predictions. [[12]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn0199_79) Later predictive-processing theories generalized this concept across cortical systems; Bastos et al. proposed a canonical cortical microcircuit interpretation and Keller and Mrsic-Flogel reviewed predictive processing as a candidate canonical cortical computation. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23177956/) The distinction between theory and anatomy matters: a literal dedicated “error neuron” for every scalar residual should not be presented as established neurobiology.

There is nevertheless an especially strong reason to integrate memory into this framework. Hindy, Ng and Turk-Browne reported that hippocampal pattern completion was associated with predictive coding in early visual cortex, directly suggesting that retrieved associative information can contribute to sensory prediction. [[22]](https://www.nature.com/neuro/articles?page=10&searchType=journalSearch&sort=PubDate&year=2016) Barron, Auksztulewicz and Friston subsequently developed a broader predictive-coding account of the relationship between prediction and memory. [[23]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32446883/) The proposed EPC-SNN operationalizes that interaction as a recurrent pathway from a cortical high-level representation into an engram network and back from the retrieved engram into the cortical generative hierarchy.

**Biologically plausible learning.** Conventional error backpropagation poses several biological problems, including exact transpose relationships, nonlocal gradients and storage of detailed forward states. Predictive-coding models offer one alternative: Whittington and Bogacz showed that a predictive-coding network using local Hebbian-like plasticity can approximate backpropagation-like learning, while Sacramento et al. demonstrated that multilayer circuits with apical dendritic mismatches and interneurons can support local continuous error-driven learning and classification. [[24]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28333583/) Guerguiev et al. similarly exploited segregated dendritic compartments in a multilayer spiking model. [[25]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/22901) These studies do not establish that cortex literally performs backpropagation; rather, they demonstrate that useful hidden-layer credit can be generated without imposing standard machine-learning backpropagation as the biological mechanism.

At the synaptic scale, STDP establishes a natural millisecond-level eligibility signal: classic experiments showed that the sign and magnitude of synaptic change depend strongly on relative pre/post spike timing. [[26]](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/18/24/10464) BCM supplies a slower competitive/homeostatic principle through an activity-dependent sliding modification threshold. [[27]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7054394/) Synaptic tagging and capture supplies still another timescale: Frey and Morris distinguished an early LTP phase from protein-synthesis-dependent persistent potentiation and reported a decaying synaptic tag lasting less than several hours in their preparation. [[28]](https://www.nature.com/articles/385533a0) Thus a biologically richer classifier should contain **milliseconds, seconds and consolidation-timescale states**, rather than representing all learning as one instantaneous weight update.

Finally, reinforcement does not have to arrive simultaneously with the spikes that created an eligibility trace. Brzosko et al. demonstrated retroactive dopaminergic modulation of hippocampal STDP and explicitly interpreted the result as evidence for a synaptic eligibility mechanism capable of linking earlier neural activity to later reinforcement. [[29]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09685) E-prop formalizes the same general decomposition computationally: locally computable eligibility traces are combined with later neuron-specific or broadcast learning signals, avoiding backpropagation through time. [[30]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17236-y) This is the basis for using dopamine-like signals as a **third factor**, rather than as the carrier of detailed hidden-layer gradients.

## Proposed Network Architecture

I propose a four-system architecture: a sensory encoder, a two-level predictive cortical hierarchy, a recurrent engram memory, and a winner-take-all classification circuit. Three modulatory systems regulate learning rather than carrying detailed stimulus representations.

| --- |

The separation of positive and negative error populations is important. A scalar residual

$$
 \epsilon =x - \hat{x}
$$

would ordinarily be allowed to take either sign, whereas spike rates are nonnegative. EPC-SNN therefore uses

$$
{ \epsilon }^{+}=[x - \hat{x}{]}_{+},  { \epsilon }^{ - }=[\hat{x} - x{]}_{+},
$$

and reconstructs the signed computational residual as

$$
 \epsilon ={ \epsilon }^{+} - { \epsilon }^{ - }.
$$

Both ${ \epsilon }^{+}$ and ${ \epsilon }^{ - }$ populations are excitatory. Negative effects on downstream neurons are implemented through local inhibitory interneurons. The scheme consequently does **not** require a neuron to emit excitatory output for one error sign and inhibitory output for the other.

The predictive hierarchy has two signal directions. Ascending connections convey unexplained sensory information; descending connections generate predictions. This follows the computational logic of hierarchical predictive coding, while the precise division among pyramidal and interneuron populations is a proposed implementation informed by, but not identical to, canonical predictive-circuit hypotheses. [[31]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn0199_79)

Each representation neuron is conceptually compartmentalized. Basal drive carries feedforward evidence, whereas apical drive carries feedback from the higher predictive layer and, for ${R}_{2}$, from retrieved memory. This is motivated by dendritic-learning models in which the mismatch between feedback and locally predicted apical activity provides a neuron-accessible teaching signal. Sacramento et al. explicitly demonstrated a multilayer model in which local dendritic prediction errors arise from mismatch between top-down feedback and interneuron predictions and are sufficient for useful classification learning. [[32]](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8089-dendritic-cortical-microcircuits-approximate-the-backpropagation-algorithm)

The engram module differs from a generic recurrent hidden layer in four ways:

**Sparse competitive allocation.** Only a fraction ${ \rho }_{M}$ of memory neurons strongly participates in a particular encoded episode. Allocation probability depends on input drive, current excitability and inhibition. This directly incorporates the experimentally supported role of relative excitability in memory allocation. [[33]](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1139438)

**Recurrent pattern completion.** Strong recurrent E→E connections among co-active allocated neurons allow a partial cortical cue to reinstate the larger ensemble. The reinstated ensemble then feeds back to ${R}_{2}$, where it behaves as a retrieved prior rather than only as a downstream stored representation. The latter interaction is motivated by the empirical association between hippocampal pattern completion and predictive activity in visual cortex. [[22]](https://www.nature.com/neuro/articles?page=10&searchType=journalSearch&sort=PubDate&year=2016)

**Plastic inhibition.** Inhibitory connections prevent a memory ensemble from recruiting an ever-growing fraction of the network. Computational engram work and theoretical inhibitory-plasticity studies show that activity-dependent inhibitory plasticity can stabilize recurrent memory dynamics. [[18]](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1211095?ijkey=7sbMtv2j1jVv2&keytype=ref&siteid=sci)

**Fast and slow memory states.** An episode first creates eligible and tagged synapses. Only a subset of these changes transitions into a slower consolidated component, following the logic of synaptic tagging/capture rather than instantaneous permanent storage. [[11]](https://www.nature.com/articles/385533a0)

The output contains ten excitatory decision populations, one for each MNIST class, coupled to a shared inhibitory winner-take-all circuit. During supervised training, the correct class receives an external teaching depolarization. During inference that teaching input is absent, and classification is determined by competition among decision populations driven jointly by ${R}_{2}$ and the retrieved memory ensemble.

Neuromodulatory pathways have **different computational responsibilities**. A dopamine-like signal gates conversion of recent eligibility into plasticity after correct/incorrect outcomes; an acetylcholine-like signal alters the precision or effective gain of sensory prediction errors; and an optional noradrenergic novelty signal increases engram-allocation probability when global surprise is high. The first assignment has relatively direct support from dopaminergic modulation of eligible plasticity, whereas the precision and novelty assignments are best treated as mechanistic hypotheses rather than exact one-neuromodulator/one-variable identities. [[34]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09685)

## Mathematical Formulation

**Spiking neuron dynamics.** Every recurrent cortical, engram and decision neuron is a conductance-based LIF neuron with spike-frequency adaptation. This choice provides enough biological structure for timing-dependent plasticity and replay without introducing the large parameter burden of Hodgkin–Huxley or detailed multicompartment conductance models. Recent engram simulations have used essentially this class of adaptive LIF model with AMPA-, NMDA- and GABA-like conductances. [[35]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

For neuron $i$,

$$
{C}_{m}\frac{d{V}_{i}}{dt}={g}_{L}({E}_{L} - {V}_{i})+{g}_{i}^{A}({E}_{E} - {V}_{i})+{g}_{i}^{N}({E}_{E} - {V}_{i})+{g}_{i}^{I}({E}_{I} - {V}_{i}) - {a}_{i}+{I}_{i}^{ext}+{I}_{i}^{mod}.
$$

A spike is emitted at

$$
{V}_{i}(t) \geq {V}_{th,i}(t)
$$

after which

$$
{V}_{i} \leftarrow {V}_{reset}
$$

for a refractory interval ${ \tau }_{ref}$.

Adaptation obeys

$$
{ \tau }_{a}\frac{d{a}_{i}}{dt}= - {a}_{i}+{b}_{a}{S}_{i}(t),
$$

with spike train

$$
{S}_{i}(t)=\sum_{k}^{​} { \delta }(t - {t}_{i}^{k}).
$$

Synaptic conductances are driven by presynaptic spikes:

$$
{ \tau }_{A}\frac{d{g}_{i}^{A}}{dt}= - {g}_{i}^{A}+\sum_{j}^{​} {{w}_{ij}^{A}}{S}_{j}(t),
$$

$$
{ \tau }_{N}\frac{d{g}_{i}^{N}}{dt}= - {g}_{i}^{N}+\sum_{j}^{​} {{w}_{ij}^{N}}{S}_{j}(t),
$$

$$
{ \tau }_{I}\frac{d{g}_{i}^{I}}{dt}= - {g}_{i}^{I}+\sum_{j}^{​} {{w}_{ij}^{I}}{S}_{j}(t).
$$

The slow NMDA component provides a natural bridge from millisecond spikes to the tens-to-hundreds-of-milliseconds inference dynamics needed for predictive settling. Adaptive LIF neurons together with distinct fast AMPA-like and slower NMDA-like conductances are also used in the recent Tomé et al. engram model. [[35]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

For predictive computations, spikes are converted locally into filtered rates,

$$
{ \tau }_{r}\frac{d{r}_{i}}{dt}= - {r}_{i}+{S}_{i}(t),
$$

where ${r}_{i}$ is not a separate artificial neuron state but a proxy for synaptic/dendritic low-pass filtering of spikes.

**Hierarchical prediction.** Let ${r}_{0}$ denote filtered sensory activity, ${r}_{1}$ and ${r}_{2}$ the two cortical representation levels, and $m$ the engram population.

The prediction of level $l - 1$ is

$$
{\hat{r}}_{l - 1}= \phi \left( {G}_{l}{r}_{l}+{U}_{l}m+{b}_{l - 1} \right),
$$

where ${U}_{l}=0$ except where memory feedback is anatomically instantiated.

Prediction precision is represented by diagonal gain matrix ${ \Pi }_{l - 1}$:

$$
{d}_{l - 1}={ \Pi }_{l - 1}^{1/2}({r}_{l - 1} - {\hat{r}}_{l - 1}).
$$

The firing-rate targets of positive and negative error populations are

$$
{e}_{l - 1}^{+}=[{d}_{l - 1}{]}_{+},
$$

$$
{e}_{l - 1}^{ - }=[ - {d}_{l - 1}{]}_{+}.
$$

Hence

$$
{d}_{l - 1}={e}_{l - 1}^{+} - {e}_{l - 1}^{ - }.
$$

A useful analytical objective, although not something any individual neuron must explicitly calculate, is

$$
{\mathcal{E}}_{PC}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l}^{​} { \parallel }{ \Pi }_{l}^{1/2}({r}_{l} - {\hat{r}}_{l}){ \parallel }_{2}^{2}+{ \lambda }_{s}\sum_{l}^{​} { \parallel }{r}_{l}{ \parallel }_{1}.
$$

Rao and Ballard's foundational formulation similarly treats hierarchical processing in terms of top-down predictions and residual prediction errors. [[12]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nn0199_79)

Representation dynamics can be implemented using **independent** feedforward and feedback pathways:

$$
{ \tau }_{l}{\dot{r}}_{l}= - {r}_{l}+ \phi \left[ {F}_{l}({e}_{l - 1}^{+} - {e}_{l - 1}^{ - }) - {H}_{l}({e}_{l}^{+} - {e}_{l}^{ - })+{g}_{M}{B}_{l}m \right].
$$

Crucially,

$$
{F}_{l} \neq {G}_{l}^{ \top }
$$

is allowed. Exact reciprocal weight symmetry is therefore **not** an architectural requirement. Separate pathways are trained by local rules and can become functionally aligned through learning. Dendritic predictive-learning models illustrate how local compartmental errors and interneuron circuitry can provide useful multilayer credit without assuming textbook backpropagation at each synapse. [[36]](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8089-dendritic-cortical-microcircuits-approximate-the-backpropagation-algorithm)

**Precision and acetylcholine.** A simple experimentally testable modulation is

$$
{ \Pi }_{l}(t)={ \Pi }_{l}^{(0)}exp\left[ { \kappa }_{A}A(t) \right],
$$

where $A(t)$ is normalized ACh-like activity. Higher ${ \Pi }_{l}$ makes a sensory residual more influential on cortical state estimation. Computational work has explicitly associated cholinergic modulation with precision/learning in predictive-processing formulations, but EPC-SNN treats the exact mapping as a hypothesis to test rather than settled physiology. [[37]](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/33/19/8227)

**Intrinsic excitability and engram allocation.** Memory neuron $i$ has a slowly varying excitability variable ${ \xi }_{i}$,

$$
{V}_{th,i}={V}_{th,0} - { \xi }_{i},
$$

with

$$
{ \tau }_{ \xi }{\dot{ \xi }}_{i}= - { \xi }_{i}+{ \eta }_{ \xi }{g}_{enc} {r}_{i}^{M}.
$$

Cells active during encoding therefore remain temporarily easier to recruit. The biological inspiration is the experimentally supported relationship between relative excitability and memory allocation; the exact differential equation is a model proposal. [[38]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25102562/)

The proposed allocation score is

$$
{A}_{i}^{alloc}={ \alpha }_{r}{\bar{r}}_{i}^{M}+{ \alpha }_{ \xi }{ \xi }_{i}+{ \alpha }_{ \epsilon }\mathcal{S} - { \alpha }_{I}{\bar{I}}_{i},
$$

where

$$
\mathcal{S}=\sum_{l}^{​} {\frac{1}{{N}_{l}}} \parallel {e}_{l}^{+}+{e}_{l}^{ - }{ \parallel }_{1}
$$

is global unsigned predictive surprise.

During encoding, the highest-scoring fraction ${ \rho }_{M}$ is eligible to develop strong recurrent plasticity. Importantly, the first two terms—activity and excitability—are grounded in engram-allocation work, whereas

$$
{ \alpha }_{ \epsilon }>0
$$

is a **new hypothesis of EPC-SNN**: prediction error should bias which experiences receive durable episodic allocation.

**Recurrent memory and retrieval.** Filtered memory activity obeys the population-level approximation

$$
{ \tau }_{M}\dot{m}= - m+ \phi \left( {W}_{MM}m+{W}_{CM}{r}_{2} - {W}_{IM}{i}_{M}+{b}_{M} \right).
$$

Partial sensory cues propagate to ${r}_{2}$, which cues $m$; recurrent ${W}_{MM}$ then performs pattern completion. Retrieved memory contributes the high-level cortical prior

$$
{I}_{2}^{M}={g}_{ret}{U}_{M}m.
$$

This is the key architectural coupling between engram memory and predictive inference.

**STDP eligibility.** For a synapse $j \rightarrow i$, define traces

$$
{ \tau }_{x}{\dot{x}}_{j}= - {x}_{j}+{S}_{j},
$$

$$
{ \tau }_{y}{\dot{y}}_{i}= - {y}_{i}+{S}_{i},
$$

and a slower postsynaptic trace

$$
{ \tau }_{z}{\dot{z}}_{i}= - {z}_{i}+{S}_{i}.
$$

A simplified triplet-like eligibility is

$$
{e}_{ij}^{STDP}={A}_{+}{x}_{j}{z}_{i}{S}_{i} - {A}_{ - }{y}_{i}{S}_{j}.
$$

Triplet STDP is attractive for recurrent engram connections because it allows rate dependence beyond simple pair timing; recent spiking engram models have explicitly used triplet STDP alongside additional stabilizing mechanisms. [[39]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

The eligibility itself does not immediately have to become a persistent synaptic change:

$$
{ \tau }_{e}{\dot{E}}_{ij}= - {E}_{ij}+{e}_{ij}^{STDP}.
$$

A delayed modulatory signal ${M}_{i}(t)$ then converts eligibility into fast plasticity:

$$
{\dot{w}}_{ij}^{F}={ \eta }_{w}{M}_{i}(t){E}_{ij} - { \lambda }_{F}{w}_{ij}^{F}.
$$

This is a three-factor rule:

$$
\text{presynaptic activity} \times \text{postsynaptic state} \times \text{modulatory/teaching factor}.
$$

The decomposition is biologically motivated by work showing that dopamine can act retrospectively on synapses rendered eligible by previous spike timing, and computationally by e-prop's combination of local eligibility traces and later learning signals. [[40]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09685)

Different synapse classes use different third factors rather than forcing one global formula:

| Synapses | Eligibility/local factor | Third factor | Functional purpose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Higher-level → lower-level generative ${G}_{l}$ | preactivity × local residual | local dendritic prediction error | Improve sensory prediction |
| Lower error → representation ${F}_{l}$ | pre-error activity × post activity | apical mismatch | Improve inference mapping |
| Memory E→E | triplet STDP eligibility | encoding/salience gate $g_Dg_{\rm enc}$ | Bind co-active engram cells |
| Memory I→E | presynaptic inhibitory activity | local E-rate deviation from target | E/I stabilization |
| ${R}_{2},M \rightarrow $ class output | STDP eligibility | label/outcome error | Supervised classification |
| Cortical recurrent E→E | voltage/STDP eligibility | local prediction mismatch | Feature assembly formation |

**BCM-like metaplasticity.** A slower postsynaptic activity threshold is

$$
{ \tau }_{ \theta }{\dot{ \theta }}_{i}=⟨{r}_{i}^{2}⟩ - { \theta }_{i}.
$$

A BCM-like factor

$$
{B}_{ij}={r}_{j}{r}_{i}({r}_{i} - { \theta }_{i})
$$

can either contribute weakly to plasticity or adjust the LTP/LTD balance in the STDP rule. The purpose is not to add another independent learner, but to make frequently active neurons progressively harder to potentiate, supporting competition. BCM's defining idea is precisely an activity-dependent modification threshold rather than a fixed Hebbian threshold. [[41]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7054394/)

**Inhibitory homeostasis.** For memory inhibitory synapses,

$$
 \Delta {w}_{ij}^{I \rightarrow E}={ \eta }_{I}({\bar{r}}_{i} - {r}_{0}){x}_{j}^{I}.
$$

Thus, when the target excitatory neuron fires persistently above ${r}_{0}$, active inhibitory inputs strengthen; when it falls below target, they weaken. This equation is deliberately simpler than specific published inhibitory-STDP formulations but follows their homeostatic logic. Vogels et al. established inhibitory-plasticity models that balance excitation and inhibition, and Tomé et al. used activity-dependent inhibitory STDP to stabilize their engram network. [[18]](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1211095?ijkey=7sbMtv2j1jVv2&keytype=ref&siteid=sci)

**Synaptic tagging and consolidation.** Fast eligibility and permanent memory are separated by a tag,

$$
{ \tau }_{q}{\dot{q}}_{ij}= - {q}_{ij}+H(|{E}_{ij}| - { \vartheta }_{q}),
$$

and a cell-wide plasticity-resource variable,

$$
{ \tau }_{p}{\dot{p}}_{i}= - {p}_{i}+{g}_{D}(t)H({\bar{r}}_{i} - {r}_{p}).
$$

The consolidated component changes according to

$$
{ \tau }_{c}{\dot{w}}_{ij}^{L}={ \kappa }_{c}{q}_{ij}{p}_{i}\left( {w}_{ij}^{F} - {w}_{ij}^{L} \right).
$$

Effective synaptic efficacy is

$$
{w}_{ij}={w}_{ij}^{0}+{w}_{ij}^{F}+{w}_{ij}^{L}.
$$

This is not intended as a molecularly exact model of protein synthesis. It captures the defining synaptic-tagging/capture distinction: local activity marks eligible synapses, whereas a slower cell-wide process determines which tagged changes become durable. Frey and Morris' experiments motivated precisely this separation between local tags and persistent protein-dependent potentiation. [[28]](https://www.nature.com/articles/385533a0)

**Classification.** Let ${S}_{k}^{O}$ be spikes of class-output population $k$. Its decision rate is

$$
{ \nu }_{k}=\frac{1}{{T}_{dec}}\int_{{t}_{0}}^{{t}_{0}+{T}_{dec}} {{S}_{k}^{O}}(t) dt.
$$

The prediction is

$$
\hat{y}=arg\mathop{max}\limits_{k \in {0, \ldots ,9}}{ \nu }_{k}.
$$

During training the target class population receives a transient teaching depolarization. A scalar reward/outcome error can be

$$
D(t)=R(t) - \hat{R}(t),
$$

or, for strictly supervised training, a class-specific modulatory signal can be

$$
{D}_{k}={y}_{k} - {\tilde{ \nu }}_{k}.
$$

This signal gates existing readout eligibility rather than calculating hidden-layer derivatives.

A critical methodological point is that **biological consolidation time should not be simulated one biological millisecond at a time** for MNIST. Experimental synaptic tagging can persist on much longer scales than ordinary membrane and STDP dynamics. [[28]](https://www.nature.com/articles/385533a0)

## Learning, Engram Formation, and Predictive Credit Assignment

Training should alternate between **online inference/encoding** and **offline-like consolidation**, rather than treating the entire dataset as one stationary optimization pass.

| --- |

**Predictive settling.** On each image, cortical plasticity is initially low. The sensory encoder injects spikes, ${R}_{1}$ and ${R}_{2}$ respond, descending generative predictions suppress the explained portion of lower-level activity, and positive/negative error populations represent the residual. The system is iterated for roughly 50–250 ms until prediction energy

$$
{\mathcal{E}}_{PC}(t)
$$

stabilizes or until a fixed presentation deadline.

The architectural purpose of this settling is twofold. First, it infers a latent representation useful for classification. Second, it leaves every cortical level with a **local mismatch quantity** that can subsequently gate its own plasticity. This is precisely the computational attraction of predictive/dendritic error models for biological credit assignment: the hidden synapse need not receive a digitally transported derivative from the final class loss. [[24]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28333583/)

**Local cortical learning.** A descending generative synapse from high-level neuron $j$ to lower-level target $i$ can use

$$
 \Delta {G}_{ij}={ \eta }_{G} { \epsilon }_{i} {r}_{j},
$$

where ${ \epsilon }_{i}$ is available in the postsynaptic comparator circuit. In spiking form,

$$
 \Delta {G}_{ij}={ \eta }_{G}{M}_{i}^{PC}{E}_{ij},
$$

with ${M}_{i}^{PC}$ derived from the local dendritic mismatch. All variables needed for the update are therefore present at or near the synapse.

Feedforward recognition connections need not be tied to ${G}^{ \top }$. They adapt according to correlations between lower-level residuals and the postsynaptic representation, with apical mismatch influencing whether an active postsynaptic neuron should strengthen or weaken those inputs. This resembles the general strategy of dendritic error models rather than exact gradient transport. [[43]](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8089-dendritic-cortical-microcircuits-approximate-the-backpropagation-algorithm)

**Engram allocation.** Once the cortical representation has settled, the memory network receives ${R}_{2}$. Encoding gate strength is

$$
{g}_{enc}= \sigma \left[ { \beta }_{S}(\mathcal{S} - { \vartheta }_{S})+{ \beta }_{D}|D|+{ \beta }_{N}N \right],
$$

where $\mathcal{S}$ is unsigned predictive surprise, $D$ an outcome/salience variable and $N$ an optional novelty signal.

Within the memory network, allocation depends on relative

$$
{A}_{i}^{alloc},
$$

so that only the highest-scoring neurons receive enhanced recurrent potentiation. Baseline excitability heterogeneity is essential: removing it provides a direct ablation of the memory-allocation mechanism. The empirical literature supports excitability-biased allocation; the proposed additional dependence on predictive surprise is the integration point that distinguishes EPC-SNN from a simple engram model. [[38]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25102562/)

The model should **not force one fixed engram per digit**. Different examples of the same digit may initially recruit partially distinct episodic ensembles. Repeated experience and replay can subsequently increase overlap among examples sharing similar high-level representations. This is more consistent with biological memory than hard-coding ten class-specific attractors, and the ability of temporally related memories to occupy overlapping ensembles is supported experimentally. [[9]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17955)

**Label and neuromodulator phase.** At the end of a training trial, the correct output population is transiently activated. A dopamine-like factor then multiplies eligibility traces that were generated earlier:

$$
 \Delta {w}_{ij} \propto D(t){E}_{ij}(t).
$$

Thus the outcome signal says, approximately, **“plasticity associated with the recent event should count”**, not “increase synapse 4,318 by exactly $0.00173$.” This is much closer to experimentally motivated three-factor learning. Dopamine has been shown to alter the outcome of preceding timing-dependent plasticity, and e-prop shows mathematically how eligibility plus later learning signals can substitute for explicit temporal backpropagation in recurrent spiking networks. [[40]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09685)

For standard MNIST supervision, using explicit label information is unavoidable if the objective is supervised accuracy. Biological plausibility concerns **how the label affects upstream plasticity**, not pretending that a supervised task contains no teaching information. In a reinforcement variant, labels can instead be replaced by binary reward after the network's decision, providing a stricter three-factor test.

**Competition and homeostasis.** STDP alone can create pathological positive feedback: neurons that happen to win early can become increasingly likely to win. The model therefore combines activity-dependent inhibition, adaptation, weight bounds and a BCM-like sliding threshold. The rationale is supported by theoretical work on competitive Hebbian/STDP learning, voltage-based STDP with homeostasis and inhibitory plasticity. [[44]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10966623/)

A particularly informative prediction is that inhibition should shape **engram specificity**, not merely mean firing rate. Tomé et al.'s simulations found that inhibitory as well as excitatory plasticity participated in the emergence and stabilization of selective engram dynamics. [[45]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w) EPC-SNN therefore predicts that blocking memory inhibitory plasticity will increase ensemble overlap and false pattern completion even if overall network activity is externally normalized.

**Offline replay and consolidation.** After a training block, direct sensory input is reduced and a subset of recent memory ensembles is spontaneously or probabilistically replayed. Replay can be seeded according to

$$
P(replay e) \propto exp\left( { \beta }_{R}[{\mathcal{S}}_{e}+{ \lambda }_{R}|{D}_{e}|] \right),
$$

so surprising or behaviorally important episodes are preferentially reactivated.

A memory replay activates $m$, which reconstructs ${R}_{2}$; ${R}_{2}$ generates ${R}_{1}$; and ${R}_{1}$ generates sensory-level activity. Residual cortical error during replay tunes the generative model to explain the stored memory more accurately. Simultaneously, repeated coactivation renews relevant synaptic tags and allows tagged fast recurrent memory connections to enter the consolidated component.

This gives consolidation an explicit computational role:

$$
\text{episodic engram} \rightarrow \text{cortical prediction training} \rightarrow \text{better generative representation}.
$$

The mechanism provides a route by which episodic memories become progressively integrated into a predictive model without simply copying all hippocampal weights into cortex. Contemporary experimental and computational work indicates that memory representations and circuit contributions evolve during consolidation rather than remaining fixed copies of the original encoding state. [[46]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28386011/)

Replay also creates **engram turnover**. A cell that was strongly active during initial encoding can leave the functional ensemble if its recurrent connections weaken or its excitability bias decays, while previously peripheral neurons can join as recurrent and feedforward weights reorganize. That prediction is consistent with the dynamic engrams obtained in Tomé et al.'s computational consolidation study. [[47]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

**Retrieval and inference.** At test time, dopamine-driven weight updates are disabled or sharply reduced. An image first produces bottom-up predictive activity. If the high-level state overlaps a stored trace, it activates part of the appropriate engram. Recurrent connectivity completes the trace:

$$
{r}_{2} \rightarrow {m}_{partial} \rightarrow {m}_{complete}.
$$

Memory feedback then changes high-level inference:

$$
{m}_{complete} \rightarrow {\hat{r}}_{2} \rightarrow {\hat{r}}_{1} \rightarrow {\hat{r}}_{0}.
$$

For a clean familiar image, the memory feedback may have little effect because feedforward evidence is already decisive. For an occluded or corrupted image, it should supply missing latent structure. This is the model's main predicted functional advantage and is consistent with the conceptual link between hippocampal pattern completion and cortical prediction. [[14]](https://www.nature.com/neuro/articles?page=10&searchType=journalSearch&sort=PubDate&year=2016)

The resulting credit-assignment hierarchy can be summarized as

$$
\boxed{\text{spike timing} \rightarrow \text{synaptic eligibility} \rightarrow \text{local predictive mismatch} \rightarrow \text{neuromodulatory gating} \rightarrow \text{tagged change} \rightarrow \text{consolidation}}
$$

rather than

$$
\boxed{\text{global loss} \rightarrow \text{exact reverse-mode gradient} \rightarrow \text{instantaneous weight update}.}
$$

This does not mean EPC-SNN mathematically reproduces backpropagation exactly. Its success should be judged empirically against both conventional backpropagation and local-learning baselines.

## Simulation Plan, Ablations, and Expected Results

The first study should deliberately separate **proof of mechanism** from **state-of-the-art classification performance**. MNIST is an appropriate default because its handwritten-digit classification task is well characterized and small enough to permit many plasticity and circuit ablations. [[7]](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/726791) A synthetic associative dataset should be run in parallel because MNIST alone does not provide clean control over memory overlap, temporal proximity or cue completeness.

**Datasets and perturbations.**

For MNIST, use the canonical training/test protocol with pixel values normalized to $[0,1]$. Pixels should initially be converted into either Poisson spike trains

$$
{ \lambda }_{p}={ \lambda }_{max}{x}_{p}
$$

or latency-coded spikes

$$
{t}_{p}={t}_{min}+(1 - {x}_{p})({t}_{max} - {t}_{min}).
$$

Poisson coding is the safer first implementation because it is straightforward, whereas latency coding is useful as a later efficiency check.

The main test set should include clean digits plus controlled corruption:

$$
\text{mask fraction} \in {0,0.2,0.4,0.6},
$$

additive noise at several signal-to-noise levels, and partial cues confined to image quadrants. These conditions are critical because a memory-augmented predictive system should earn its additional complexity through robustness rather than merely matching a feedforward network on clean digits.

The synthetic benchmark should contain sparse binary or Gaussian prototype patterns with independently controllable within-class similarity, between-class similarity, corruption and temporal spacing. This allows explicit tests of the Cai-style temporal-overlap hypothesis: experiences presented close together should form more-overlapping ensembles when the excitability trace is active, whereas the same examples separated beyond ${ \tau }_{ \xi }$ should be represented more independently. The experimental motivation for time-dependent ensemble overlap comes from memory-linking studies; applying it to synthetic classification patterns is a model extrapolation. [[20]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17955)

**Core model comparison.**

| Variant | Predictive hierarchy | Recurrent engram memory | Excitability allocation | Tag/capture consolidation | Memory→cortex feedback | Expected signature |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Full EPC-SNN** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Best combined robustness, continual retention and cue completion |
| Predictive-only | Yes | No | No | No | No | Strong clean classification/reconstruction; weaker episodic cue completion |
| Engram-only | Minimal feedforward encoder | Yes | Yes | Yes | No predictive loop | Strong stored-pattern retrieval but poorer abstraction/generalization |
| No allocation | Yes | Yes | Random | Yes | Yes | More diffuse engrams; lower temporal linking and greater interference |
| No consolidation | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Yes | Good immediate recall but stronger forgetting/engram drift |
| No memory feedback | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | No | Memory may aid output directly but cannot repair cortical prediction under occlusion |
| No inhibitory plasticity | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Enlarged/overlapping assemblies, higher false recall and unstable rates |
| No precision modulation | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Reduced adaptation to uncertainty/noise |
| No PE→allocation term | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes | Tests the novel hypothesis that surprise prioritizes engram storage |
| Backprop/SNN reference | No biological constraint | Optional | No | No | No | Accuracy ceiling/computational control rather than biological comparator |

The most informative ablation is **No PE→allocation**, because it isolates the genuinely integrative claim from a mere concatenation of predictive coding and an associative memory.

Define future-memory recruitment probability as

$$
{P}_{i}^{engram}=P(i \in {E}_{future}),
$$

and fit

$$
logit{P}_{i}^{engram}={ \beta }_{0}+{ \beta }_{r}{r}_{i}+{ \beta }_{ \xi }{ \xi }_{i}+{ \beta }_{ \epsilon }\mathcal{S}+{ \beta }_{int}{ \xi }_{i}\mathcal{S}.
$$

The model's new prediction is

$$
{ \beta }_{ \epsilon }>0
$$

after controlling for neural activity and intrinsic excitability. The result should disappear in the PE-allocation ablation.

**Experimental protocol.**

A robust study would use at least five independent random seeds per architecture and perform all ablations at matched neuron counts where possible. Because recurrent spiking networks can be sensitive to initialization, reporting only the best seed would provide weak evidence.

Each seed should include an initial **predictive pretraining phase** with no class supervision, followed by labeled encoding and interleaved replay. This tests whether the cortical generative model can first learn reusable visual structure rather than having all feature learning driven directly by labels.

A practical schedule is:

| Phase | Neural state | Plastic synapses | Suggested duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unsupervised pre-exposure | sensory + PC hierarchy | cortical predictive | 1–5 passes over training images |
| Labeled encoding | all online circuits | cortical + memory + readout | 100–300 ms/image |
| Outcome pulse | recent eligibility retained | gated fast plasticity | 50–500 ms equivalent |
| Inter-block consolidation | spontaneous/replayed memory | memory + cortical slow plasticity | 1–10 replay sweeps/block |
| Evaluation | full inference; teaching absent | frozen or near-frozen | 100–500 ms/image |
| Long-term retention test | delayed/replay-modified state | frozen during measurement | after each sequential block |

**Primary metrics.** Classification accuracy alone is insufficient. The principal outcome should be a vector of behavioral and mechanistic measurements:

$$
{\text{accuracy},\text{robust accuracy},\text{forgetting},{\mathcal{E}}_{PC},\text{reconstruction error},\text{engram sparsity},\text{engram overlap},\text{turnover},\text{pattern completion},\text{firing cost}}.
$$

Clean classification accuracy is

$$
{A}_{clean}=\frac{{N}_{correct}}{{N}_{test}},
$$

and corruption retention can be expressed as

$$
{R}_{corr}(q)=\frac{A(q)}{A(0)},
$$

where $q$ is corruption severity.

Continual-learning forgetting for task/class block $b$ is

$$
{F}_{b}=\mathop{max}\limits_{t \leq T}{A}_{b,t} - {A}_{b,T}.
$$

Engram sparsity is

$$
{S}_{E}=\frac{|E|}{{N}_{M}},
$$

and overlap between episodes $a,b$ is preferably reported as Jaccard similarity

$$
J({E}_{a},{E}_{b})=\frac{|{E}_{a} \cap {E}_{b}|}{|{E}_{a} \cup {E}_{b}|}.
$$

Engram turnover between consolidation times ${t}_{1},{t}_{2}$ can be reported as

$$
{T}_{E}=1 - J({E}_{{t}_{1}},{E}_{{t}_{2}}).
$$

Using overlap/turnover explicitly is important because consolidation models predict that engram identity can evolve even while memory remains retrievable. [[47]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

Pattern completion gain should compare full and partial cues:

$$
{G}_{PCmpl}=sim({m}_{partial},{m}_{full}) - sim({r}_{2,partial},{r}_{2,full}).
$$

A positive value means recurrent memory restores latent structure beyond what the feedforward cortical code already supplied.

The key expected interaction is

$$
 \Delta {A}_{memory}={A}_{full} - {A}_{predictive - only}
$$

increasing with cue degradation:

$$
\frac{ \partial  \Delta {A}_{memory}}{ \partial q}>0
$$

for moderate corruption $q$.

A second predicted interaction concerns surprise. Let ${S}_{e}$ denote predictive surprise during encoding and ${R}_{e}$ later recall success. In the full model,

$$
corr({S}_{e},{R}_{e})
$$

should become positive within a range where surprise triggers allocation but does not reflect completely unlearnable noise. In the PE→allocation ablation that relationship should diminish. An **inverted-U** relationship is plausible at extreme surprise because random-noise stimuli produce high prediction error but little reusable structure; this possibility should be evaluated rather than hidden by averaging.

A third prediction concerns consolidation:

$$
{\mathcal{E}}_{PC}^{replay}(n+1)<{\mathcal{E}}_{PC}^{replay}(n)
$$

for repeatedly replayed memories, even without new sensory input. This would demonstrate that the engram is teaching the cortical generative system rather than merely storing a redundant classifier representation.

**Engineering success criteria.** For an initial mechanistic paper, the model need not establish MNIST state of the art. Reasonable preregistered targets could be clean accuracy above 90%, preservation of at least 70% of clean accuracy under a predefined severe masking condition, a statistically significant advantage of the full system over predictive-only under partial cues, and significantly lower sequential forgetting than an otherwise matched no-consolidation condition. These are proposed acceptance thresholds, not empirical forecasts.

**Statistical analysis.** Model comparisons should use seed-level paired contrasts whenever variants share initialization and input order. For corruption experiments, fit accuracy with model variant, corruption severity and their interaction. Engram overlap should be compared against a degree- and size-matched shuffled ensemble null. Recruitment analyses should control for firing rate before attributing variance to excitability or prediction error. This last control is especially important because a trivial correlation between surprise and engram membership could arise if surprising stimuli simply evoke more spikes.

**Suggested figures.** Beyond the architecture diagram, the most informative paper-quality figures would be:

- a raster plot of sensory, ${R}_{1}$, ${R}_{2}$, engram E/I and decision populations for clean versus occluded instances of the same digit;

- input, top-down reconstruction and residual-error images at successive settling times;

- prediction energy ${\mathcal{E}}_{PC}$ versus inference time for full and no-memory-feedback variants;

- accuracy versus masking severity with confidence intervals across seeds;

- engram Jaccard overlap versus temporal separation between two learning episodes;

- engram membership Sankey/alluvial visualization or turnover curve across consolidation epochs;

- recurrent E→E and I→E weight distributions before encoding, immediately after encoding and after consolidation;

- eligibility ${E}_{ij}$, synaptic tag ${q}_{ij}$, dopamine pulse and fast/slow weight components for representative synapses;

- a confusion matrix for clean and corrupted MNIST;

- firing-rate and spike-count distributions to verify that accuracy is not obtained through unrealistic persistent high-rate activity.

The input/reconstruction/error figure is especially diagnostic. A genuine predictive-memory interaction should show that memory retrieval changes the **internal reconstruction and subsequent residual**, not merely that a memory neuron provides another direct shortcut to the class output.

## Neuroscientific Predictions, Limitations, and Prioritized Sources

The architecture is useful only if it generates predictions that distinguish it from generic associative-memory and predictive-coding models.

**Prediction-error-dependent engram allocation.** The strongest new prediction is that, holding sensory-evoked activity and baseline intrinsic excitability constant, larger cortical predictive mismatch during an episode should increase the probability that neurons participating in that episode become members of a subsequently reactivatable engram:

$$
P(engram∣r, \xi ,{\mathcal{S}}_{high})>P(engram∣r, \xi ,{\mathcal{S}}_{low}).
$$

A plausible experiment would combine cortical and hippocampal calcium/electrophysiological recording with activity-dependent memory tagging. Stimulus sequences could be engineered so that identical items are either highly expected or surprising. The critical analysis would regress later engram membership against surprise while controlling for firing rate and excitability proxies. Existing work establishes excitability-dependent allocation, but not this particular predictive-error term; a failure of the term after adequate power would directly falsify a central EPC-SNN assumption. [[38]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25102562/)

**Engram reactivation should suppress matched sensory error.** If a memory ensemble corresponding to an expected stimulus is reactivated immediately before that stimulus, the model predicts reduced subsequent error-population responses:

$$
{e}_{matched}^{+}+{e}_{matched}^{ - }<{e}_{control}^{+}+{e}_{control}^{ - }.
$$

If the same engram predicts a stimulus that conflicts with the incoming input, error should instead rise. This provides a bidirectional test: memory is not simply expected to suppress cortex globally; it should suppress **matched** input and enhance the residual created by mismatches. The empirical association between hippocampal pattern completion and predictive visual responses motivates this experiment. [[22]](https://www.nature.com/neuro/articles?page=10&searchType=journalSearch&sort=PubDate&year=2016)

**Memory feedback should matter disproportionately for partial evidence.** Transiently silencing hippocampal/engram-to-cortical feedback is predicted to impair recognition of occluded or ambiguous stimuli more strongly than clean, high-contrast familiar stimuli:

$$
 \Delta {A}_{silence}^{partial}> \Delta {A}_{silence}^{clean}.
$$

A generic hippocampal-lesion effect would be less specific. EPC-SNN predicts the impairment should track the amount of missing information that can be filled by pattern completion.

**Manipulating excitability should bias both memory identity and later cortical prediction.** Increasing intrinsic excitability in a subset of candidate memory neurons before encoding should increase their probability of allocation, consistent with the existing allocation literature. [[33]](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1139438) EPC-SNN adds the prediction that later activation of those biased cells should measurably change cortical prediction signals for the associated sensory pattern. Thus the causal chain would be

$$
\text{excitability manipulation} \rightarrow \text{engram allocation} \rightarrow \text{retrieval} \rightarrow \text{sensory prediction}.
$$

**Inhibitory plasticity should govern engram selectivity.** Blocking inhibitory plasticity during consolidation is predicted to produce larger, more-overlapping ensembles and more false pattern completion, even if an experimenter restores mean excitatory firing rates through another manipulation. This distinguishes “inhibition as simple rate normalization” from the stronger EPC-SNN claim that plastic inhibition helps sculpt memory specificity. Computational engram work already finds inhibitory plasticity important for stable/selective dynamics, giving this prediction a concrete precedent. [[48]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

**Replay should selectively reduce future prediction error.** Replayed experiences should become better predicted on later encounters:

$$
 \Delta {\mathcal{S}}_{replayed}< \Delta {\mathcal{S}}_{nonreplayed},
$$

with the strongest effect for representations whose tags were active during replay. Blocking replay or interfering with consolidation should therefore leave later recognition more dependent on hippocampal pattern completion and less well captured by cortical generative predictions. Dynamic reorganization of engrams during consolidation in computational work provides a mechanistic precedent, while broader systems-consolidation work establishes that cortical and hippocampal contributions evolve after learning. [[49]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01551-w)

**Neuromodulator timing should dissociate eligibility from storage.** A dopamine-like pulse delivered after relevant neural activity but within the eligibility interval should reinforce tagged synapses more strongly than the same pulse delivered after eligibility has decayed. This temporal interaction has direct conceptual support from experiments showing retrospective dopaminergic modulation of timing-dependent plasticity. [[29]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09685) In contrast, manipulation of an ACh-like precision signal should change the relative impact of sensory residuals during inference more immediately. The resulting double dissociation would be more informative than asking whether either neuromodulator simply “improves learning.”

There are also important limitations.

First, **engram memory is not equivalent to a machine-learning class prototype**. Biological memory engrams are task-, circuit- and state-dependent neuronal/synaptic ensembles. The proposal therefore stores individual experiences and allows class-related organization to emerge through repeated encoding and consolidation instead of assuming one engram per output category. Causal engram studies and reviews support this more nuanced interpretation. [[19]](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028)

Second, **predictive coding is a computational framework, not an agreed cell-by-cell cortical wiring diagram**. The explicit positive/negative error populations in EPC-SNN are a convenient, Dale-compatible realization. Other implementations using dendritic mismatch within pyramidal neurons may be equally or more biologically realistic. Canonical-microcircuit and dendritic-learning proposals should therefore be compared rather than conflated with established anatomy. [[50]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23177956/)

Third, the model still contains some **engineered abstractions**: externally provided MNIST labels, global or region-wide neuromodulatory variables, compressed consolidation times and a hand-selected division between cortical and hippocampal-like modules. These abstractions make the mechanism testable but preclude claiming that it is a literal simulation of mammalian memory.

Fourth, prediction-error learning does not magically eliminate the entire credit-assignment problem. Predictive-coding and dendritic models demonstrate approximate/local solutions under particular assumptions, while e-prop likewise depends on appropriately informative learning signals. [[51]](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/8089-dendritic-cortical-microcircuits-approximate-the-backpropagation-algorithm) The full EPC-SNN should therefore be compared against exact-gradient references to measure the computational cost of biological constraints rather than assuming equivalent optimization.

Fifth, **MNIST is insufficient as a neuroscience benchmark**. Its static digits lack meaningful temporal structure, active sensing, context and delayed reward. After mechanistic validation, the model should progress to sequentially presented symbols, permuted/rotated continual-learning variants, temporally structured synthetic environments and eventually event-camera or naturalistic temporal data. MNIST is appropriate here because the objective of the first experiment is controlled circuit analysis, not ecological validity. [[7]](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/726791)

Sixth, the model may form **incorrect attractors**. Strong recurrent engram connections can turn a weak ambiguous cue into confident but wrong pattern completion. Predictive error offers a potential safeguard: when the retrieved memory predicts sensory evidence poorly,

$$
{\mathcal{S}}_{retrieved} \gg 0,
$$

the memory-feedback gate should be weakened. A useful extension is therefore reliability-dependent retrieval:

$$
{g}_{M}(t+ \Delta t)={g}_{M}(t)exp[ - { \kappa }_{S}\mathcal{S}(t)].
$$

This creates an experimentally relevant competition between memory priors and contradictory sensory evidence.

Seventh, the proposed ACh, dopamine and optional NE assignments should not be interpreted as strict biochemical identities. Neuromodulatory systems have diverse receptor-, cell-type-, region- and state-dependent effects. The computational variables “precision,” “plasticity gate” and “novelty” are abstractions whose correspondence with neuromodulators must itself be experimentally tested. Evidence for dopaminergic modulation of eligible plasticity is substantially more direct than a one-to-one mapping of every predictive-processing variable to a single transmitter. [[34]](https://elifesciences.org/articles/09685)

The overall falsifiable claim of EPC-SNN can therefore be stated compactly:

$$
\boxed{\text{prediction error determines what requires memory;} \text{engram retrieval determines what should be predicted next;} \text{local plasticity makes the two systems converge over consolidation}.}
$$

The first clause extends existing memory-allocation theory; the second is motivated by observed relationships between pattern completion and sensory prediction; and the third builds on predictive-learning, synaptic-tagging and replay/consolidation mechanisms. [[52]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25102562/)

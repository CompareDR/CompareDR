# Experimental protocol: vision-language models as stand-in labelers for skin-lesion images

## 1. Substitution framing and decision logic

The claim "a VLM can stand in for a human specialist" is a non-inferiority claim, not a superiority claim, and it is bounded by the *labeling task* the specialist is asked to perform. That task can range from a coarse binary (benign vs. malignant) through an eight-class ISIC taxonomy, a 7-point checklist of dermoscopic features, an ABCDE feature score, a triage priority, or a structured free-text report. The protocol must fix this label set before the study starts, because metric selection, reference-standard design, and power all depend on it. A VLM that produces high-quality top-3 differential diagnoses is not the same as a VLM that reproduces the exact ICD-O morphology code a dermatopathologist would assign, and the substitution claim should be evaluated against the most demanding task for which substitution is being proposed.

Two further framings matter. First, "stand in for" implies substitution, not augmentation; if the VLM is positioned as a triage filter, the bar is lower and a different decision rule applies. Second, the human specialist does not produce a single deterministic label — even board-certified dermatopathologists disagree. In an 8-pathologist panel review of 792 melanoma-suspicious lesions, full agreement was 53.5% and majority-vote (≥5/8) agreement 90.9%, with the expert panel disagreeing with the original local pathologists' and dermatologists' diagnoses in 14.9% and 33.5% of cases respectively [1]. The VLM should therefore be benchmarked against a *panel-derived* ground truth, not against a single source label.

## 2. Reference standard: panel composition, blinding, and adjudication

The ground truth in this study is itself an experimental artifact, and its design is the single most consequential decision in the protocol. The reference panel should comprise at least three practicing dermatologists and one dermatopathologist, blinded to each other and to the VLM outputs, with a chairperson who adjudicates non-consensus cases. Each specialist records the label independently using the locked label set, plus a confidence score (1–5) and a brief rationale. Adjudication uses a two-round Delphi: a structured discussion of cases with ≤3/4 panel agreement, with re-voting; unresolved cases are flagged and reported separately rather than coerced to consensus.

For the binary malignancy task, histology-confirmed biopsy reports serve as the tie-breaker, and only images with such a report are eligible. For multi-class diagnosis, the panel accepts a structured consensus and treats the dermatopathologist's histology reading as authoritative only when the clinical–pathological agreement fails. Reporting should follow TRIPOD-LLM (19 main items, 50 subitems) for the AI evaluation layer [2] and DECIDE-AI (17 AI-specific items, 10 generic items) for the early-stage clinical study framing [3]; the same protocol must adopt TRIPOD+AI's fairness-stratified reporting requirements [4].

## 3. Dataset design: sources, stratification, contamination controls

Public datasets are the obvious starting point, but most are not fit-for-purpose in their raw form. The protocol should use a tiered combination: (a) a **retrospective evaluation set** drawn from public benchmarks with skin-tone coverage, (b) a **prospective holdout** enrolled after the model snapshots are pinned, and (c) a **leakage probe** drawn from images that post-date every model's knowledge cutoff.

Tier (a) should include Diverse Dermatology Images (DDI; 656 images, 570 patients, FST I–II/III–IV/V–VI balanced, biopsy-confirmed) [5], Fitzpatrick17k (16,577 images, 114 conditions, but with ~13% FST V–VI representation, ~6,622 known duplicate pairs, and a 3.4% mislabel rate that requires deduplication before use) [6], SCIN (10,000+ images, weighted differential labels, but with only 0.57% FST-VI representation) [7], and the ISIC 2019/2024 archives for dermoscopic histopathology-confirmed cases. Tier (b) is the strongest defense against the contamination that has already been documented for medical VQA benchmarks (e.g., 19.8% image-side source overlap reported for SLAKE-En under SigLIP-B-16) [8]. Tier (c) is a small (≈100 images) cleanly sourced prospective set with documented acquisition date after the cutoff.

Sampling rules: patient-level (not lesion-level) splits to prevent leakage; stratification by Fitzpatrick or Monk skin tone, body site, lesion class, and image source (dermoscopic, smartphone, histopathology); minimum quotas so that no subgroup falls below 50 cases for any pre-specified analysis. Exclude duplicate and near-duplicate clusters identified by embedding-based deduplication (cosine ≥ 0.95, as in the Fitzpatrick17k audit [6]). Report class prevalence and skin-tone distribution in the Methods, not just in supplementary tables.

## 4. VLM conditions: models, prompts, reproducibility

The protocol should test at least six VLM configurations, spanning proprietary generalist, open-weight generalist, and medical-specialized families, and run each under at least three prompt strategies. The minimum matrix is: GPT-5, GPT-4o, Claude (or Gemini 2.5 Pro) for proprietary generalists; LLaVA-OneVision-Qwen2-7B, Qwen2.5-VL-7B, or InternVL3-8B for open-weight generalists; LLaVA-Med v1.5-Mistral-7B [9], MedGemma, and SkinGPT-4 or SkinCLIP-VL [10] for medical-specialized models. Each model is queried with: (i) zero-shot with a fixed structured prompt asking for top-1, top-3, and confidence; (ii) few-shot (k=3) using in-distribution exemplars from a *separate* training partition; (iii) chain-of-thought with structured JSON output, parsed and validated. Each condition is run at temperature 0 and again at temperature 0.7 across three random seeds to estimate self-consistency.

Two VLM behaviors deserve explicit instrumentation. First, refusals and safety-style abstentions: a documented finding is that Gemini disproportionately blocks dermatology cases on darker skin [11]; refusal rate must be reported per subgroup. Second, hallucinated confidence: a separate study showed that removing a single clinical field ("Mpox development stage") from the input collapsed accuracy from 90.4% to a fraction of the reported value, indicating the label was being carried by the text rather than the image [12]; the protocol should run an ablation arm with all clinical-context fields stripped to estimate the marginal contribution of the image.

## 5. Labeling task: granularity, label set, output format

The lock-down step. For the primary analysis, define one primary task and one secondary task. The recommended primary task is **binary malignancy** (benign vs. malignant or atypical), because it is the only label that can be tied to a histology gold standard for every image and the only one for which a non-inferiority margin has clinical meaning. The recommended secondary task is **multi-class diagnosis** on the ISIC 8-class taxonomy, with the understanding that a VLM matching dermatologists on top-3 is a different and weaker claim than matching on top-1. Tertiary tasks (7-point checklist, ABCDE features, triage priority) are optional and should be pre-registered to avoid p-hacking.

Output format must be structured JSON with explicit fields for the predicted label, top-3 alternatives, confidence, and a brief rationale. Free-text answers are not admissible for the primary endpoint; if a VLM fails to produce parseable structured output, the case is counted as incorrect for primary endpoints and is reported separately as a format-failure rate.

## 6. Metrics: agreement, accuracy, calibration, fairness

Four families, each with a defined primary statistic and a bootstrap 95% CI:

| Family | Primary metric | Secondary | Common pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agreement with panel | Gwet's AC1 (quadratic-weighted) | Cohen's κ, percent agreement, Krippendorff's α | Cohen's κ collapses under class imbalance; a documented case found 88% observed agreement but κ = 0.43 vs. AC1 = 0.85 on the same data [13] |
| Accuracy vs. panel consensus | Balanced accuracy; top-1 and top-3 sensitivity/specificity; macro-F1; AUROC | Raw accuracy, AUPRC | Raw accuracy on long-tailed ISIC classes is misleading; balanced accuracy and macro-F1 are mandatory |
| Calibration | Expected Calibration Error (ECE); Brier score | Reliability diagram; verbalized vs. token-probability confidence | VLM token-level probabilities are overconfident; temperature scaling is required before ECE is reported [14] |
| Fairness | Per-subgroup balanced accuracy, sensitivity gap, ECE gap, refusal rate | Demographic parity, equalized odds, certain-gap analysis | Reporting only aggregate accuracy masks subgroup harm |

Both Gwet's AC1 and Cohen's κ must be reported on imbalanced data, but AC1 is the primary statistic because it is robust to the "kappa paradox" seen in long-tailed medical classification [13]. Top-3 sensitivity should be reported alongside top-1: the gap between the two is a defining feature of current VLMs (e.g., GPT-5 reaches 46% top-1 sensitivity but 100% top-3 sensitivity on 100 ISIC images [15]) and a study that reports only top-1 systematically under-estimates clinical utility.

## 7. Statistical plan: power, non-inferiority margin, decision rule

Sample size is computed for the agreement-and-accuracy co-primary endpoints, taking the larger of the two. For Cohen's κ, the closed-form approximation gives n = ⌈(z_{α/2} + z_β)² · 2(1−κ₀)² / (κ₁−κ₀)²⌉; with κ₀ = 0.60, κ₁ = 0.75, two-sided α = 0.05, 85% power, and 40% positive-class prevalence, the requirement is ≈177 paired ratings per subgroup [16]. For estimating κ to within ±0.05 at expected κ = 0.70 and π = 0.5, ≈784 cases are required. The protocol should target the larger of the two and pre-specify subgroup minimums (≥50 per Fitzpatrick I–II, III–IV, V–VI), so the realized sample is typically in the 1,500–2,500 image range. Each VLM condition is run on the same fixed image set; the unit of analysis is the image, not the model run.

The non-inferiority margin for the binary task is set at Δ = 5 percentage points on balanced accuracy (and on top-3 sensitivity, given the asymmetry of harm from missed malignancy). The 5-point margin is consistent with the non-inferiority conventions used in diagnostic-accuracy trials, where margins of 5–10 percentage points are typical and must be defended clinically rather than chosen to favor the experimental arm [17].

The pre-specified go/no-go rule is conjunctive: substitution is supported only if (i) VLM vs. panel Gwet AC1 ≥ 0.80 and the lower 95% CI excludes 0.75, (ii) balanced accuracy is non-inferior to the dermatologist mean within 5 percentage points, (iii) ECE ≤ 0.10, and (iv) no pre-specified subgroup shows an F1 drop of more than 5 points versus the overall F1. Failure of any single condition returns the VLM to the *augmentation* category rather than *substitution*. The decision rule is locked before the first VLM is queried; post-hoc thresholds are not admissible.

## 8. Threats to validity and mitigations

Six classes of threat should be named in the protocol and pre-handled. (a) **Train–test contamination** of public datasets in proprietary VLM training corpora — addressed by tier (b) and tier (c) of the dataset design and by a per-model image-side near-neighbor overlap audit against the model's likely training distribution [8]. (b) **Single-source label noise** in ISIC and Fitzpatrick17k — addressed by the panel reference standard and by deduplication [6]. (c) **Refusal bias** in safety-tuned models — addressed by per-subgroup refusal reporting and a refusal-arm sensitivity analysis [11]. (d) **Prompt sensitivity and self-consistency** — addressed by the 3-prompt × 3-seed matrix per model and by reporting the within-condition variance. (e) **Label leakage from clinical context** — addressed by the field-stripped ablation arm [12]. (f) **Model version drift** — addressed by pinning the exact model ID and snapshot date and by re-running the prospective tier at the end of the study to detect silent updates.

## 9. Reporting and pre-registration

The study should be pre-registered on OSF or ClinicalTrials.gov before the first VLM query, with the locked label set, the locked decision rule, the locked prompt templates, and the locked panel adjudication procedure. Reporting follows TRIPOD-LLM [2] for the AI evaluation, DECIDE-AI [3] for the early-stage clinical framing, and TRIPOD+AI's fairness-stratified requirements [4]. The published Methods must include: the model versions queried, the prompt templates (verbatim), the temperature and seed settings, the exact panel composition, the adjudication procedure, the contamination-audit results, and the refusal and format-failure rates per subgroup. The single most useful sentence in the final report is the one that places the VLM's balanced accuracy and AC1 next to the panel's own inter-rater AC1 on the same images: substitution is only meaningful against the human ceiling, and the human ceiling is lower than most non-specialist readers assume.

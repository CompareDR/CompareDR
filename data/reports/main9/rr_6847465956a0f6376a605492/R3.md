# Can a Vision-Language Model Replace Dermatologists for Labeling Skin-Lesion Images? An Experimental Plan

## TL;DR

- **Not yet, on current evidence — and the answer forks by use case.** For coarse binary triage (refer/don't-refer) a VLM *might* reach a defensible non-inferiority bar in a research setting, but for fine-grained multi-class diagnosis, dermoscopic attribute labeling, and darker-skin subgroups, published 2023–2026 evidence shows general-purpose VLMs (GPT-4V/4o class) still fall well short of specialists; as of March 2026 no generative/LLM-based device has ever been FDA-authorized for marketing.
- **The experiment must be designed as two distinct studies:** (a) an *annotation-replacement* study judged by inter-rater agreement against a *measured* human-human ceiling, and (b) a *clinical-reader* study judged by a prespecified multi-reader multi-case (MRMC) non-inferiority design on sensitivity/AUC — with granularity (binary vs. fine-grained) and evidence bar (internal vs. regulatory-grade) set as explicit design forks.
- **The single biggest threat to validity is contamination:** ISIC, HAM10000 and other public sets are almost certainly in VLM pretraining data, so the plan hinges on a prospectively collected or private, never-web-published, histopathology-confirmed, skin-tone-stratified held-out test set, a frozen/version-pinned inference protocol, and prespecified subgroup fairness analyses.

## Key Findings

1. 1. **General-purpose VLMs are mediocre dermatology diagnosticians.** On PH2 dermoscopy, GPT-4 achieved 68.5% accuracy, 52.5% sensitivity, 72.5% specificity for melanoma — significantly below the clinical standard (P=.002). [nih][ref-01] GPT-4o struggles most with fine-grained multi-class distinctions (e.g., SCC vs. BCC vs. AK). By contrast, specialist deep-learning systems match or beat dermatologists on curated dermoscopy: Esteva et al. (Nature 2017, 542:115–118) trained a CNN on 129,450 images / 2,032 diseases and matched 21 board-certified dermatologists on biopsy-proven binary tasks.
2. 2. **Purpose-built dermatology foundation models are far stronger than general VLMs** — PanDerm (Yan et al., Nature Medicine, Aug 2025, 31:2691–2702), self-supervised on 2.1M images from 11 institutions across 4 modalities, reached state-of-the-art across 28 benchmarks and, in reader studies, "improved clinicians' skin cancer diagnostic accuracy by 11% on dermoscopy images and enhanced non-dermatologist healthcare providers' differential diagnosis by 16.5% across 128 skin conditions," plus outperformed clinicians by 10.2% in early-stage melanoma detection via longitudinal analysis. But these are not the off-the-shelf "modern VLM" a generic deployment would use, and even they are validated mostly on retrospective public data.
3. 3. **Fairness failure is documented and large.** GPT-4 gave the correct diagnosis for 44% (11/25) of light-skin vs. only 12% (3/25) of dark-skin images (Akuffo-Addo et al., *Clinical and Experimental Dermatology*, Oct 2024, 49(10):1244–1245; p<0.05; 50 Fitzpatrick17k images). The DDI benchmark (Daneshjou et al., *Science Advances* 2022, 8(32):eabq6147, n=656) showed ROC-AUC "dropping by 27–36 percent compared to the models' original test results," collapsing toward chance on Fitzpatrick V–VI (ModelDerm 0.55, DeepDerm 0.50, HAM10000 0.57) vs. Fitzpatrick I–II (0.64/0.61/0.72). Any plan without prespecified skin-tone subgroup analysis is invalid.
4. 4. **The human benchmark is not perfect and must be measured, not assumed.** Dermatologist–dermatologist agreement on pigmented-lesion/melanoma diagnosis is typically κ≈0.3–0.6; even expert dermatopathology sits near κ≈0.5. The annotation-replacement study must therefore establish a human-human agreement *ceiling* first, and judge the VLM against that realistic ceiling using prevalence-robust statistics (Gwet's AC1), not against a fictional perfect gold standard.
5. 5. **Regulatory reality: generative AI is unproven ground.** As of March 2026 the FDA's database lists 1,451 AI-enabled devices (up from 1,250 in 2025), but *none* is powered by generative AI or large language models (a March 2026 Breakthrough Device Designation to a genAI app notwithstanding — that is not marketing authorization). Under the EU AI Act, dermatology diagnostic software is high-risk (MDR Class IIa+), with high-risk obligations phasing in through August 2026–2027.

## Details

### A. State of the art (2023–2026)

**General-purpose VLMs (the "modern VLM" in question):**

- **GPT-4/GPT-4V/GPT-4o**: On PH2 dermoscopy, 68.5% accuracy / 52.5% sensitivity / 72.5% specificity for melanoma, significantly worse than clinical diagnosis (P=.002; Perlmutter et al. 2025). [Sage Journals][ref-02] On multi-class classification GPT-4o showed low sensitivity for BCC (0.081) and SCC (0.477) despite high specificity [JMIR Dermatology][ref-03] — i.e., it misses cancers. NEJM Image Challenge work (npj Digital Medicine 2024) found GPT-4V matched physicians on multiple-choice accuracy (81.6% vs 77.8%) [Nature][ref-04] but produced flawed rationales in 35.5% of *correct* answers, with image-comprehension errors dominating (27.2%) [PubMed Central][ref-05] — a warning that correct labels can rest on wrong reasoning.
- **Documented failure modes**: hallucination; heavy reliance on/deference to patient history (a sycophancy/anchoring risk when a user suggests a diagnosis); the ability to "change answers with guidance in multi-round interaction"; [arxiv][ref-06] positional/ordering bias in multiple-choice; performance variability/inconsistency across runs; and degraded performance on dermoscopy vs. clinical photos (Yang et al., "Can GPT-4V serve medical applications?" 2023; multiple prompt-sensitivity studies).
- **Med-Gemini / Gemini** (Google, 2024): Med-Gemini-2D surpassed baselines on 18/20 histopathology/ophthalmology/dermatology tasks and beat GPT-4V by an average 44.5% relative margin on multimodal benchmarks — but dermatology evaluation used PAD-UFES-20 (in the fine-tuning mix) and remained "approaching," not exceeding, task-specific models.
- **AMIE (multimodal, Nature Medicine 2026)**: In simulated text/image consultations grounded on the SCIN dermatology set, an 18-specialist panel rated AMIE at or above primary-care physicians on diagnostic accuracy, multimodal reasoning and empathy across 105 scenarios — but this is a *simulated dialogue* research setting, explicitly "not appropriate for real-world diagnostic use without considerable further research." [arxiv][ref-07]

**Purpose-built dermatology models (stronger, but different tools):**

- **PanDerm** (Nature Medicine 2025): see Key Finding 2.
- **MONET** (Nature Medicine 2024): image-text concept retriever trained on 105,550 image-caption pairs from literature; densely annotates dermoscopic/clinical concepts (color, morphology) verified by board-certified dermatologists [Stanford Health Care][ref-08] — directly relevant to the *attribute-labeling* branch and to auditing datasets for contamination/bias.
- Others: SkinGPT-4, LLaVA-Med, Med-Flamingo, Google's Derm Foundation model. Note LLaVA-Med collapses on dark skin (near-zero group performance on FST V–VI in one 2025 evaluation), [arxiv][ref-09] reinforcing the fairness concern for open VLMs.

**Bottom line for the fork:** For (b) clinical reading, off-the-shelf general VLMs are currently sub-specialist, especially fine-grained and on dark skin; dermatology-specific models are far better but not what a generic deployment uses. For (a) annotation, a VLM may be a useful *pre-labeler* or concept-annotator (MONET-style) but not an autonomous ground-truth source.

**Critical contamination problem:** ISIC, HAM10000, PH2 and web-sourced sets (SD-198, Fitzpatrick17k) are almost certainly in frontier-VLM pretraining corpora. Any headline accuracy on these sets is confounded by memorization. **Design implication:** the primary test set must be a **prospectively collected or private, never-web-published, histopathology-confirmed, skin-tone-stratified** hold-out. Contamination should be probed (e.g., prompt the model to reproduce known image IDs/captions; canary/membership checks; compare performance on public vs. matched private cases).

### C. Reader-study methodology (clinical-reader branch)

- **Foundational reader studies to anchor design:**
  
  - *Esteva et al. 2017 (Nature)*: CNN trained on 129,450 images / 2,032 diseases; tested vs. 21 board-certified dermatologists on **biopsy-proven** binary tasks (keratinocyte carcinoma vs. benign seborrheic keratosis; melanoma vs. nevus) [SciSpace][ref-18] — matched dermatologists.
  - *Haenssle et al. 2018 (Ann Oncol)*: 100-image test set, 58 dermatologists (30 experts), two levels (dermoscopy only vs. + clinical data). CNN AUC 0.86 vs. dermatologists' mean 0.79 (P<.01); CNN specificity 82.5% vs. 71.3% (level I). Establishes the **level-I/level-II metadata design** directly relevant to VLM comparability.
  - *Tschandl et al. 2019 (Lancet Oncol)* and *2020 (Nature Medicine)*: web-based international reader studies (up to 511 human readers vs. 139 algorithms), and human-computer collaboration showing AI support raised accuracy >10%, with least-experienced clinicians gaining most.
- **Analysis framework:** fully-crossed **MRMC** with **Obuchowski-Rockette / Dorfman-Berbaum-Metz (OR/DBM)** analysis (unified by Hillis); endpoint = **difference in ROC-AUC** (or partial AUC restricted to the clinically relevant high-sensitivity region), generalizing to both reader and case populations. [ResearchGate][ref-19] Software: MRMCaov , MRMCsamplesize (R).
- **Non-inferiority:** prespecify margin δ. A regulatory-grade precedent (Fujifilm DBT pivotal study) used **δ=0.05 AUC**, α=0.05, power=0.80, assumed AUC₁=0.80, requiring **60 cancer + 240 non-cancer cases and 18 readers**. [clinicaltrials][ref-20] This is a concrete, defensible starting template.
- **Reader panel:** stratify by expertise (board-certified dermatologist / resident / GP / nurse practitioner) — expertise materially changes the benchmark and the "who is being replaced" question. Randomize case order; use **washout periods** (typically ≥4 weeks, per crossover reader-study convention) between sequential/crossover reads to prevent recall.
- **Metadata fork:** decide whether the VLM and humans both get age/sex/site/history (Haenssle level-II) or images only (level-I). Comparability requires matched information; give both arms the same inputs.

### D. Agreement statistics (annotation-replacement branch)

- **Establish the human ceiling first.** Measure human-human agreement on the exact task. Published ceilings: clinical-photo pigmented-lesion classification inter-rater κ≈0.29; [PubMed Central][ref-21] dermoscopic algorithm reproducibility κ≈0.27–0.33 (7-point checklist, ABCD, pattern analysis, Menzies — Carli et al., *Br J Dermatol*); best-reproduced dermoscopic criteria κ≈0.52–0.62; invasive-vs-in-situ melanoma κ≈0.47 (*Dermatol Pract Concept* 2021); expert dermatopathology κ≈0.50 for malignant/borderline/benign melanocytic tumors. **The VLM should be judged non-inferior to this measured ceiling, not to perfection.**
- **Statistic selection:**
  
  - **Cohen's κ** (two raters) / **Fleiss' κ** (multiple raters) — standard but subject to the **kappa paradox** under high prevalence imbalance (high observed agreement, paradoxically low κ).
  - **Gwet's AC1** — preferred under prevalence imbalance; stays close to observed agreement and is insensitive to the paradox (documented in lung-cancer pathology pattern labeling: acinar pattern observed agreement 88%, Cohen's κ 0.43 vs. Gwet's AC1 0.85). Report with bootstrap 95% CIs (e.g., irrCAC , 1,000 iterations).
  - **Krippendorff's α** for missing data/multiple raters; **ICC** for continuous outputs (e.g., lesion diameter, probability scores); **Fleiss' κ** for multi-class attribute presence.
- **No-histopathology cases:** use label-noise-aware evaluation — treat consensus/follow-up labels as noisy references, report agreement against the *distribution* of expert labels, and where possible restrict primary endpoints to biopsy-confirmed cases with consensus/follow-up cases as secondary.

### E. Fairness and subgroup evaluation

- **Prespecify subgroups:** skin tone (primary), age, sex, body site, lesion size, image quality, common vs. rare disease.
- **Skin-tone measurement:** Fitzpatrick (FST) is standard but limited (designed for UV response, not tone; unreliable on dark skin). Complement with **individual typology angle (ITA)** from calibrated images, the **Monk Skin Tone (MST) scale**, and where possible eumelanin-based measures. Have ≥2 dermatologists assign FST by consensus (DDI approach).
- **Magnitude to expect:** GPT-4 44% (light) vs 12% (dark) correct; DDI ROC-AUC drop 27–36%, dark-skin AUC toward chance (0.50–0.57). Robust/fairness-aware training alone did *not* fix this in DDI — only fine-tuning on diverse data did.
- **Multiplicity:** control family-wise error across subgroups (Holm or Hochberg; Tschandl 2020 used Holm) or FDR; prespecify primary vs. exploratory subgroups.
- **Minimum per-subgroup n:** power each key subgroup (esp. FST V–VI) independently; a subgroup underpowered to detect a clinically meaningful gap (e.g., ≥5–10% sensitivity difference) cannot clear the fairness bar. Use DDI-style matched sampling to enable direct light-vs-dark comparison.

### F. Calibration, uncertainty and selective prediction

- **Calibration metrics:** Expected Calibration Error (ECE), Brier score, reliability diagrams. VLMs are known to be poorly calibrated and to verbalize unwarranted confidence.
- **Conformal prediction** gives distribution-free coverage guarantees (prediction sets containing the true label with, e.g., 90% probability) — but with documented **pitfalls in medicine**: guarantees break under distribution shift (needs recalibration), don't hold for subgroups without Mondrian/group-conditional variants, and add little value when there are few classes (common in binary derm tasks). [ResearchGate][ref-22] Prefer **selective prediction / reject option** (Geifman–El-Yaniv risk-coverage curves) [arxiv][ref-23] for the deferral decision.
- **Evaluate the hybrid workflow, not just full automation:** report **risk-coverage (coverage-accuracy) curves** where the VLM abstains on low-confidence cases and routes to a human. Cost-aware deferral (weighting false-negatives > false-positives > deferrals) reflects clinical reality. [Nature][ref-24] This reframes the question from "replace" to "how much can be safely automated at what coverage" — usually the more defensible deployment.

### G. Prompting and inference protocol as an experimental variable

- **Freeze the protocol before touching the test set.** Prompt sensitivity is a first-order threat to validity: zero-shot vs few-shot, chain-of-thought, self-consistency/majority voting over samples, temperature, structured-output constraints, and multi-VLM ensembling all move results. Prespecify and register the exact prompt, temperature, sampling, and parsing logic.
- **Report variance:** run multiple samples per image (VLMs are non-deterministic) and report run-to-run inconsistency as a metric, not a nuisance.
- **Metadata handling** (ties to §C fork): decide a priori whether history/age/sex/site are provided; providing them improves human and model performance (Haenssle level-II) but must be matched across arms, and note anchoring/sycophancy risk if history hints at a diagnosis.
- **Structured outputs:** constrain to a fixed label set / ontology (e.g., MONET/SKINCON concepts, 7-point checklist items) so outputs are directly comparable to human annotations and scoring is unambiguous.

### H. Regulatory, reporting and ethics framing

- **Reporting guidelines to follow from the start:**
  
  - **CLAIM 2024** (medical-imaging AI checklist), **STARD 2015** + **STARD-AI** (Nature Medicine 2025; 18 new/modified items [Nature][ref-25] for AI diagnostic-accuracy studies) for the reader study.
  - **TRIPOD+AI** (BMJ 2024, 27 items) and **TRIPOD-LLM** for model prediction/reporting; **PROBAST+AI** (BMJ 2025) and **QUADAS-2 / QUADAS-AI** for risk-of-bias.
  - **DECIDE-AI** (Nature Medicine 2022) for early-stage clinical evaluation; **SPIRIT-AI / CONSORT-AI** (BMJ 2020) if it advances to a trial protocol/RCT.
- **FDA:** dermatology diagnostic software is **Software as a Medical Device (SaMD)**, typically Class II. FDA's Dec 2024 final guidance introduced the **Predetermined Change Control Plan (PCCP)** for evolving models. [IntuitionLabs][ref-26] Crucially, **no generative-AI/LLM device has ever been FDA-authorized for marketing** (1,451 AI devices as of March 2026, ~76–84% radiology); [Productcreationstudio][ref-27] the Nov 2024 [Bipartisan Policy Cen…][ref-28] and Nov 2025 [fda][ref-29] Digital Health Advisory Committee meetings flagged hallucination and output variability as unresolved. A generative VLM claiming diagnostic labeling would be novel-pathway, high-scrutiny.
- **EU:** Under **MDR**, diagnostic AI is generally Class IIa+ (Rule 11), requiring a notified body. Under the **EU AI Act** (Reg. 2024/1689), any MDR device needing notified-body review is automatically **high-risk**; [IntuitionLabs][ref-30] most provisions apply from **August 2026**, with the high-risk classification for medical devices (Art. 6(1)) from **August 2027** (per MDCG 2025-6). Plan for both regimes.
- **IRB / data governance:** de-identify — dermatology images can contain faces, tattoos, and identifiable marks; strip EXIF/GPS metadata; obtain consent covering AI use and secondary sharing; document data provenance; ensure no PHI is transmitted to third-party VLM APIs (a real constraint when using commercial cloud endpoints).

### I. Practical costs and threats to validity

- **Costs:** frontier VLM inference is on the order of cents per image via API; dermatologist reader time is the dominant cost (specialist compensation × ~15–30 readers × hundreds of cases), plus prospective data collection and histopathology confirmation. Budget months, not weeks, for a regulatory-grade MRMC study.
- **Threats to validity (prespecify mitigations):**
  
  - **Contamination/leakage** (§B) — private prospective test set; contamination probes.
  - **Spectrum bias** — public sets over-represent classic/severe lesions; enrich with ambiguous, early, and real-world-distribution cases.
  - **Verification/work-up bias** — only suspicious lesions get biopsied, so histopathology ground truth exists preferentially for hard/positive cases (DDI is explicitly enriched for biopsied "ambiguous" lesions); account for this in prevalence and PPV/NPV interpretation.
  - **Non-stationarity of commercial endpoints** — models are silently updated mid-study. **Version-pin** (use dated/snapshot API versions), record model version and timestamp for every call, and re-run a calibration subset if the endpoint changes.
  - **Statistical non-inferiority ≠ clinical acceptability** — a VLM can be statistically non-inferior on AUC yet clinically unacceptable if it misses a subset of aggressive melanomas or fails on dark skin. Prespecify safety endpoints (e.g., melanoma sensitivity floor) alongside the primary non-inferiority endpoint.

## Recommendations

**Stage 0 — Decide the forks (before any data).** Write a Statistical Analysis Plan (SAP) fixing: (i) annotation vs. clinical-reading use case; (ii) binary/coarse vs. fine-grained/attribute/segmentation granularity; (iii) internal-research vs. regulatory-grade evidence bar. Register the protocol (OSF/ClinicalTrials.gov). Freeze the prompt/inference protocol and version-pin the VLM.

**Stage 1 — Contamination-controlled pilot (weeks).** Assemble a small private, histopathology-confirmed, skin-tone-balanced pilot set. Run contamination probes. Estimate VLM accuracy, calibration (ECE/Brier), run-to-run variance, and prompt sensitivity. **Go/no-go threshold:** if binary melanoma sensitivity is below the dermatologist floor (~85–90%) or dark-skin accuracy is <½ of light-skin accuracy (the DDI/GPT-4 danger zone), stop and do not proceed to expensive reader studies for the clinical branch.

**Stage 2a — Annotation-replacement study.** Measure the human-human agreement ceiling (≥3 dermatologists) first. Then evaluate VLM-vs-consensus using **Gwet's AC1** (primary), Fleiss' κ and Krippendorff's α (secondary), with bootstrap CIs, stratified by skin tone and disease rarity. **Acceptance:** VLM-human AC1 non-inferior (prespecified margin, e.g., 0.05) to human-human AC1 within every prespecified subgroup.

**Stage 2b — Clinical-reader MRMC study.** Fully-crossed MRMC, OR/DBM analysis, primary endpoint = difference in ROC-AUC (or high-sensitivity partial AUC) with **δ=0.05** non-inferiority margin; start from **~60 malignant + 240 benign cases, 15–18 readers** stratified by expertise, randomized case order, ≥4-week washout, matched metadata across arms. Co-primary safety endpoint: melanoma sensitivity floor. Report risk-coverage curves for a human-AI deferral workflow.

**Stage 3 — Fairness and regulatory packaging.** Prespecified subgroup analyses (FST/ITA/MST) with multiplicity control; per-subgroup power for FST V–VI. Report against CLAIM, STARD-AI, TRIPOD+AI, QUADAS-AI, DECIDE-AI. If pursuing market authorization, engage FDA (novel generative pathway; PCCP) and prepare for EU AI Act high-risk + MDR obligations.

**Benchmarks that change the recommendation:** proceed to deployment consideration only if the VLM is (a) statistically non-inferior on the primary endpoint, (b) meets the melanoma-sensitivity safety floor, (c) shows no clinically meaningful subgroup gap (e.g., <5% sensitivity difference across skin tones), and (d) is well-calibrated (ECE below a prespecified threshold) or paired with a validated deferral policy. Failure on fairness or safety overrides statistical non-inferiority.

## Caveats

- **Evidence is thin and contested for exactly the "replacement" claim.** Most positive general-VLM results are on contaminated public sets, small samples (PH2 n=200; the GPT-4 skin-tone study n=50, a research letter with industry ties), or simulated dialogues (AMIE) — none establish autonomous ground-truth labeling.
- **Practice differs sharply between the two branches.** Annotation-replacement is judged by agreement statistics against an imperfect human ceiling; clinical-reading is judged by MRMC non-inferiority against a true (histopathology) reference. Conflating them is a common design error.
- **Purpose-built derm models ≠ general VLMs.** PanDerm/MONET/Med-Gemini results should not be read as evidence that an off-the-shelf GPT-class model will perform similarly; it generally won't.
- **The "gold standard" is itself noisy.** Histopathology inter-rater κ≈0.5 means even biopsy labels carry uncertainty; frame all agreement targets relative to this.
- **Commercial-endpoint drift and prompt sensitivity can silently invalidate results** unless version-pinning and a frozen protocol are enforced.
- Some cited figures come from preprints (medRxiv/arXiv) or research letters not yet fully peer-reviewed; these are flagged in-text and should be treated as provisional.

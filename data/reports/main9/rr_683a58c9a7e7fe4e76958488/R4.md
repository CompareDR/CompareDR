# A Unified Engram–Predictive-Coding Network for Classification

## 1. Integration thesis: one network, one objective

The proposal is an embedding, not a juxtaposition. Predictive coding (PC) is a variational-inference scheme in which a cortical hierarchy minimizes a free-energy bound on sensory evidence by comparing top-down predictions against bottom-up, precision-weighted prediction errors ([Friston 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2666703/)). It supplies an inference engine—iterative, precision-weighted minimization of prediction error that reads a stored trace, completes a noisy cue, and computes confidence—but it lacks a fast, stable, sparse write mechanism: new episodes overwrite slowly interleaved weights, the catastrophic-interference problem that motivated Complementary Learning Systems (CLS) theory ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7624455/)).

Engram neuroscience supplies exactly that missing mechanism. An **engram** is the enduring physical and/or chemical change produced by learning; an **engram cell** is a neuron activated by a learning event, carrying persistent cellular changes, whose reactivation by part of the original stimulus suffices to recall the memory ([Tonegawa et al. 2015](https://cbmm.mit.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Week2_Tonegawa2015.pdf)). Engrams are sparse, distributed ensembles formed by competitive allocation and stabilized by synaptic tagging and capture ([Liu et al. 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028); [Guskjolen et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02137-5)). On their own, however, they store traces without a reading/completion engine and without a principled confidence signal.

The design therefore embeds an engram store inside a PC hierarchy and makes both halves minimize one quantity: the variational free energy of the episode. The single objective is

$$L = L_{\text{pred}} + \lambda L_{\text{consolidation}},$$

where $L_{\text{pred}} = \sum_l \pi^{(l)} \| x^{(l)} - g_l(x^{(l+1)}) \|^2$ is the precision-weighted prediction error (the variational free energy $F$ of the episode), and $L_{\text{consolidation}}$ is an anchor on already-consolidated slow weights plus a replay-reconstruction term. $\lambda$ is mode-dependent: $\lambda_{\text{on}} \approx 0$ during online perception and $\lambda_{\text{off}} \geq \lambda_{\text{on}}$ during offline consolidation. Inference minimizes $L_{\text{pred}}$ over activities; engram writing minimizes $L_{\text{pred}}$ over engram states; replay minimizes $L_{\text{consolidation}}$ plus replay prediction error. Every parameter and state receives its update from the same scalar $L$.

```
x^(1) ⇄ x^(2) ⇄ … ⇄ x^(L) ⇄ E  (engram layer, recurrent)
                                     │
                              class-posterior readout → ĉ
                                     │
                     fast E ──replay──► slow weights θ^cons
```

Operationally, "one network" means four things simultaneously: shared parameters (the same weights generate predictions and are the target of consolidation), shared dynamics (the same message-passing equations run online and offline), a shared objective (one $L$), and a shared substrate (the modules are compartments of a single hierarchy, not separate networks). The engram layer is a fast-learning recurrent associative store embedded near the top of the PC hierarchy; its states serve as high-level priors and initial conditions for inference. This is a functional identity rather than a compromise: a recurrent autoassociative store that is repeatedly reactivated becomes, under PC, a prior that biases lower levels and completes partial cues. The placement follows the hippocampal literature in which the hippocampus sits at the apex of a cortical hierarchy and reinstates cortical patterns during recall ([Barron et al. 2020](https://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/prediction-and-memory-predictive-coding-account)) and in which CA3 stores internal representations while CA1 compares prediction against input ([Chen et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38917804/)).

The integration is architectural, not a new component. CLS provides fast and slow systems with interleaved replay but no PC inference or engram-level allocation ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7624455/)); Spens and Burgess provide hippocampal replay training a generative neocortical model but no engram priors inside the hierarchy ([Spens & Burgess 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01799-z)); Barron et al. provide two processing modes via precision but no allocation/capture machinery ([Barron et al. 2020](https://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/prediction-and-memory-predictive-coding-account)); BayesPCN provides PC-based associative memory but no engram-level allocation/consolidation ([Yoo et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.09930v3)); PredRAE provides a hippocampal predictive circuit but no classifier ([Chen et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38917804/)). The integrated model combines all three: an allocated, tagged, fast-writing engram store inside a PC hierarchy, a single free-energy objective, and a class-posterior readout.

## 2. Biological foundations

### 2.1 Engrams: causal traces, competitive allocation, and tagging and capture

The contemporary definition is causal rather than correlational. Activity-dependent labeling (c-fos/TetTag) paired with optogenetic and chemogenetic manipulation yields gain-of-function (sufficiency) and loss-of-function (necessity) evidence ([Tonegawa et al. 2015](https://cbmm.mit.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Week2_Tonegawa2015.pdf); [Roy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5699085/)).

| Criterion | What it requires | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Activity dependence | Neuron activated by the learning event | c-fos/TetTag labeling ([Tonegawa et al. 2015](https://cbmm.mit.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Week2_Tonegawa2015.pdf)) |
| Enduring change | Persistent molecular/structural/functional change | CREB- and excitability-based allocation ([Guskjolen et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02137-5)) |
| Sufficiency | Artificial reactivation elicits the memory | Optogenetic reactivation of dentate-gyrus engram cells induces freezing ([Liu et al. 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028)) |
| Necessity | Silencing impairs recall | Loss-of-function in lateral amygdala; downstream connectivity inactivation blocks optogenetic recall ([Roy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5699085/)) |
| Natural-cue reactivation | Partial cue reactivates the engram | Pattern completion/ecphory; fails in silent engrams ([Tonegawa et al. 2015](https://cbmm.mit.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Week2_Tonegawa2015.pdf); [Roy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5699085/)) |

A key nuance is that **silent engrams** fail natural-cue reactivation yet retain optogenetic sufficiency: under protein-synthesis-inhibition-induced amnesia, engram cells lack augmented synaptic strength and show reduced spine density, yet memory information is retained and optogenetic recall correlates with persistent functional connectivity between engram ensembles ([Roy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5699085/)). Storage and retrievability are dissociable. The computational analogue is a latent variable that is stored but not expressed: a silent engram is a latent variable with a physical substrate but no behavioral readout until a cue or perturbation expresses it.

**Allocation.** Engram formation is competitive and inhibition-gated rather than a uniform potentiation of co-active neurons. Changing CREB function alters the probability that individual lateral-amygdala neurons are recruited into a fear-memory trace, and lowering CREB excludes neurons from allocation ([Han et al. 2007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17446403/)). Recruitment is relative: neurons more excitable immediately before training are more likely to enter the trace ([Park et al. 2024](https://www.bmbreports.org/journal/view.html?uid=1990&vmd=Full)). A rate-based recurrent network that includes both synaptic plasticity and neural excitability confirms that excitability biases allocation, that allocation is shaped by competitive inhibition, and that engrams for contexts presented close in time become structurally and functionally overlapping ([Delamare et al. 2024](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/17092)). The computational bridge is a **k-winners-take-all (k-WTA)** gate over an excitability variable: assign each candidate neuron an allocability/excitability score, let recurrent and lateral inhibition enforce competition, and admit only the top-$k$. This is a modeling abstraction of graded, inhibition-mediated competition, not a literal neural algorithm.

**Synaptic tagging and capture (STC).** STC provides the synapse-level counterpart of neuronal allocation. A weakly tetanized synapse can set a local tag that captures plasticity-related proteins (PRPs) synthesized in response to a strong tetanus elsewhere, converting early long-term potentiation (LTP) into late, protein-synthesis-dependent LTP ([Frey & Morris 1997](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9020359/)). The tagged state and the expression of LTP are dissociable, and functional and structural plasticity differ mechanistically ([Redondo & Morris 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21170072/)). Computationally, the tag is an **eligibility trace** that decays unless captured: a synapse is eligible if it was recently active, and it is consolidated only if a PRP/resource signal is available. The resulting tagged-slot write rule—*eligible + resource → stable weight*—is synapse-specific in its tag but population-level in its capture signal. It also explains co-allocation: synapses on the same postsynaptic neuron that are co-active can share PRPs, and a tag set by one event can be captured by resources released by a temporally proximal event ([Rogerson et al. 2014](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24496410/)).

**Pattern separation and completion.** In the hippocampus, sparsely active dentate gyrus (DG) granule cells orthogonalize similar cortical inputs (**pattern separation**), CA3 recurrent collaterals support autoassociation (**pattern completion**), and CA1 broadcasts the result back to cortex ([Goode et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7486247/)). Heterosynaptic plasticity at excitatory-to-inhibitory synapses lets inhibitory neurons associate with specific assemblies during encoding and selectively inhibit competing engrams during retrieval; this selective inhibition substantially improves recall stability and accuracy over global inhibition and reproduces DG-CA3-CA1 signatures ([Kim & Kim 2025](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013267)). For the model, this is the biological basis for a pattern-separation stage before the associative store: orthogonalizing similar class prototypes reduces interference and increases capacity.

**Consolidation, replay, and the index–content question.** The hippocampus–neocortex division solves a stability–plasticity problem: the hippocampus encodes individual episodes rapidly without overwriting structured knowledge, while the neocortex extracts statistical structure slowly; replay is the transfer mechanism that gradually renders memories hippocampus-independent by reinstating hippocampal activity in cortical circuits and strengthening cortico-cortical connections ([Frankland & Bontempi 2005](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn1607); [McClelland et al. 1995](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7624455/)). Replay is not verbatim playback: hippocampal sequences during sleep or rest can depict paths to future goals, random trajectories, shortcuts, and preplay sequences, motivating the view that replay is generative resampling from a learned model rather than readout of a buffer ([Foster 2017](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/10.1146/annurev-neuro-072116-031538); [Stoianov et al. 2022](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.01.16.908889v5.full-text)).

| Position | Core claim | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Index | Hippocampus stores an index to distributed neocortical content | [Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3008780/); [Goode et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7486247/) | Plausible for episodic recall |
| Content | Hippocampal synapses/connections store content | [Roy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5699085/); [Liu et al. 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028) | Supported for hippocampal-dependent memories |
| Hybrid | Engrams index cortical content while storing sparse content | [Zaki et al. 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-024-01979-z); [Tonegawa et al. 2015](https://cbmm.mit.edu/sites/default/files/documents/Week2_Tonegawa2015.pdf) | Plausible synthesis |

The hybrid is adopted as the default: the engram layer stores a sparse predictive model whose reactivation indexes distributed cortical-like representations. The exact proportion of content stored in hippocampal synapses versus cortical sites remains a free parameter.

### 2.2 Predictive coding: free-energy inference with local, biologically constrained updates

**Free-energy formulation.** In the free-energy formulation, the brain models the world as a hierarchy of dynamical systems that encode causal structure in the sensorium, and perception inverts that internal model to explain sensory data. Writing the generative model as $y = g(x,v,\theta) + z$ and $\dot{x} = f(x,v,\theta) + w$, with sensory input $y$, hidden states $x$, causes $v$, parameters $\theta$, and fluctuations $z,w$, Gaussian assumptions furnish the likelihood and empirical priors ([Friston 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2666703/)). **State units** encode hidden states and causes; **error units** encode the mismatch between predicted and actual input; **precision**—the inverse variance of the fluctuations—weights how strongly each prediction error influences belief updating. Variational free energy upper-bounds surprise, so minimizing it approximates the posterior over latent causes and maximizes model evidence.

**Local learning and the backpropagation limit.** A predictive-coding network (PCN) defines a hierarchical Gaussian generative model in which level $l$ predicts the level below, $x^{l-1} \approx f_l(x^l;\theta^l)$, and represents the mismatch in error units $\varepsilon^{l-1} = x^{l-1} - f_l(x^l)$. The weight update reduces to a local Hebbian rule,

$$\Delta\theta^l \propto \Pi_l\,\varepsilon^l\,(x^{l+1})^\top,$$

a precision-weighted product of the postsynaptic error and the presynaptic prediction, with no weight transport and no global gradient ([Millidge et al. 2022](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0774.pdf); [Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)). When activities equilibrate quickly relative to weight updates and precision is identity, the PC weight change converges to the backpropagation (BP) weight change ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)). This satisfies four biological-plausibility criteria—local computation, local plasticity, minimal external control, and plausible architecture—although the architecture criterion is only partially satisfied because one-to-one nudging and symmetric feedback are simplifications ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)). Weight transport is avoided either by feedback alignment or by dendritic error computation: a multilayer model with simplified dendritic compartments encodes error-driven synaptic plasticity through local dendritic prediction errors arising from the mismatch between top-down feedback and lateral inhibition from somatostatin-expressing (SST+) interneurons ([Sacramento et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11393v1)).

**Microcircuit constraints.** The neuroanatomical plausibility comes from a correspondence between PC's computational dependencies and the physiology of the cortical column. Superficial pyramidal cells (layers 2/3) encode prediction errors, while deep pyramidal cells (layers 5/6) encode predictions/state; feedforward connections originate predominantly from superficial layers and feedback connections from deep layers ([Bastos et al. 2012](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Canonical%20Microcircuits%20for%20Predictive%20Coding.pdf)). Dendritic predictive coding shifts error computation out of separate populations and into dendritic compartments: predictions ascend to basal dendrites, where lateral inhibition computes bottom-up prediction errors, while predictions descend to apical dendrites, where they predict somatic spiking and guide inference ([Mikulasch et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.05303)). Experimental evidence for classical separate error units is inconclusive, so the dendritic account is a proposal rather than an established mechanism.

**PCN as associative memory.** A PCN stores training patterns as attractors of its energy function and retrieves them from partial or noisy cues by inference—pattern completion is energy minimization. Hierarchical generative PCNs outperform backprop-trained autoencoders and modern Hopfield networks in retrieval accuracy and robustness, and complete partially presented natural images from a tiny fraction of pixels ([Salvatori et al. 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/1fb36c4ccf88f7e67ead155496f02338-Abstract.html)). Classical recurrent models such as Hopfield networks learn the covariance of inputs through recurrent connections; earlier PCNs that learn covariance explicitly do perform associative memory, but in an implausible and numerically unstable way. Implicit-covariance alternatives are analytically equivalent, numerically stable, and can use dendritic structures to encode prediction errors ([Tang et al. 2023](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010719)). Modern Hopfield networks store exponentially many patterns, retrieve a pattern in one update, and have an update rule equivalent to attention ([Ramsauer et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217)).

**Stability and scaling.** The canonical PCN is shallow and fragile. Depth-μP reparameterization stabilizes up to 128-layer residual networks by addressing ill-conditioning of the inference landscape and vanishing/exploding forward activations ([Innocenti et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13124v2)). Performance degrades beyond five to seven layers because of exponentially imbalanced inter-layer errors; dynamical precision weighting plus a new weight-update mechanism brings deep PCNs to BP-comparable accuracy on VGG-15 and ResNet-18 ([Qi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23800v3)). BayesPCN performs continual one-shot writes without meta-learning, gradually forgets to free capacity, and recalls corrupted high-dimensional data observed hundreds to a thousand timesteps earlier ([Yoo et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.09930v3)).

**Two processing modes.** During online predictive processing, descending predictions suppress neocortical prediction errors to explain away ascending input via cortical inhibition. During episodic recall, the hippocampus instead exerts an excitatory influence that reinstates cortical activity patterns and increases the gain (precision) ascribed to prediction-error units. Memory recall is then cast as arising from **fictive prediction errors**—internal signals, generated in the absence of sensory data, that furnish training signals to optimize generative models of the world ([Barron et al. 2020](https://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/prediction-and-memory-predictive-coding-account)). Precision is the mode-switching variable.

## 3. The unified network

### 3.1 Architecture and module mapping

The hierarchy is a stack of PC layers $x^{(1)} \dots x^{(L)}$ with paired state and error units; top-down predictions descend and precision-weighted errors ascend. The engram layer $E$ sits at the top with recurrent collaterals and bidirectional links to $x^{(L)}$; a readout maps class-engram posteriors to decisions; a consolidation pathway copies fast engram weights into a slow store via a replay buffer.

| Module | Computational role | Biological substrate | Plausibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input/sensory layer $x^{(1)}$ | Encodes stimulus; lowest prediction error; precision = sensory reliability | Visual/association cortex; thalamic relay | Established |
| PC hierarchy (states, errors, precision) | Iterative inference; top-down prediction; bottom-up precision-weighted error; pattern completion | Cortical microcircuit; dendritic error computation | Established (formal); plausible (neural) |
| Engram/associative layer $E$ | Fast sparse write; recurrent completion; high-level prior/initial condition | DG–CA3–CA1; CREB allocation; STC | Plausible |
| Consolidation pathway (fast→slow, replay) | Interleaved replay writes tagged changes into slow weights; protects priors | Systems consolidation; sharp-wave-ripple replay | Plausible |
| Classification readout | Posterior over class-engram states; Bayesian model selection; argmax decision | Cortical categorization circuit | Plausible |

The computational mapping is conjectural at two points: the identity of the engram layer with a single hippocampal field, and the readout's correspondence to a specific categorization circuit. Both are design choices constrained by the evidence reviewed above.

### 3.2 Engram write dynamics

Let the $N$ engram neurons receive feedforward drive $u_i$ from the PC hierarchy and maintain an intrinsic excitability variable $a_i$ (CREB-like). The allocation score and gate are

$$s_i = u_i + \lambda_a a_i, \qquad g_i = \mathbb{1}[s_i > \theta_k], \quad \sum_i g_i = k,$$

where $\theta_k$ is the k-WTA threshold that keeps exactly $k$ neurons active. The excitability variable follows jump-and-decay dynamics that mimic CREB-governed intrinsic plasticity:

$$\frac{d a_i}{dt} = -\frac{a_i}{\tau_a} + \eta_a r_i,$$

with $r_i$ the engram neuron's firing rate and $\tau_a$ the decay constant. More excitable neurons are preferentially recruited, and competitive inhibition keeps the ensemble sparse.

Each engram synapse carries an eligibility trace $e_{ij}$ (the synaptic tag):

$$\frac{d e_{ij}}{dt} = -\frac{e_{ij}}{\tau_e} + r_j^{\text{pre}}\, r_i\, g_i,$$

where $r_j^{\text{pre}}$ is the presynaptic drive and $r_i$ the postsynaptic engram firing rate. The trace is a transient, local mark: it does not by itself change the weight, but it makes the synapse eligible for capture.

A capture signal $c$ (PRP availability, a neuromodulatory consolidation event, or a replay event) converts the tagged, eligible trace into a weight change. Fast and slow weights are updated by

$$w_{ij}^{\text{fast}} \leftarrow w_{ij}^{\text{fast}} + \alpha_f\, e_{ij}\, g_i,$$
$$w_{ij}^{\text{slow}} \leftarrow w_{ij}^{\text{slow}} + \alpha_s\, c\, e_{ij}\, g_i - \mu\, w_{ij}^{\text{slow}},$$

where $\alpha_f$ and $\alpha_s$ are fast and slow learning rates and $\mu$ is a slow-weight decay. Only synapses that are both tagged ($e_{ij}>0$) and allocated ($g_i=1$) are consolidated, and only when the capture signal is present. The slow learning rate is modulated by the history of capture at that synapse (metaplasticity, the activity-dependent regulation of plasticity thresholds):

$$\alpha_s^{(n)} = \alpha_s^{(0)}\, h(m_{ij}), \qquad m_{ij} \leftarrow m_{ij} + c\, e_{ij}\, g_i,$$

where $m_{ij}$ counts prior capture events and $h$ is a monotone function (e.g., a decreasing-plasticity sliding threshold).

Engram episodes are stored in a replay buffer $\mathcal{B}$. During offline consolidation, a minibatch $b \sim \mathcal{B}$ drives the slow weights:

$$w_{ij}^{\text{slow}} \leftarrow w_{ij}^{\text{slow}} + \alpha_s\, \mathbb{E}_{b \sim \mathcal{B}}\left[ c_b\, e_{ij,b}\, g_{i,b} \right].$$

Replay is interleaved across episodes so that new consolidation does not overwrite old structure. Catastrophic forgetting is further limited by protecting previously captured slow weights, in the form of an importance-weighted quadratic penalty (the machine-learning analogue of elastic weight consolidation, EWC):

$$L_{\text{consolidation}} = \sum_{ij} \lambda\, F_{ij}\, \left( w_{ij}^{\text{slow}} - w_{ij}^{\text{slow},*} \right)^2,$$

where $F_{ij}$ is an importance measure and $w_{ij}^{\text{slow},*}$ is the protected value ([Kirkpatrick et al. 2017](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1611835114)). Consolidation is not a one-time transfer: in a spiking model, memories transition from unselective to selective as neurons drop out of and drop into the engram, and inhibitory synaptic plasticity during consolidation is critical for selectivity ([Tomé et al. 2024](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/dynamic-and-selective-engrams-emerge-with-memory-consolidation/)). The allocation gate and slow weights must therefore remain plastic during consolidation.

**Class-identity acquisition.** The model uses unsupervised prototype formation with labels entering only as a readout mapping. Competitive Hebbian clustering over inputs forms engram prototypes; a readout maps prototype activity to class posteriors. A one-hot teacher supervises only the readout in experiments; it is not a claimed neural mechanism.

### 3.3 Inference with engram priors

Levels $i=0,\dots,L$: $x^0$ is the clamped sensory input; $x^i$ ($i\ge 1$) are state units; $g^i(x^i)$ is the top-down generative mapping predicting $x^{i-1}$; $\varepsilon^i = x^{i-1} - g^i(x^i)$ is the bottom-up prediction error; and $\Pi^i$ is the precision weighting that error. The free energy that inference minimizes is

$$F=\sum_{i=1}^{L}\tfrac{1}{2}\,\varepsilon^{i\top}\Pi^{i}\varepsilon^{i}\;+\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,(x^{L}-W_{eL}e)^{\top}\Sigma_{e}^{-1}(x^{L}-W_{eL}e),$$

where $W_{eL}$ projects the engram state onto the top PC state and $\Sigma_e^{-1}$ is the precision of that engram prior. The first term is the summed precision-weighted prediction error; the second ties the top state to the stored trace.

The engram layer is a recurrent associative memory with state $e$ and stored prototypes $\{\xi_c\}$. In inference, $e$ plays three mathematical roles:

- **Initial condition.** At inference onset the cue-driven engram state seeds the top state, $x^L(0)=W_{eL}e(0)$, so inference starts already biased toward the stored pattern.
- **Prior mean.** The term $\Sigma_e^{-1}(x^L-W_{eL}e)$ pulls the top state toward the engram projection throughout settling; $\Sigma_e^{-1}$ controls how strongly the engram prior dominates over bottom-up evidence.
- **Recurrent state.** $e$ evolves by pattern completion, $e\leftarrow\phi(Me)$ with the Hebbian memory matrix $M=\sum_c \xi_c\xi_c^\top$, so the prior sharpens onto a single prototype as inference proceeds.

Gradient descent on $F$ gives the state dynamics

$$\frac{dx^i}{dt}=\Big(\frac{\partial g^i}{\partial x^i}\Big)^{\!\top}\Pi^{i}\varepsilon^{i}-\Pi^{i+1}\varepsilon^{i+1},\quad i=1,\dots,L-1,$$

$$\frac{dx^L}{dt}=\Big(\frac{\partial g^L}{\partial x^L}\Big)^{\!\top}\Pi^{L}\varepsilon^{L}-\Sigma_{e}^{-1}(x^{L}-W_{eL}e).$$

The top-down term propagates the local precision-weighted error into the state that predicts it; the bottom-up term passes the higher-level error down. In the dendritic realization, these two signals are computed locally in apical and basal dendrites of the same pyramidal cell ([Mikulasch et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.05303)).

For each input the network performs a forward inference pass: clamp $x^0$ to the full input (classification), a partial/noisy cue (pattern completion), or leave it free (generation); initialize $x^i(0)$ neutrally and $x^L(0)=W_{eL}e(0)$; iterate the state and error updates until convergence; then read out the reconstructed input, settled top state, label state, and free energy. At equilibrium the errors are minimized and the top-down predictions from the engram prior have reconstructed the pattern that best explains the input.

Because the same settling process serves all three functions, the network is simultaneously a classifier, a generator, and an associative memory. PCNs can function as classifiers, generators, and associative memories simultaneously and can be defined on arbitrary graph topologies ([Millidge et al. 2022](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0774.pdf)). A PC network is both discriminative and generative: clamping the input and running to equilibrium yields the classification, while clamping the output class and running to equilibrium yields a generated input ([Sun & Orchard 2020](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jorchard/academic/SunOrchard_NECO2020.pdf)). Top-down engram predictions and bottom-up sensory errors must be optimized jointly in one landscape, not switched between as separate modes ([Oliviers et al.](https://2024.ccneuro.org/pdf/414_Paper_authored_BidirectionalPC_CCN.pdf)).

### 3.4 Classification readout and decision rule

The integrated network classifies by Bayesian model selection over class-engram states. Each class engram is treated as a generative hypothesis: when its pattern is used as a top-down prior, the hierarchy predicts the input, and the hypothesis with the lowest variational free energy wins. Let $\epsilon_l^{(c)}$ be the prediction error at layer $l$ under the class-$c$ hypothesis. The variational free energy for class $c$ is

$$F_c = \underbrace{\sum_{l} \epsilon_l^{(c)T}\,\Pi_l\,\epsilon_l^{(c)}}_{\text{accuracy}} \;+\; \underbrace{D_{KL}\!\left[q(\theta)\,\|\,p(\theta \mid c)\right]}_{\text{complexity}}.$$

The class posterior and decision rule are

$$p(c \mid x) = \frac{\exp(-F_c)}{\sum_{c'=1}^{C}\exp(-F_{c'})}, \qquad \hat{c} = \arg\max_c p(c \mid x) = \arg\min_c F_c.$$

Minimizing variational free energy over class-engram states therefore yields a classification decision: the class whose engram, used as a prior, explains the input with the lowest free energy. The softmax over negative free energies is the posterior over latent class variables; the argmax is the decision rule.

Precision controls confidence: because $\Pi_l$ multiplies the squared errors in $F_c$, higher precision on a class's prediction errors sharpens its free-energy landscape and its posterior. Confidence and uncertainty are read out as $\mathrm{conf}(x) = p(\hat{c} \mid x)$ and $U(x) = -\sum_c p(c \mid x)\ln p(c \mid x)$, and an input is flagged as ambiguous or out-of-distribution (OOD) when no class hypothesis reduces the residual error:

$$\mathrm{OOD}(x) = \mathbf{1}\left[\min_c F_c > \tau_F \;\lor\; U(x) > \tau_U\right].$$

This generative route is the core: it exposes uncertainty, supports OOD rejection, and treats each class as a model. A discriminative readout (a label node or learned readout mapping) can be added for benchmark convenience, but it is a convenience layer, not the core mechanism. Hierarchical PC recognizers tolerate position, illumination, size, partial occlusion, and within-category variation, supporting robustness under input degradation ([Spratling 2017](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/a-hierarchical-predictive-coding-model-of-object-recognition-in-n/)).

### 3.5 Local synaptic learning rules

The engram layer needs no learning principle beyond predictive coding; it needs a third factor. The cortical hierarchy is trained by the canonical, local, precision-weighted prediction-error rules, and the engram layer is trained by the same rules gated by an allocation gate and an eligibility trace, plus a replay/consolidation signal that captures tagged changes into slow weights. The canonical base is

$$\Delta W_{ij}^l = \eta\,\Pi_i^l\,\varepsilon_i^l\,x_j^{l-1}. \tag{E1}$$

The engram-modulated additions are:

$$\Delta V_{ij}^{f} = \alpha\,\gamma_i\gamma_j\left(y_i y_j - V_{ij}^{f}\right). \tag{E2}$$

$$\tau_{ij} \leftarrow \rho\,\tau_{ij} + \gamma_i\gamma_j\,y_i y_j,\qquad \rho=e^{-\Delta t/T_{\text{tag}}}. \tag{E3}$$

$$\Delta V_{ij}^{s} = \eta_c\,\tau_{ij}\,\gamma_j\,M(t). \tag{E4}$$

$$\Delta W_{ij}^{E} = \eta_E\,\Pi_i^E\,\varepsilon_i^E\,x_j^{E-1}\,\gamma_j. \tag{E5}$$

$$\Delta W_{ij}^{neo} = \eta_t\,\varepsilon_i^{neo}\,y_j^E\,M(t). \tag{E6}$$

Equation (E2) is a gated Hebbian trace with a homeostatic, anti-Hebbian decay term that stabilizes the attractor and supports pattern separation. Equation (E3) is the eligibility trace / synaptic tag. Equation (E4) is the three-factor capture: the pre–post coincidence carried by $\tau_{ij}$, postsynaptic allocation $\gamma_j$, and the phasic replay/neuromodulatory gate $M(t)$. Equation (E5) restricts plasticity at the engram interface to allocated cells. Equation (E6) is replay-driven teacher–student consolidation: during replay the engram (teacher) drives the neocortical PC (student), which reproduces stored patterns; this is the distillation mechanism by which hippocampal replay trains a neocortical generative model ([Spens & Burgess 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01799-z)). Precision acts as a local gain signal on error-driven plasticity: $\eta \rightarrow \eta\,\Pi_i^l$ in (E1)/(E5) and $\eta_c \rightarrow \eta_c\,M(t)$ in (E4)–(E6).

| Rule | Equation | Biological mechanism | Special-case reduction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Precision-weighted error Hebbian | (E1) | Pyramidal error units; precision from neuromodulators | Hebbian (linear $f$) |
| Allocation-gated engram trace | (E2) | CREB/excitability allocation; LTP + homeostatic decay | Oja (with normalization) |
| Eligibility trace / tag | (E3) | Synaptic tag; CaMKII / STC | Spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) window |
| Three-factor capture | (E4) | PRP capture; phasic neuromodulator / replay | Reward-modulated STDP; BCM-like |
| Allocation-gated PC at engram | (E5) | Error units gated by interneurons/allocation | Hebbian |
| Replay teacher–student | (E6) | Sharp-wave-ripple replay; hippocampal→neocortical transfer | Distillation |

The three-factor framework—presynaptic–postsynaptic coincidence × postsynaptic allocation/excitability × a phasic neuromodulatory or replay signal—is experimentally grounded in neo-Hebbian learning rules ([Gerstner et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6079224/); [Frémaux & Gerstner 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2015.00085/full)). The third factor is implemented biologically by phasic dopamine, acetylcholine, or noradrenaline signalling novelty, reward, or surprise. The model requires no exact symmetric feedback weights: predictive coding converges asymptotically, and in practice rapidly, to exact BP gradients using only local, mostly Hebbian rules, so the feedback pathway carries predictions, not gradients ([Millidge et al. 2022](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0774.pdf)). Where a local dendritic account is preferred, the apical dendrite encodes the mismatch between top-down feedback and lateral inhibition, leaving only the backpropagated error as the apical signal ([Sacramento et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11393v1)).

The covariance-learning constraint is a hard design rule: any engram-modulated covariance learning must use implicit, local, numerically stable rules—recurrent connections that learn covariance implicitly, or dendritic error encoding—rather than explicit, non-local covariance rules that are implausible and unstable ([Tang et al. 2023](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010719)). Equation (E2) satisfies this constraint.

### 3.6 Operating modes and mode scheduling

The network alternates between online perceptual inference and offline/engram recall. In perception, bottom-up input drives the state units, top-down predictions from the winning engram suppress prediction errors, and the class posterior is read out. In recall, sensory input is gated off and the engram layer is reactivated by internal cues or replay; the resulting fictive prediction errors furnish training signals that optimize the generative model in the absence of sensory data ([Barron et al. 2020](https://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/prediction-and-memory-predictive-coding-account)).

| Mode | Driver | Sensory precision | Engram precision | Error signal | Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Perceptual inference | Bottom-up input | High (neuromodulator-gated) | Low–moderate | Sensory prediction errors | Classification; free-energy model selection |
| Engram recall / replay | Internal cue (context, ripple) | Low (gated toward zero) | High | Fictive prediction errors | Generative replay; consolidation; generalization |

The mode-scheduling rule is

$$m_t = \begin{cases} \mathrm{recall} & \text{if } r_t = 1 \text{ and } \Pi_{\mathrm{sen}} < \kappa,\\ \mathrm{perception} & \text{otherwise}, \end{cases}$$

where $r_t \in \{0,1\}$ is a replay cue (a contextual/hippocampal cue or a ripple event), $\Pi_{\mathrm{sen}}$ the precision on sensory prediction errors, and $\kappa$ a threshold. During recall, $\Pi_{\mathrm{sen}} \to 0$, the cue reactivates the engram state, and fictive errors update the slow weights; during perception, $\Pi_{\mathrm{sen}}$ is set by neuromodulatory gain and the input drives inference.

Recall-mode reactivation improves subsequent classification. Generative replay—akin to imagining new category instances—facilitates generalization to new experiences, and category replay is most beneficial for newly acquired knowledge ([Barry & Love 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9758580/)). A hippocampus-inspired memory-indexing module that encodes training samples into compressed memory engrams and uses them as cues to generate conditioned replay samples improves the balance and quality of generative replay and is robust to task order and replay amount ([Maekawa et al. 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.eacl-main.65.pdf)). If the precision/gain schedule fails—for example, if recall mode is engaged during perception—internally generated engram activity can be misread as sensory evidence, producing confident classifications of a class that was not present. This is a falsifiable prediction: such errors should correlate with recall-mode markers and be abolished by restoring sensory-precision gating.

## 4. Anatomical and physiological grounding

The model's components map onto two established anatomical substrates. The cortical hierarchy maps onto the canonical microcircuit; the engram layer maps onto the hippocampal DG–CA3–CA1 system; precision is implemented as neuromodulator-regulated postsynaptic gain.

| Model component | Anatomical substrate | Cell type / oscillation / neuromodulator | Plausibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input and feedforward hierarchy | Visual/association cortical hierarchies; thalamic relay | Superficial (L2/3) pyramidal cells | Established |
| Top-down predictions | Deep (L5/6) pyramidal cells | Deep pyramidal cells; alpha/beta feedback | Established |
| Prediction-error units | Apical dendrites of pyramidal neurons | Apical dendritic compartments | Plausible |
| Prediction-cancellation interneurons | SST+ interneurons | SST+ smooth inhibitory cells; lateral inhibition | Plausible |
| Precision weighting | Postsynaptic gain of superficial error units | Acetylcholine; monoamines | Plausible |
| Unsigned prediction error / reliability | VTA/SNc dopamine projections | Dopamine | Plausible |
| Engram / associative memory | Hippocampal DG–CA3 ensembles | DG granule cells; CA3 recurrent collaterals | Established (anatomy); plausible (computational role) |
| Pattern separation | DG granule cells / mossy fibers | Sparse DG code | Established |
| Pattern completion / attractor | CA3 recurrent collaterals | CA3 attractor network | Plausible |
| Comparator / output | CA1 | CA1 pyramidal cells; Schaffer collaterals | Plausible |
| Consolidation / replay | Sharp-wave ripples (SPW-R) | CA3→CA1 apical dendritic input; perisomatic inhibition | Established |
| Encoding/retrieval scheduling | Theta (4–8 Hz) | Theta oscillation | Plausible (human scalp evidence) |
| Engram allocation | CREB-mediated excitability | CREB transcription factor | Established |

Sources: [Bastos et al. 2012](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Canonical%20Microcircuits%20for%20Predictive%20Coding.pdf); [Mikulasch et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2205.05303); [Sacramento et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11393v1); [Cherubini et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncel.2015.00019/full); [Kim et al. 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2013.00044/full); [Kocagoncu et al. 2021](https://www.ovid.com/journals/brain/fulltext/10.1093/brain/awab254~evidence-and-implications-of-abnormal-predictive-coding-in); [Gilbert et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2022.787495/full); [Diederen et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7804370/); [Liu et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-33536-x); [Hsieh and Ranganath 2014](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4sg053v3/qt4sg053v3_noSplash_e04e55caf0e8cbd0a5ff70ea53cf2598.pdf); [Xie et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.614950/full).

Connectomic constraints follow from these mappings: basal dendrites receive bottom-up input and apical dendrites receive top-down input, so feedforward and feedback synapses should remain segregated by compartment ([Sacramento et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11393v1)); superficial pyramidal cells are the feedforward/error cells and deep pyramidal cells are the feedback/prediction cells ([Bastos et al. 2012](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Canonical%20Microcircuits%20for%20Predictive%20Coding.pdf)); SST+ interneurons provide lateral cancellation of top-down input ([Sacramento et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/html/1810.11393v1)); thalamic input targets superficial and inhibitory populations ([Bastos et al. 2012](https://www.fil.ion.ucl.ac.uk/~karl/Canonical%20Microcircuits%20for%20Predictive%20Coding.pdf)); CA3 recurrent collaterals are spatially extensive and sparse, constraining the engram layer's recurrent connectivity ([Cherubini et al. 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncel.2015.00019/full)).

Oscillations organize replay and consolidation. SPW-Rs are the canonical signature of hippocampal replay during sleep and quiet wakefulness and support memory consolidation and retrieval ([Liu et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-33536-x)). Theta oscillations are a candidate scheduling signal for encoding and retrieval, though the strongest theta evidence reviewed here is human scalp/EEG rather than direct hippocampal recording ([Hsieh and Ranganath 2014](https://escholarship.org/content/qt4sg053v3/qt4sg053v3_noSplash_e04e55caf0e8cbd0a5ff70ea53cf2598.pdf); [Xie et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/human-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnhum.2021.614950/full)). Neuromodulators implement precision and learning-rate signals: acetylcholine and norepinephrine have been proposed to signal expected and unexpected uncertainty respectively, and dopamine conveys reward prediction error ([Yu & Dayan 2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15944135/); full text not verified). Phasic neuromodulation gates STDP induction and can depress GABA release to promote LTP ([Frémaux & Gerstner 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2015.00085/full); [Bocchio et al. 2017](https://dandrite.au.dk/uploads/media/Bocchio_et_al_Neuron_2017_01.pdf)). Metaplasticity acts on slower timescales than Hebbian plasticity and regulates synaptic update rules based on past activity ([Yger & Gilson 2015](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2015.00138/full)). Intrinsic plasticity supports temporal memory linking ([Peregrim et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11391492/)), and systems reconsolidation shows that recalling remote memories re-engages the hippocampus and reopens plasticity ([Lei et al. 2025](https://www.cell.com/neuron/fulltext/S0896-6273(24)00835-3); metadata verified only).

Several mappings are analogies rather than homologies: the dendritic error-unit mapping is a model, not an established fact; the precision-to-neuromodulator identity is plausible but not one-to-one; mapping the engram layer onto CA3 as a content store versus an index is a design decision; and the fast/slow hippocampus–neocortex distinction is a design template from CLS theory, not a verified homology.

## 5. Spiking realization

The rate-based model can be realized as a spiking neural network without abandoning its defining mechanisms, but the realization is currently component-wise feasible rather than demonstrated end-to-end. Verified precedents cover each required piece.

| Precedent | Mechanism | Demonstrated result | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Lee et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2024.1338280/full) | Explicit positive/negative error neurons | MNIST reconstruction of unseen samples (unsupervised) | Verified |
| [N'dri et al., PCL](https://tub-rip.github.io/eventvision2023/papers/2023CVPRW_Predictive_Coding_Light.pdf) | Inhibitory STDP suppresses predictable spikes | Unsupervised representation learning; surround suppression | Verified |
| [Koren et al. 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/820c61a0cd419163ccbd2c33b268816e-Abstract-Conference.html) | Generalized leaky integrate-and-fire with excitatory/inhibitory populations and homeostatic currents | Efficient coding; low-rank E–E connectivity shapes responses | Verified |
| [Zhang et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013112) | Multi-compartment spiking with energy objective | Emergent predictive-coding-like responses; reconstruction from top-down signals | Verified |
| [Mazurek et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05341) | Three-factor STDP review | Credit assignment; scalability challenges | Verified |
| [Tomé et al. 2024](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/dynamic-and-selective-engrams-emerge-with-memory-consolidation/) | Inhibitory plasticity in spiking network + mouse dentate gyrus | Engrams become selective during consolidation | Verified |

The binding constraint is a trade-off: spiking predictive-coding models gain energy efficiency, temporal coding, and biological realism but lose accuracy and stability relative to rate-based networks. Binary spikes cannot directly signal a negative prediction error, so signed-error wiring remains a hardware difficulty ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013112)). No verified spiking PCN in this set matches rate-based PCN or backpropagation accuracy on a classification task; the integrated model should treat spiking accuracy as an open empirical question.

A concrete blueprint follows from the precedents: use leaky integrate-and-fire neurons with excitatory/inhibitory populations and explicit positive/negative error neurons ([Koren et al. 2022](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/820c61a0cd419163ccbd2c33b268816e-Abstract-Conference.html); [Lee et al. 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2024.1338280/full)); use inhibitory STDP for redundancy suppression ([N'dri et al.](https://tub-rip.github.io/eventvision2023/papers/2023CVPRW_Predictive_Coding_Light.pdf)); use three-factor STDP with the allocation signal as the third factor ([Mazurek et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05341)); use inhibitory plasticity to reshape engram membership during consolidation ([Tomé et al. 2024](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/dynamic-and-selective-engrams-emerge-with-memory-consolidation/)); and use an energy objective plus task objective to obtain predictive-coding-like responses ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013112)). The classification readout is not demonstrated in any verified spiking PCN and would need to be added and tested.

## 6. Evaluation protocol and falsifiable predictions

The integration is falsifiable only on tasks where a fast, sparse, cue-driven write and a slow, precision-weighted consolidation read-out are both necessary. The minimal suite is static MNIST/Fashion-MNIST classification with corrupted and few-shot probes; a five-task split-MNIST continual-learning sequence in which co-allocation/memory linking is manipulated; and an OOD split scored by prediction-error magnitude. Baselines must be matched per task: standalone PCNs span a wide range depending on formulation and depth, so the integrated model should be compared against the strongest standalone PCN per task, not a generic PCN.

| Task | Metric | Baseline | Expected integrated-model behavior |
|---|---|---|---|
| MNIST / Fashion-MNIST 10-way | Accuracy, expected calibration error (ECE), inference iterations | Backprop CNN; PCN; μPC | Match or exceed standalone PCN; better calibration |
| Corrupted MNIST | Robustness, pattern-completion rate | PCN; Hopfield/ENN; KNN | Superior robustness and completion from engram pattern completion |
| Few-shot 5-way 1/5-shot | Sample efficiency, within/cross-episode accuracy | PCN; Hopfield; prototypical network | Fast engram write gives 1-shot gains; consolidation improves 5-shot across episodes |
| Split-MNIST continual | Average accuracy, forgetting, engram-overlap–interference | EWC; PCN; VAE+MHN (CLS) | Co-allocation/memory linking reduces interference versus controls |
| OOD (held-out digit / cross-dataset) | Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) from free energy / error magnitude | PCN; backprop softmax | Higher AUROC; no stable attractor on OOD inputs |

Standalone PCNs do not form a single accuracy ceiling: reported MNIST results span roughly 25% to 99.6% depending on the energy/loss formulation, architecture, and depth. Standard squared-error PCNs remain far below backprop, whereas modern bi-directional or parameter-scaled PCNs approach backprop on MNIST ([Pinchetti et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01163); [Qiu et al. 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0893608025006653); [Innocenti et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13124v2)). These figures are configuration-dependent and should be re-checked against results tables.

The ablation matrix tests whether any component is decorative:

| Ablation | Representational/behavioral deficit | Predicted metric change | Biological counterpart |
|---|---|---|---|
| No allocation | Dense engram layer; loss of pattern separation; overlapping prototypes | Accuracy ↓ moderate; forgetting ↑ large; OOD AUROC ↓ moderate | CREB/excitability manipulation; engram silencing |
| No tagging/capture | Labile fast weights; no stable engram write | Forgetting ↑ large; sample efficiency ↓ moderate; accuracy ↓ moderate | Protein-synthesis blockade (late-LTP/PRP) |
| No consolidation | No systems transfer; engrams remain labile | Forgetting ↑ large; sample efficiency ↓ large; accuracy ↓ moderate | Systems-consolidation disruption |
| No replay | No generative consolidation; weak class prototypes | Generalization ↓ moderate; sample efficiency ↓ moderate; forgetting ↑ small | Sharp-wave-ripple disruption |
| No error units | No precision-weighted errors; no pattern completion | Accuracy ↓ large; robustness ↓ large; OOD AUROC ↓ moderate | PCN error-unit ablation |
| No precision | Unweighted errors; no confidence/uncertainty signal | Calibration/ECE ↑ large; OOD AUROC ↓ large; robustness ↓ moderate | Cholinergic/noradrenergic manipulation |

The directions follow from the mechanism: sparse allocation keeps class prototypes separated, so its removal should most strongly increase forgetting; error signaling implements pattern completion, so its removal should most strongly reduce accuracy and robustness; precision converts prediction error into confidence, so its removal should most strongly degrade calibration and OOD detection.

**Falsifiable predictions.** (1) Engram reactivation is generative, not merely discriminative: reactivating a class engram from a partial cue should complete the stored pattern and support reconstruction, not just read out a label ([Millidge et al. 2022](https://ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/0774.pdf); [Tang et al. 2023](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1010719)). (2) Precision/gain on error units changes between perception and recall ([Barron et al. 2020](https://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/prediction-and-memory-predictive-coding-account)). (3) Offline replay generates prototypical category instances rather than veridical episodes and is prioritized by each memory's contribution to performance ([Barry & Love 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9758580/)). (4) Sparser engram ensembles show less inter-task interference and lower forgetting ([Guskjolen et al. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02137-5)). (5) Engram silencing abolishes recall but not feedforward perception; disrupting error units abolishes pattern completion ([Roy et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5699085/); [Liu et al. 2012](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature11028)).

The model is falsified if: engram silencing leaves recall intact; replay disruption leaves consolidation and generalization intact; precision manipulation leaves error-unit gain and calibration unchanged; engram sparsity does not correlate with interference; or the network cannot perform generation/reconstruction as well as classification.

A validation roadmap would proceed in stages: (M0) a rate model tested on static, corrupted, few-shot, and sequential benchmarks, with acceptance requiring engram priors to improve robustness and retention over a standalone PCN and the improvement to be abolished by engram silencing; (M1) ablations demonstrating that each mechanism is necessary and that engram-dependent recall is dissociable from feedforward perception; (M2) a spiking realization matching the rate model on pattern completion and retention; (M3) a neural-data comparison of reactivation, precision/gain shifts, and replay content; and (M4) scaling to larger input dimensionality and class count. Each milestone passes only when its specific signature is observed, not when aggregate accuracy rises.

## 7. Limitations and threats to validity

The most consequential vulnerabilities are (1) the stability of deep predictive-coding stacks under local learning, (2) the segregation of error and prediction neurons, (3) the hybrid engram-as-index/content decision, and (4) the unresolved gap between rate-based and spiking implementations.

Deep PCNs face prediction-error imbalance and exploding/vanishing prediction errors, and performance degrades beyond five to seven layers unless precision weighting and parameterization are carefully designed ([Qi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23800v3); [Innocenti et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13124v2)). The integrated model must either adopt such fixes and justify them biologically, or demonstrate that its engram priors avoid the pathologies without them. A second objection targets the canonical microcircuit's error/prediction segregation: classical PC requires specific connectivity motifs whose emergence is unexplained, and one-to-one correspondence between error and prediction neurons is a biologically problematic assumption ([Zhang et al. 2025](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1013112)). A third objection concerns local learning stability in recurrent circuits: Hebbian learning embodies positive feedback that tends to cause instabilities, and recurrent excitation compounds the problem. The engram layer is recurrent and its consolidation pathway writes into slow weights, so the model must specify how it avoids the instability.

The hybrid engram-as-index/content decision is more robust to a pure index-theory critique than a pure index model, but it still depends on the index component; a reassessment of hippocampal index theory could constrain the model's retrieval claims. The model also omits glia, vasculature, development, receptor kinetics, and full connectomics. The omissions most likely to change behavior are receptor kinetics and neuromodulator dynamics (precision is treated as a scalar gain, but acetylcholine, norepinephrine, and dopamine have slow, overlapping time courses and receptor-specific effects), interneuron diversity (the model abstracts PV+, SST+, and VIP+ subtypes), and glial modulation of synaptic plasticity and neuromodulator clearance.

The most consequential simplification for classification is the treatment of precision. Most machine-learning formulations of predictive coding set precision to 1 for simplicity, overlooking its role in stabilizing learning dynamics ([Qi et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23800v3)). If the integrated model fixes precision, its classification performance may be brittle to corrupted inputs and its OOD-detection claims unsupported. The label pathway is also a design choice, not a demonstrated neural mechanism: the default self-supervised prototype formation with labels entering only as a readout mapping should be described as such, and a one-hot teacher as an experimental handle.

MNIST is small, clean, and low-variance; strong accuracy on MNIST does not establish robustness, few-shot generalization, or continual-learning ability. The model's claims should be treated as proof-of-concept until tested on corrupted inputs, few-shot episodes, and split-MNIST task sequences. The spiking realization is especially uncertain: the spiking predictive-coding literature is under-studied and fragmented, prediction errors are represented in at least three distinct ways, and whether predictive coding reduces energy consumption is still a matter of debate ([spiking PC survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05386v1)). Full connectome-scale simulation remains infeasible.

Finally, several primary sources were not retrievable in full text during the preparation of this report, including the canonical necessity/sufficiency review and some neuromodulation and replay references. Where a claim rests on an abstract or secondary description rather than a verified full text, it should be treated as provisional. The core learning-rule, dendritic-error, associative-memory, and allocation findings rest on verified primary or authoritative sources.

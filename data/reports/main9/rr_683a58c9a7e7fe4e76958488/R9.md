# Engram-Predictive Coding Network: A Biologically Plausible Classification Model Integrating Memory Assemblies, Inhibitory Gating, and Local Error-Based Inference

## Abstract

This report proposes an **Engram-Predictive Coding Network (E-PCN)**: a biologically plausible recurrent spiking architecture that integrates **engram theory**—memory as dynamic excitatory and inhibitory cell assemblies—with **predictive coding**—hierarchical inference through top-down predictions and bottom-up residual errors. The model is designed to perform classification tasks while preserving core biological constraints: local synaptic plasticity, spike-based computation, inhibitory interneuron diversity, neuromodulatory gating, recurrent cortical dynamics, and consolidation-like replay. The central claim is that classification can be implemented not as a purely feedforward mapping, but as **recognition by predictive-memory stabilization**: sensory inputs activate candidate engram assemblies, top-down predictions suppress expected activity, residual errors iteratively refine the representation, and a sparse classification readout is selected when prediction error and assembly competition converge. The most defensible model, based on the supplied evidence, is a **hybrid**: predictive coding supplies the inference and representation-learning engine, while excitatory and inhibitory engrams supply stable, recallable class memories and pattern separation. Three-factor and state-dependent plasticity provide biologically plausible credit assignment without requiring full backpropagation at every synapse.

---

## 1. Introduction: Why Combine Engrams and Predictive Coding?

A biologically plausible classifier must solve two linked problems. First, it must form **stable category memories** that can be reactivated by partial or noisy inputs. Second, it must perform **credit assignment** so that synapses responsible for correct or incorrect classifications are modified appropriately. Standard deep learning solves the second problem with backpropagation, but this requires non-local information and sequential backward updates that are biologically problematic. Predictive coding has been advanced as a more biologically plausible alternative because it can use local computations and has theoretical links to backpropagation while supporting classifiers, generators, associative memories, and arbitrary graph topologies ([Millidge et al., 2022](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2022/774)).

Engram theory supplies the complementary memory substrate. Contemporary models increasingly treat memories as **excitatory cell assemblies**, but recent work emphasizes that inhibitory assemblies—sometimes called **ingrams**—are essential for balancing, gating, stabilizing, and recalling those memories through disinhibition ([bioRxiv, 2026a](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.751748v1.full-text)). In this report, I argue that the most biologically plausible classification system is not a feedforward CNN-like pipeline, nor a pure predictive coding hierarchy, nor a pure reinforcement-learning spiking network. It is a **recurrent predictive memory system** in which classification emerges from the interaction of sensory prediction, engram completion, inhibitory competition, and neuromodulated plasticity.

## 2. Foundational Concepts

### 2.1 Engrams as Dynamic Excitatory-Inhibitory Assemblies

Engrams are often modeled as ensembles of excitatory neurons whose coactivation stores a memory. However, memory stability in cortex and hippocampus also depends on inhibition. A recurrent spiking-network model showed that co-active inhibitory plasticity at both inhibitory-to-excitatory and inhibitory-to-inhibitory synapses can stabilize excitatory-inhibitory memory assemblies in cortical-like asynchronous irregular activity and allow recall either by direct engram stimulation or by disinhibition ([bioRxiv, 2026a](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.751748v1.full-text)).

This finding is important for classification. A classifier must distinguish overlapping classes—for example, cat versus dog images, or similar handwritten digits. Inhibitory-to-inhibitory plasticity appears especially important for high-quality disinhibitory recall: networks lacking it show weaker recall and fail to discriminate overlapping engrams, while broad inhibitory plasticity supports both pattern completion and pattern separation ([bioRxiv, 2026a](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.751748v1.full-text)).

Engrams are also dynamic rather than fixed. A 2024 spiking-network model and mouse dentate gyrus experiments suggest that memories become more selective during consolidation as neurons enter and leave engrams; inhibitory activity during recall and inhibitory synaptic plasticity during consolidation are critical for selectivity ([Jiang et al., 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10827-024-00889-9)). This directly motivates a classifier whose class assemblies are not static labels but evolving attractor-like states refined by experience and replay.

### 2.2 Predictive Coding as Local Error-Based Inference

Predictive coding proposes that higher cortical areas send top-down predictions to lower areas, while feedforward pathways transmit residual prediction errors upward. Rao and Sejnowski’s early formulation connected predictive coding to cortical feedback and spike-timing-dependent plasticity, proposing that feedback connections instantiate generative models and recurrent connections encode temporal dynamics ([Rao & Sejnowski, 2002](http://papers.cnl.salk.edu/PDFs/Predictive%20Coding%2C%20Cortical%20Feedback%2C%20and%20Spike-Timing%20Dependent%20Plasticity%202002-3365.pdf)).

Modern machine-learning work strengthens the case that predictive coding can train useful classifiers. Whittington and Bogacz showed that predictive coding networks can approximate error backpropagation while using local Hebbian synaptic plasticity; under certain parameter settings, their updates converge to backpropagation ([Whittington & Bogacz, 2017](http://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/approximation-error-backpropagation-algorithm-predictive-coding-network-local-hebbian)). A major advantage is that predictive coding reduces reliance on non-local synaptic information, which is one of the main biological objections to standard backpropagation ([Whittington & Bogacz, 2017](http://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/approximation-error-backpropagation-algorithm-predictive-coding-network-local-hebbian)).

Empirically, Wen et al.’s Deep Predictive Coding Network used bidirectional recurrent convolutional processing in which higher layers predicted lower-layer representations and feedforward pathways sent prediction errors upward. On CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and MNIST, the PCN consistently outperformed its matched feedforward-only CNN counterpart, and performance generally improved as more recursive computation cycles were used ([Wen et al., 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/wen18a/wen18a.pdf)).

## 3. Proposed Model: Engram-Predictive Coding Network

### 3.1 Overview

The proposed **Engram-Predictive Coding Network (E-PCN)** is a hierarchical recurrent spiking network with four interacting subsystems:

1. **Sensory predictive coding hierarchy**  
   Learns compressed representations through bottom-up prediction errors and top-down predictions.

2. **Excitatory class engram layer**  
   Forms sparse class-specific and feature-specific assemblies.

3. **Inhibitory ingram layer**  
   Provides matched inhibitory replicas or “negative images” of excitatory representations to stabilize, gate, and suppress familiar or irrelevant activity.

4. **Neuromodulated classification and consolidation system**  
   Uses three-factor plasticity, reward-modulated STDP, and state-dependent routing of credit-assignment signals.

The classifier does not simply map an image to a label. Instead, it performs **iterative recognition**: sensory input activates lower-level features; prediction errors climb the hierarchy; candidate class engrams generate top-down predictions; inhibitory ingram circuits suppress expected components and sharpen competition; the winning class is the assembly that best minimizes residual prediction error while maintaining stable recurrent activation.

---

## 4. Architecture

### 4.1 Hierarchical Predictive Layers

Each layer contains:

- excitatory pyramidal neurons representing latent causes;
- prediction-error populations, including positive and negative prediction-error units;
- PV+, SST+, and VIP+ inhibitory interneuron populations;
- recurrent lateral connections for temporal context and attractor stabilization.

The top-down pathway predicts lower-level activity, while the feedforward pathway transmits residual errors. This follows the predictive coding principle that higher levels predict lower-level representations, and only mismatches drive further updating ([Wen et al., 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/wen18a/wen18a.pdf)).

### 4.2 Engram Layer

At upper levels, sparse excitatory assemblies represent learned classes and prototypes. For image classification, an engram may correspond to a digit, object class, or abstract feature combination. These assemblies are not fixed “grandmother cells”; they are distributed, overlapping, and dynamic.

Distributed engrams across connected regions may balance generalization and discrimination: multiple regions initially promote generalization, while a subset later switches toward discrimination during consolidation ([Jiang et al., 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10827-024-00889-9)). In E-PCN, early training allows broad class assemblies that generalize, while replay and inhibitory plasticity later refine them into more selective class manifolds.

### 4.3 Inhibitory Ingram Layer

The inhibitory layer forms matched inhibitory patterns associated with excitatory engrams. Inhibitory engrams can be interpreted as negative images of excitatory representations, formed through homeostatic potentiation of inhibition onto highly excited postsynaptic cells. They maintain excitation-inhibition balance, reduce responses to familiar stimuli, and keep excitatory memories latent until contextually relevant recall ([Barron, 2021](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:8bb38480-782d-486f-8da3-a4d6f11014be/files/m3c629f963ce19dc5d2989a8b074ad79c)).

This is crucial for classification because class memories must be stable but not constantly active. The inhibitory ingram layer keeps class assemblies silent until the input and top-down context disinhibit the appropriate assembly.

### 4.4 Interneuron Motif

The E-PCN uses a conserved hippocampal-neocortical inhibitory motif:

| Interneuron type | Main target | Proposed E-PCN function |
|---|---:|---|
| PV+ | Soma and axon initial segment | Fast competition, gain control, oscillatory coordination |
| SST+ | Dendrites | Gating top-down predictions and dendritic credit signals |
| VIP+ | SST+ interneurons | Contextual disinhibition and recall gating |

This motif is biologically grounded: PV+ interneurons target principal-cell soma and axon initial segments, SST+ interneurons target dendrites, and VIP+ interneurons inhibit SST+ cells, giving state-dependent control over glutamatergic engram neurons ([Booker & Vida, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2021.750541/full)).

## 5. Learning Rules

### 5.1 Predictive Hebbian Plasticity

The core predictive coding update is local. Synapses update based on presynaptic activity, postsynaptic activity, and local prediction error. This is consistent with the result that predictive coding networks can approximate backpropagation while using local Hebbian plasticity ([Whittington & Bogacz, 2017](http://www.bndu.ox.ac.uk/papers/approximation-error-backpropagation-algorithm-predictive-coding-network-local-hebbian)).

A simplified excitatory update is:

\[
\Delta W_{ij}^{E} = \eta \cdot e_i \cdot r_j
\]

where \(e_i\) is the local prediction error at the postsynaptic unit and \(r_j\) is presynaptic activity.

### 5.2 Three-Factor Inhibitory Plasticity

The model uses three-factor learning at inhibitory synapses:

\[
\Delta W_{ij}^{I} \propto c(t)(R_i - R_0)r_j
\]

where \(r_j\) is presynaptic inhibitory activity, \(R_i\) is postsynaptic pyramidal activity, \(R_0\) is a target activity, and \(c(t)\) is a third factor such as surprise, mismatch, reward, or global error.

A 2026 Nature Communications model showed that a biologically plausible three-factor learning rule can guide local inhibitory synapses to compute mismatches between stimuli and predictions, producing positive and negative prediction-error neurons and converging to the same optimal solution as gradient descent ([Wang et al., 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-70354-x)). This is especially relevant to E-PCN because predictive classification requires inhibition to cancel expected input while preserving mismatch signals.

### 5.3 Reward-Modulated STDP for Classification Readout

The final classification readout uses reward-modulated STDP. Standard STDP depends on pre- and postsynaptic spike timing, but reward-modulated STDP adds a third neuromodulatory signal such as reward or expected reward. This allows microscopic synaptic eligibility traces to support macroscopic behavioral learning ([Legenstein et al., 2008](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000180)).

For supervised classification, correct labels can be implemented as a teaching or reward signal. For reinforcement-like settings, reward arrives after action selection. In both cases, eligibility traces bridge the delay between spike events and feedback, solving a temporal credit-assignment problem.

### 5.4 State-Dependent Plasticity Switching

The E-PCN adopts a selective credit-assignment strategy inspired by plasticity-switching RNNs. In those models, low-weight synapses used local Hebbian-like updates, while high-weight synapses received an idealized non-local credit-assignment signal. These networks learned temporal and working-memory tasks in fewer trials than fully backpropagation-trained RNNs, despite routing backpropagation-based updates to fewer recurrent weights ([bioRxiv, 2026b](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.06.10.731456v2.full)).

In E-PCN, this principle is biologized as follows:

- weak synapses use unsupervised local predictive/Hebbian learning;
- candidate engram synapses strengthen through repeated coactivation;
- strong, stable synapses become eligible for modulatory credit signals;
- credit assignment is routed only to synapses participating in stable class assemblies.

This is a reasonable compromise between biological plausibility and task performance. It avoids claiming that the brain performs full backpropagation everywhere, while acknowledging that complex classification likely requires more than unguided Hebbian learning.

## 6. Inference and Classification Dynamics

The E-PCN classifies an input through recurrent settling:

1. **Input encoding**  
   Spiking sensory neurons encode an image or event stream.

2. **Bottom-up prediction error propagation**  
   Lower layers compare input against top-down predictions and transmit residual errors upward.

3. **Candidate engram activation**  
   Upper-level class assemblies compete to explain the sensory evidence.

4. **Top-down reconstruction**  
   Candidate engrams send predictions downward to reconstruct expected lower-level features.

5. **Inhibitory cancellation and mismatch sharpening**  
   Ingram circuits suppress predictable activity and amplify residual mismatch.

6. **Class decision**  
   The class whose engram minimizes prediction error and sustains stable activation is selected.

7. **Plasticity update**  
   Local predictive updates refine representations; reward or label signals modulate readout and selected high-value synapses.

This mechanism resembles Wen et al.’s PCN, where repeated bottom-up and top-down cycles refine internal representations before classification ([Wen et al., 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/wen18a/wen18a.pdf)). The difference is that E-PCN embeds this recurrence in a spiking excitatory-inhibitory memory architecture.

---

## 7. Consolidation and Replay

A biologically plausible classifier should not only learn during task exposure but also consolidate memories offline. The E-PCN includes a sleep-like replay mode:

- principal neurons and PV+ interneurons become more active;
- SST+ interneurons are relatively quiescent;
- selected engrams reactivate;
- inhibitory plasticity sharpens class boundaries;
- weakly useful neurons drop out of assemblies.

This is consistent with evidence that during post-learning sleep, principal neurons and PV+ interneurons generally become more active while SST+ interneurons are relatively quiescent, a state that may permit selective engram reactivation and synaptic strengthening during consolidation ([Booker & Vida, 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neural-circuits/articles/10.3389/fncir.2021.750541/full)).

The consolidation phase is especially important for classification. During initial learning, broad engrams may promote generalization. During replay, inhibitory refinement improves discrimination. This mirrors the prediction that distributed engrams initially promote generalization, while a subset later shifts toward discrimination during consolidation ([Jiang et al., 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10827-024-00889-9)).

## 8. Why This Model Should Perform Classification

### 8.1 Evidence from Predictive Coding Classifiers

Predictive coding has already demonstrated classification capacity. Wen et al.’s PCN consistently outperformed matched feedforward CNNs on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, SVHN, and MNIST, and accuracy generally improved with more recurrent computation cycles ([Wen et al., 2018](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/wen18a/wen18a.pdf)). This supports the idea that recurrent prediction-error refinement is not merely biologically attractive but computationally useful.

### 8.2 Evidence from Spiking Predictive Coding

Predictive Coding Light, introduced in 2025, shows that recurrent hierarchical spiking networks can learn useful unsupervised visual representations by suppressing predictable spikes rather than transmitting prediction errors upward. PCL achieved strong downstream classification performance while reducing spiking activity, including 89.12% accuracy on the DVS128 Gesture dataset among unsupervised approaches ([Milde et al., 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-64234-z)). This supports the feasibility of spike-based predictive representations for classification.

### 8.3 Evidence from Engram-Inhibitory Models

The E-PCN’s classification advantage should be strongest when categories overlap. Inhibitory-to-inhibitory plasticity improves disinhibitory recall and discrimination of overlapping engrams, while broader inhibitory plasticity supports pattern completion and separation ([bioRxiv, 2026a](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.09.15.751748v1.full-text)). In classification terms, this means the model should handle partial inputs, corrupted images, and ambiguous class boundaries better than a system lacking inhibitory memory assemblies.

## 9. Comparative Summary

| Model family | Strength | Limitation | E-PCN integration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feedforward CNN | Strong benchmark performance | Biologically implausible training; no recurrent inference | Used only as computational baseline |
| Predictive coding network | Local inference; recurrent refinement; can approximate backprop | Often abstract, non-spiking, limited memory mechanism | Supplies hierarchical inference engine |
| Reward-modulated STDP SNN | Biologically plausible reinforcement learning | Sensitive to reward scaling and delayed credit | Used for readout and feedback-modulated synapses |
| Engram assembly model | Stable recall, pattern completion | Often lacks explicit classification objective | Supplies class memory substrate |
| Inhibitory ingram model | Gating, stabilization, pattern separation | Still emerging experimentally | Supplies competition and latent memory control |
| Plasticity-switching RNN | Efficient selective credit assignment | Uses idealized BP in current form | Inspires state-dependent routing of modulatory signals |

## 10. Proposed Experimental Evaluation

A credible evaluation should test both machine-learning performance and biological predictions.

### 10.1 Classification Benchmarks

The model should be tested on:

- MNIST and N-MNIST for digit recognition;
- CIFAR-10 for object classification;
- DVS128 Gesture for event-based neuromorphic classification;
- corrupted or occluded variants for pattern completion;
- overlapping class subsets to test pattern separation.

### 10.2 Key Metrics

| Metric | Prediction |
|---|---|
| Accuracy | Should exceed comparable local-learning SNNs and approach PCN-like recurrent classifiers |
| Recurrent-cycle benefit | Accuracy should improve with additional inference cycles, as in PCN |
| Energy proxy | Spike count should fall when predictable features are suppressed |
| Robustness | Occluded inputs should trigger class engram completion |
| Separability | Inhibitory plasticity should improve overlapping-class discrimination |
| Biological signature | Class learning should produce sparse excitatory assemblies and matched inhibitory negative images |

### 10.3 Lesion Predictions

The model makes concrete biological predictions:

1. **PV+ disruption** should impair fast class competition and increase false positives.
2. **SST+ disruption** should impair dendritic top-down prediction gating.
3. **VIP+ disruption** should impair contextual recall and disinhibitory class activation.
4. **I-to-I plasticity blockade** should reduce discrimination between overlapping classes.
5. **Neuromodulatory third-factor removal** should preserve unsupervised representation learning but degrade task-relevant classification.

These predictions connect classification behavior to circuit-level mechanisms rather than treating accuracy as the only outcome.

---

## 11. Limitations and Reliability of Evidence

The strongest evidence comes from peer-reviewed predictive coding and reward-modulated STDP work, including Wen et al. (2018), Whittington and Bogacz (2017), Legenstein et al. (2008), and recent Nature Communications studies. The inhibitory engram and plasticity-switching RNN findings are highly relevant but appear as 2026 bioRxiv preprints in the supplied material; they should therefore be treated as promising but not yet definitive. Their value lies in offering mechanistic hypotheses that are testable and directly aligned with the proposed architecture.

A further limitation is that theory alone may not distinguish predictive-coding or target-learning accounts from backpropagation-like accounts. Experimental observations of cortical activity dynamics during inference and learning are needed to determine which mechanism better matches cortex ([Beumer, 2025](https://fse.studenttheses.ub.rug.nl/35898/1/mCCS2025LheaBeumer.pdf)). Therefore, the E-PCN should be evaluated not only by classification accuracy but also by neural dynamics, inhibitory plasticity signatures, replay effects, and perturbation outcomes.

---

## 12. Conclusion

The proposed E-PCN offers a concrete biologically plausible model that integrates engrams and predictive coding into one classification-capable network. Its core mechanism is **classification by predictive engram stabilization**: sensory evidence activates candidate memory assemblies; top-down predictions attempt to explain the input; residual errors refine the representation; inhibitory ingram circuits suppress predictable or competing activity; and neuromodulated plasticity consolidates task-relevant synapses.

My concrete assessment is that this hybrid architecture is more plausible and more computationally promising than either a pure predictive coding classifier or a pure engram attractor network. Predictive coding alone explains recurrent inference and local error minimization but does not fully specify how durable class memories are gated and recalled. Engram models explain memory stability and pattern completion but do not by themselves solve hierarchical credit assignment for classification. The integration of predictive coding with excitatory-inhibitory engram dynamics, three-factor inhibitory learning, reward-modulated STDP, and state-dependent plasticity gives the most coherent route from cortical mechanism to classification behavior.

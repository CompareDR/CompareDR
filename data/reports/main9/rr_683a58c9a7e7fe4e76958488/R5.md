# EPC-Net: A Biologically Plausible Model Integrating Memory Engrams and Predictive Coding for Classification

**A research proposal for a unified engram–predictive-coding network**

---

## Executive Summary

This report proposes **EPC-Net (Engram–Predictive Coding Network)**, a single neural architecture that fuses two of the most successful frameworks in modern neuroscience — the **engram theory of memory** and **hierarchical predictive coding** — into one network capable of supervised classification with only biologically plausible machinery. The core idea is a division of labour that mirrors the mammalian brain: a predictive-coding hierarchy (neocortex analogue) performs iterative, error-driven inference and slow generative learning, while sparse **engram ensembles** allocated by excitability-biased competition provide fast, one-shot, interference-resistant storage of the hierarchy's converged states (hippocampal/amygdala analogue), and a **hippocampal index module** binds each engram to its class label and later reinstates it during offline replay for consolidation. Predictive coding contributes what engram biology lacks — a principled, local algorithm for perceptual inference and credit assignment ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)) — while engrams contribute what predictive coding lacks — fast episodic storage, competitive sparse allocation, and a biologically grounded route around catastrophic forgetting ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)). The model is specified at the level of equations, phases of operation, and anatomical mapping, and it generates falsifiable predictions at both the machine-learning benchmark level and the neurobiological level. No published work to date has proposed this specific integration, although adjacent results — predictive-coding associative memories ([Salvatori et al. 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/1fb36c4ccf88f7e67ead155496f02338-Paper.pdf)) and predictive-coding models of hippocampal replay ([Tang et al. 2023](https://openreview.net/pdf/53eef03fb906568de161b516705d06871678dc6c.pdf)) — demonstrate that the constituent pieces are individually viable.

## 1. Two Frameworks, One Network

### 1.1 The engram: sparse, allocated, and fast

The modern engram literature has converged on a strikingly mechanistic picture of memory formation. An engram is a **sparse ensemble of neurons** that was active during an experience, underwent enduring physical change, and whose reactivation is sufficient to induce recall of that experience ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)). Critically for any computational model, membership in an engram is not random: eligible neurons **compete** for allocation, and the neurons that win are those with the highest intrinsic excitability at the time of learning — neurons overexpressing CREB, which increases excitability, are roughly three times more likely than their neighbours to be recruited into a fear-memory engram in the lateral amygdala ([Han et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1139438), [Yiu et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2014.07.017)). Under purely physiological conditions, the ~10% of lateral amygdala principal neurons that are endogenously more excitable before training account for over 90% of the subsequently labelled engram, confirming that excitability-driven competition is the brain's default allocation rule rather than an artifact of artificial manipulation ([Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)).

Two further properties make engram biology uniquely relevant to machine learning. First, **engram sparsity is actively enforced**: parvalbumin-positive (PV+) interneurons in the amygdala and somatostatin-positive (SOM+) interneurons in the dentate gyrus constrain engram size through lateral inhibition, so allocation is a competitive, winner-take-all process with a roughly fixed sparsity budget per region ([Morrison et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2016.07.007), [Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)). Second, **excitability decays over hours**, and this decay implements *memory linking*: events encoded close in time (within roughly 5–6 hours in rodents) recruit overlapping ensembles because the previously allocated neurons are still more excitable, whereas events separated by ~24 hours recruit disjoint ensembles, keeping the memories distinct ([Rashid et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0594), [Josselyn & Frankland 2018](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-080317-061956)). Engrams thus implement exactly what continual-learning systems need: rapid one-shot write, sparse non-overlapping codes that minimise interference, and a temporal "glue" that integrates related episodes — and once formed, engrams are dynamic, with inhibitory plasticity essential for maintaining their selectivity ([Tomé et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hipo.70032)).

### 1.2 Predictive coding: hierarchical inference with local learning

Predictive coding models the cortex as a **hierarchical generative model** in which each level predicts the activity of the level below through feedback connections, while the residual mismatch — the prediction error — is carried upward by feedforward connections and used to revise the higher-level representation ([Rao & Ballard 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580)). Perception is thereby recast as iterative approximate Bayesian inference: state ("representation") units converge to the configuration of hidden causes that best explains the input, while error units report what remains unexplained, with each error weighted by its **precision** (inverse variance), a quantity proposed to be implemented as neuromodulatory gain on the postsynaptic responses of error-coding superficial pyramidal cells ([Friston 2005](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2005.1622), [Feldman & Friston 2010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnhum.2010.00215)). The canonical microcircuit mapping of Bastos and colleagues assigns prediction errors to superficial layers (broadcast forward, gamma-band) and predictions to deep layers (sent backward, alpha/beta-band), a scheme broadly consistent with laminar anatomy and oscillatory physiology ([Bastos et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23177956/)).

Decisively for a classification model, predictive coding is not merely an account of perception but a **trainable algorithm**. Whittington and Bogacz showed that when a supervised predictive-coding network's input and label layers are clamped and inference runs to convergence, the resulting weight updates — computed from purely local products of pre-synaptic activity and postsynaptic prediction error — approximate the backpropagation algorithm and match its performance on MNIST ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)). The result has since been generalised to arbitrary computation graphs ([Millidge et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.04182v5)), benchmarked systematically against backpropagation on convolutional architectures ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)), and extended to bidirectional variants that classify at backprop-level accuracy while remaining generative and robust to missing input dimensions ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/6164b6e5352c139e9ddc1a98c09e4e4a-Paper-Conference.pdf)). Related energy-based frameworks — Equilibrium Propagation and dendritic error-coding microcircuits — confirm that the underlying computation, settling to an equilibrium followed by local plasticity, has multiple plausible neural implementations ([Scellier & Bengio 2017](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2017.00024), [Sacramento et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.11393)).

### 1.3 Why each framework needs the other

Despite their individual successes, both frameworks have complementary blind spots that motivate integration. Predictive coding networks are fundamentally **slow learners of weights**: knowledge lives in the generative weight matrices, which must be sculpted by many iterations of error-driven updates, and standard implementations have no mechanism for storing the specific configuration of activity that occurred on a single trial — the definition of episodic memory ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)). Recent modelling of semantic versus episodic memory in predictive-coding networks makes the deficit precise: a predictive-coding "neocortex" can memorise individual examples only by overfitting when the training set is tiny, and loses per-example recall exactly when it acquires generalisable structure — dense overlapping cortical codes trade episodic specificity for semantic generalisation ([Fontaine & Alexandre 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05202850v1/file/main.pdf)). Engram biology, conversely, explains *where* a memory is written but not *how* the brain computes the representation to be stored: allocation, linking, and consolidation presuppose an inference machinery that engram theory simply does not supply, and engram studies of classification-like behaviour assume cortical feature extraction as given ([Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)).

The complementarity is summarised in the table below and constitutes the design brief for EPC-Net: the predictive-coding hierarchy supplies inference, local credit assignment, and a generative pathway for replay; the engram system supplies sparse allocation, one-shot episodic storage, and a protection mechanism against interference. Notably, the brain itself appears to organise the two systems exactly this way — complementary fast hippocampal and slow neocortical learning systems whose interaction through replay drives systems consolidation ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)).

| Capability | Predictive coding | Engram theory | Assigned in EPC-Net to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Perceptual inference / feature extraction | Core strength: iterative error minimisation ([Rao & Ballard 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580)) | Not addressed | PC hierarchy |
| Credit assignment for deep learning | Local Hebbian × error rules approximating backprop ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)) | Not addressed | PC hierarchy |
| One-shot storage of a single episode | Absent (weights change slowly) | Core strength: fast allocation & Hebbian binding ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)) | Engram ensembles + index module |
| Interference-free capacity | Poor for individual items ([Fontaine & Alexandre 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05202850v1/file/main.pdf)) | Sparse competitive codes, fixed sparsity ([Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)) | Engram ensembles |
| Linking vs separating related episodes | No native mechanism | Excitability-decay window ([Rashid et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0594)) | Excitability dynamics |
| Consolidation & generative replay | Feedback weights are generative by construction | Reactivation/replay is the biological route ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)) | Index module + PC generative path |

## 2. Design Principles

### 2.1 Constraints of biological plausibility

EPC-Net is designed under five constraints drawn from the literature on biologically plausible learning. **(i) Locality of plasticity**: every synaptic update must depend only on quantities available at the synapse — presynaptic activity, postsynaptic activity or error, and broadcast neuromodulatory signals — a constraint satisfied by predictive-coding weight updates and by three-factor Hebbian rules in which co-activation deposits a decaying eligibility trace that a delayed dopaminergic or noradrenergic signal converts into lasting change ([Gerstner et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6079224/), [Yagishita et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255514)). **(ii) No weight transport**: forward and backward pathways are separate synaptic populations that learn independently, avoiding backpropagation's biologically impossible requirement of exactly symmetric weights; in predictive coding this is handled naturally because inference dynamics carry error information upward through dedicated error units rather than through transposed weight matrices ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)). **(iii) Separation of message types**: predictions and errors travel on anatomically distinct channels, mirroring the deep-versus-superficial laminar segregation of feedback and feedforward cortical pathways ([Bastos et al. 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23177956/)).

The remaining constraints come from engram biology. **(iv) Competitive sparse allocation**: writing to memory must engage only a small, approximately fixed-size subset of cells, selected by a competition in which intrinsic excitability biases the outcome and inhibition enforces the sparsity budget ([Morrison et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2016.07.007), [Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)). **(v) Multiple timescales**: the system must support fast one-shot writing (hippocampal/engram), slow distributed learning (cortical weights), and an intermediate-hours excitability variable that links or separates episodes, together with an offline consolidation phase in which fast traces train the slow system — the complementary-learning-systems architecture in computational form ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419), [Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3008780/)). These five constraints rule out backpropagation-trained components, experience buffers of raw data, and any global synchronisation beyond diffuse neuromodulatory broadcast, while leaving ample room for a fully specified classification network.

### 2.2 Functional requirements for classification

The target behaviour is supervised and semi-supervised classification under four regimes: standard batch classification; **few-shot learning**, where one or a handful of labelled examples of a new class must be usable immediately; **class-incremental continual learning**, where new classes arrive sequentially without access to old data and where established machine-learning methods fail unless they replay data ([Van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2)); and **robust inference**, where classification must tolerate corrupted or partially missing inputs. These regimes map one-to-one onto the complementary strengths identified above: few-shot performance demands one-shot engram writing; continual learning demands sparse non-interfering codes plus generative replay; robustness demands the generative completion that bidirectional predictive coding already exhibits, where models that jointly minimise bottom-up and top-down errors classify substantially better than purely discriminative ones when half the input pixels are missing ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/6164b6e5352c139e9ddc1a98c09e4e4a-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

A final functional requirement is interpretability of the learned memory structure. Because EPC-Net's episodic knowledge lives in explicitly identified engram ensembles rather than being entangled throughout the weight matrices, the model should support **selective inspection, lesioning, and editing** of individual memories — mirroring optogenetic engram experiments in which activating or silencing the labelled ensemble drives or abolishes recall ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)). This is not only a neuroscientific nicety: engram-style gating of sparse subnetworks has already proven effective as a continual-learning mechanism in artificial networks, where task-specific stochastic "engram" masks mitigated catastrophic forgetting in binarised neural networks ([Aguilar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21436), [Khadkikar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01659v2)).

## 3. The Proposed Model: EPC-Net

### 3.1 Architecture overview

EPC-Net consists of four coupled subsystems, shown in Figure 1. The **predictive-coding backbone** is a hierarchy of L levels; each level l contains a population of state units **x**_l (deep-pyramidal analogues holding the current estimate of hidden causes) and a population of error units **ε**_l (superficial-pyramidal analogues broadcasting precision-weighted residuals upward). Generative weights **W**_l carry top-down predictions; errors ascend through dedicated error pathways, so no weight symmetry is required. Atop the hierarchy sits a **label layer** with one unit per class, clamped to the target during training and free during testing, exactly as in supervised predictive-coding formulations ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/)).

Attached to each level is an **engram ensemble** — a large pool of sparsely activated units that compete for allocation and, once allocated, bind the level's converged state pattern in a single step; the top-level ensemble is enlarged and coupled to a **hippocampal index module**, a fast associative store that binds (engram code ↔ top-level state ↔ label) triples and supports pattern completion from partial cues, in the spirit of hippocampal indexing theory in which the hippocampus stores pointers that reactivate distributed cortical patterns rather than storing content itself ([Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3008780/)). A **neuromodulatory control system** broadcasts three diffuse signals: an acetylcholine-analogue that sets the precision (gain) of error units ([Moran et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4745-12.2013)), a dopamine-analogue that serves as the third factor gating conversion of eligibility traces into weight changes ([Yagishita et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255514)), and a noradrenaline-analogue that modulates the excitability variable driving engram allocation. All learning rules are local; the only global signals are these broadcast neuromodulators, which is precisely their biological mode of action.

### 3.2 Predictive-coding backbone: dynamics and learning

Following the supervised predictive-coding formulation, the network minimises a free-energy functional that is a sum of precision-weighted squared prediction errors across levels. Let x_l denote state-unit activities at level l, f an elementwise nonlinearity, W_{l+1} the generative weights from level l+1 down to level l, and Π_l the precision (inverse variance) at level l:

$$\varepsilon_l = x_l - f(W_{l+1}\, x_{l+1}), \qquad \mathcal{F} = \sum_{l=0}^{L-1} \tfrac{1}{2}\,\big\| \Pi_l^{1/2}\, \varepsilon_l \big\|^2$$

State units perform gradient descent on F while the input (and, during training, the label layer) are clamped:

$$\tau\,\dot{x}_l = -\,\Pi_l\,\varepsilon_l \;+\; \Big( W_{l}^{T}\, \Pi_{l-1}\, \varepsilon_{l-1} \Big) \odot f'\!\big(W_l x_l\big)$$

and after inference settles, weights are updated with a purely local, Hebbian-type rule — presynaptic state activity multiplied by postsynaptic prediction error:

$$\Delta W_l \;=\; \eta\; \Pi_{l-1}\, \varepsilon_{l-1}\; f(x_l)^{T}$$

Every term in this update is available at the synapse, and with precision scaling and converged inference these updates approximate the backpropagation gradient ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/), [Millidge et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.04182v5)). **Classification at test time** requires no output layer in the conventional sense: the input is clamped, the label units are left free, inference runs to equilibrium, and the predicted class is read out as the argmax over settled label-unit activity — equivalently, the class whose top-down prediction best explains the input. Because the same generative weights can be run in the downward direction with the label clamped and input free, the backbone doubles as a class-conditional generative model, the property that makes offline replay possible without storing any raw data ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/6164b6e5352c139e9ddc1a98c09e4e4a-Paper-Conference.pdf), [Van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2)).

### 3.3 Engram allocation: excitability-biased competition

Each engram ensemble contains N_E candidate units, of which only a fixed fraction ρ (the sparsity budget, biologically on the order of a few percent) may be allocated per episode. Every candidate unit i carries a slow scalar **excitability variable e_i** — the computational shadow of CREB-mediated intrinsic excitability — which decays exponentially with an hours-scale time constant. When an allocation event is triggered (Section 3.6), the competition score of each candidate is the sum of its stimulus-driven input (a random or learned projection of the level's converged state, u = A x_l) and its excitability bias:

$$s_i = u_i + \beta\, e_i, \qquad \mathcal{E} = \mathrm{top}\text{-}k\big(\{s_i\}\big),\quad k = \lceil \rho N_E \rceil$$

The top-k selection is the abstract equivalent of the biological winner-take-all competition in which more excitable neurons out-compete their neighbours, with the effective threshold implemented by feedback inhibition from a PV/SOM-analogue interneuron population that enforces the constant sparsity budget ([Morrison et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2016.07.007), [Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)); the dentate-gyrus literature shows how recurrent excitation balanced by strong feedback inhibition achieves exactly this sparse competitive pattern separation ([Myers et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2022.826278/full)). Allocated units receive a one-shot Hebbian write of the current state pattern, ΔM_l ∝ g x_lᵀ (where g is the binary engram code), and their excitability is boosted: e_i ← e_i + Δe for i ∈ E.

The decaying excitability endows the model with **memory linking for free**: because recently allocated cells remain more excitable for a window of hours, the next episode preferentially recruits an overlapping ensemble when it arrives within the window and a disjoint ensemble after it — reproducing the experimental finding that events separated by ~5 hours share engrams while events separated by ~24 hours do not ([Rashid et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0594)). In classification terms this yields a principled mechanism for **soft class structure**: examples of the same or related categories encountered in a session share ensemble mass, giving graded generalisation between related classes, while unrelated or temporally distant classes are pattern-separated, giving low interference. ### 3.4 The hippocampal index module and one-shot label binding

The top-level engram code g, the converged category-level state x_L, and the clamped label y are written together into the index module in a single step. Formally the module maintains an association matrix H updated by outer-product (Hebbian) storage, H ← H + g (x_L; y)ᵀ, which is the local-learning equivalent of the one-shot "memory indexing" operation — storing a pointer that can reinstate the distributed cortical pattern rather than the content itself ([Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3008780/)). Retrieval is pattern completion: presenting a partial cue (e.g. the state vector of a test item with no label) recovers the associated label-side pattern, and presenting a label during generation recovers the associated state pattern that seeds top-down replay. A predictive-coding formulation of this same associative binding — where a memory vector is iteratively corrected by its own prediction errors and stored by Hebbian updates on converged activities — has been shown to store and recall episodic content bound to sequence dynamics, mimicking hippocampal function, and to outperform modern Hopfield networks and backprop-trained autoencoders in completing heavily corrupted inputs ([Salvatori et al. 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/1fb36c4ccf88f7e67ead155496f02338-Paper.pdf), [Rao et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.23.497415v4.full-text)).

The label-binding write is gated by the **dopamine-analogue third factor**: co-activation of engram code and label deposits an eligibility trace, and the phasic "label confirmed" signal converts the trace into a permanent change only if it arrives within a short window — matching the experimental demonstration that dopamine enlarges striatal spines only when delivered within ~1–2 seconds of the pre/post pairing, and that distinct neuromodulators convert cortical eligibility traces into LTP and LTD on behavioural timescales ([Yagishita et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255514), [He et al. 2015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2015.09.037)). This gating has a direct functional role in classification: it suppresses storage of uncertain or misclassified episodes (where the confirming signal is absent) and implements a biologically grounded form of **reward-modulated curriculum** over what enters long-term episodic memory. It also provides the mechanism by which the label layer influences learning without any backward error propagation from an explicit loss — supervision is a neuromodulatory event, not a gradient.

### 3.5 Consolidation through precision-gated generative replay

EPC-Net alternates wakeful encoding phases with offline consolidation phases, as required by complementary learning systems theory ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)). During consolidation, index entries are sampled for replay with probability proportional to a salience score combining recency and the magnitude of prediction error at encoding — so surprising or recent episodes replay preferentially, consistent with evidence that novelty and reward bias which memories are replayed ([Snyder & Donato 2019 model evidence](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.19.882035.full)). A sampled index reinstates its label and top-level state; the label is clamped and activity flows **down** the generative weights, hallucinating the input-level pattern; this internally generated episode is then relearned by the predictive-coding backbone with its ordinary local update rule. Because the replayed input is self-generated, the precision of sensory-layer error units is gated toward zero so that the reinstated representation is "protected" from being overwritten by ascending errors — the same manipulation used in predictive-coding models of hippocampo-cortical replay, where setting input-layer prediction errors to zero isolates the cortical hierarchy during reinstated recall ([Tang et al. 2023](https://openreview.net/pdf/53eef03fb906568de161b516705d06871678dc6c.pdf)).

This mechanism directly targets catastrophic forgetting, the canonical failure of sequential classification learning: rather than storing raw data (biologically implausible and memory-intensive), the model rehearses its own generative recollections, an approach that is currently the only known class of method able to handle class-incremental learning without data storage, and whose brain-inspired refinements — replay through feedback connections from the top of the hierarchy, and internal replay of hidden representations rather than pixels — substantially improve performance ([Van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2), [Shin et al. 2017](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/6892-continual-learning-with-deep-generative-replay)). EPC-Net's distinctive contribution is that the replay *cue* is an engram index with a biologically motivated salience and linking structure, and the replay *channel* is the very same feedback pathway used for top-down prediction during perception — so consolidation requires no separate machinery, consistent with models in which hippocampal-thalamic-cortical plasticity-timescale differences alone drive the maturation of cortical engrams during consolidation ([Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28339-z)).

### 3.6 Neuromodulatory coordination and allocation triggers

The neuromodulatory system couples the two frameworks by reading out the predictive-coding network's own statistics and using them to control the engram system. The **allocation trigger** is novelty operationalised as precision-weighted prediction error: when the total free energy F after inference remains above a threshold (the input was poorly explained), the acetylcholine-analogue rises, error-unit gain increases, and an allocation event fires — poor prediction is exactly the signal that should open a new memory write, consistent with the proposal that acetylcholine reports expected uncertainty and tilts the balance from prior-driven to sensory-driven processing ([Yu & Dayan 2005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2005.02.004)). Familiar inputs that are well predicted cause no allocation, protecting capacity; this yields a principled "when to remember" gate that pure engram models must assume and pure predictive-coding models do not need until they acquire a memory system. The experimental literature supports the link: cholinergic modulation alters the precision of prediction-error signalling in auditory cortex in an oddball paradigm ([Pérez-González et al. 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/91475)).

During offline phases the acetylcholine-analogue falls, sensory precision collapses, and the dopamine third factor is uncoupled from external labels and instead tied to internally generated label reinstates — a computational gloss on the observation that hippocampal plasticity is suppressed during slow-wave sleep while cortical plasticity continues, allowing replay-driven transfer without corrupting the hippocampal store ([Snyder & Donato 2019](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2019.12.19.882035.full)). The noradrenaline-analogue controls the gain β on the excitability bias in the allocation rule, giving the model a single dial that moves the whole system between integrative (high β, overlapping engrams, strong generalisation) and discriminative (low β, separated engrams, sharp boundaries) regimes — a trade-off the brain manages through the same excitability fluctuations ([Josselyn & Frankland 2018](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-080317-061956)).

### 3.7 The full operational cycle

Figure 3 summarises the four-phase cycle. **Phase 1 (encode and infer)**: a stimulus is clamped at the input layer, label units are clamped if supervised, and the predictive-coding dynamics settle the hierarchy into its best explanation of the input. **Phase 2 (allocate)**: residual prediction error triggers the excitability-biased kWTA competition, and the winning sparse ensemble absorbs the converged state pattern by one-shot Hebbian write. **Phase 3 (index and bind)**: the hippocampal module binds the engram code to the category state and label, gated by the dopamine third factor on confirmation. **Phase 4 (consolidate)**: during offline periods, salience-weighted indices drive generative replay that trains the slow cortical weights, with sensory precision gated to protect reinstated representations. At test time the cycle reduces to Phase 1 with free label units: the settled label activity is the classification, optionally sharpened by a pattern-completion query to the index module for recently learned or few-shot classes.

Figure 3. EPC-Net operational cycle: wakeful inference (Phase 1), excitability-biased engram allocation (Phase 2), one-shot hippocampal indexing and label binding (Phase 3), and offline salience-weighted generative replay for consolidation (Phase 4).

Two properties of the cycle deserve emphasis. First, **all four phases use the same synapses and the same local learning rule**; phases differ only in which units are clamped and in the neuromodulatory context — which is precisely how the same cortical tissue supports perception, imagery, and consolidation. Second, the cycle gives the model **two routes to a classification**: a fast index-completion route for items seen once or twice (engram/hippocampal dominance, like early memory recall) and a slow generative route for consolidated knowledge (cortical dominance, like remote recall) — mirroring the experimentally observed shift of recall dependence from hippocampus to cortex over consolidation ([Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28339-z), [Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3008780/)). The model thereby predicts a measurable transition in which internal component is responsible for a given behavioural response as a function of training age — a signature that is testable both in silico (lesion studies) and, in principle, against the biological engram literature itself.

## 4. What the Integration Buys: Analysis

### 4.1 Predictive coding solves engram theory's representational problem

Engram experiments overwhelmingly study the amygdala, hippocampus, and retrosplenial cortex in fear conditioning and related paradigms, where the *content* of the memory is supplied by sensory systems the experiments take for granted ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)). Translating "engram" into a machine-learning component therefore leaves the hard question — what representation should be allocated and stored — unanswered. EPC-Net's answer is that the representation to be stored is the **converged fixed point of predictive-coding inference**: the hierarchy's best joint explanation of the input, including top-down contributions from the label. This choice has three advantages. First, the stored pattern is already *abstraction-complete* — it has integrated context and priors, not just raw features — because inference settles only after bidirectional message passing. Second, the stored pattern is *error-filtered*: aspects of the input that were well predicted produced little error activity, so allocation operates on a representation in which irrelevant variability has already been explained away, the same efficiency principle that links sparse coding and predictive coding in the first place ([Rao & Ballard 1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/4580)). Third, the trigger to store is theoretically grounded: high residual precision-weighted error is precisely the Bayesian signal that the current generative model cannot explain the input, so a new trace is warranted — connecting engram allocation to surprise-based gating of plasticity.

The integration also resolves a subtle circularity in allocation biology: excitability biases *which* neurons join an engram, but the brain must still decide *when* an experience merits an engram at all. Predictive coding supplies that decision variable continuously and cheaply as a byproduct of perception. In EPC-Net the same free-energy quantity that drives inference (Phase 1) therefore also gates memory formation (Phase 2), so the model never runs a separate novelty detector; novelty *is* unexplained prediction error, a framing consistent with hierarchical novelty detection demonstrated in predictive-coding networks ([Li et al. 2025, as reviewed](https://openreview.net/pdf/53eef03fb906568de161b516705d06871678dc6c.pdf)).

### 4.2 Engrams solve predictive coding's memory problem

Predictive-coding networks, like all weight-based learners, store knowledge only by slowly reshaping synaptic matrices, and they exhibit the characteristic failure of dense distributed learning: exposure to many examples yields generalisation at the cost of losing the ability to recall any individual example, while exposure to few examples yields memorisation without generalisation ([Fontaine & Alexandre 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05202850v1/file/main.pdf)). The engram subsystem removes this trade-off from the backbone. Fast-changing associations live in the engram ensembles and the index module (which tolerate one-shot writes because their codes are sparse and nearly orthogonal), while the generative weights are left free to drift slowly toward semantic structure. This is precisely the division of labour that complementary learning systems theory posits between hippocampus and neocortex — fast, pattern-separated episodic storage versus slow, overlapping semantic learning ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419)) — and that predictive-coding models of hippocampo-neocortical interaction have begun to reproduce, with cortical predictive-coding networks shown to support semantic memory but to require a separate fast system for episodic recall ([Fontaine & Alexandre 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05202850v1/file/main.pdf), [Tang et al. 2023](https://openreview.net/pdf/53eef03fb906568de161b516705d06871678dc6c.pdf)).

The benefit for classification is concrete and measurable. In few-shot regimes, a single labelled example can be classified correctly on its next presentation through index completion, before the generative weights have learned anything about the class — behaviour that weight-only predictive-coding networks cannot produce. In class-incremental regimes, the sparse allocation mechanism reduces write-interference directly (only a few percent of ensemble units change per episode), and replay consolidates old classes into the weights while new classes are being encoded, the two mechanisms jointly attacking the stability–plasticity dilemma from both sides — consistent with the demonstration that sparse task-specific "engram" gating materially mitigates catastrophic forgetting in artificial networks ([Aguilar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21436)) and with broader evidence that sparse activation limits interference between tasks ([Masse et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1723839115), [Khadkikar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01659v2)).

### 4.3 The neuromodulatory bridge

The deepest part of the integration is neither architectural nor algorithmic but **modulatory**: both frameworks assign pivotal roles to the same broadcast signals, and EPC-Net exploits this overlap to make each system regulate the other. Precision weighting — the gain applied to prediction-error units — is the mechanism by which predictive coding implements attention and uncertainty, and its leading biological candidate is cholinergic gain on superficial pyramidal cells, supported both by computational modelling with pharmacological challenge in humans and by direct cholinergic manipulation in auditory cortex ([Moran et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4745-12.2013), [Pérez-González et al. 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/91475)). Engram research independently shows that allocation and linking are governed by neuromodulated excitability and that emotionally salient or surprising events — which drive large prediction errors — are preferentially encoded ([Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)). In EPC-Net these are literally the same variable: the precision signal that tells the hierarchy how much to trust its errors also tells the engram system whether to open a write, giving the unified model a single, biologically grounded "interestingness" currency.

The dopamine third factor plays the corresponding role on the output side. Supervised learning requires the label to influence plasticity, and the biologically plausible route is an eligibility trace deposited by coincident activity and confirmed by a delayed neuromodulator — demonstrated at striatal spines for dopamine within a ~1–2 s window and at cortical synapses for noradrenaline and serotonin on timescales of seconds ([Yagishita et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255514), [Gerstner et al. 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6079224/)). EPC-Net uses this mechanism twice: to gate label binding in the index module (Phase 3) and to gate consolidation of replayed episodes (Phase 4), where internally generated label patterns substitute for external ones. The result is that the entire supervision signal of the network is a neuromodulatory event with a temporal tolerance window — no global error broadcast, no gradient transport — bringing supervised classification within the repertoire of synaptic mechanisms for which direct experimental evidence exists.

Read as a whole, the table reveals the structural feature that makes the integration economical rather than additive: most rows pair a component from one framework with a *use* that belongs to the other. The precision mechanism, for instance, sits inside the predictive-coding literature yet controls the engram subsystem's write gate, and the excitability variable sits inside the engram literature yet shapes the generalisation behaviour of the classifier. This cross-wiring is also why the model resists a common criticism of hybrid architectures — that they are two models stapled together — because removing either side removes a function the other side cannot perform: without the engram subsystem the network has no episodic trace and no protection against sequential interference, and without the predictive-coding hierarchy the engrams have no abstracted representation to store and no generative channel through which to consolidate. The plausibility claim therefore applies not just component-wise but architecturally: the *pattern of coupling* itself has a biological precedent in the reciprocal anatomy and complementary timescales of the hippocampal–neocortical loop ([McClelland et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.102.3.419), [Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28339-z)).

### 5.2 Known deviations from biology

Intellectual honesty requires listing where EPC-Net simplifies. The state/error unit split is rate-based and ignores spikes; mapping to spiking implementations is possible in principle — predictive-coding-like dynamics have been realised in spiking networks and equilibrium-propagation spiking models reach 97.6% on MNIST with STDP-type rules ([bioRxiv 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.19.726261v1.full.pdf)) — but the proposal as stated is a rate-model abstraction. The one-shot outer-product index write is a simplification of hippocampal plasticity, which involves dentate pattern separation, CA3 recurrence, and CA1 comparator structure rather than a single association matrix, and real engrams are more dynamic than a fixed ensemble: engram membership turns over within hours and inhibitory plasticity is required to maintain selectivity ([Tomé et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hipo.70032)). Finally, predictive coding's empirical status as *the* account of cortical computation remains contested — much of the physiological evidence is consistent with predictive coding without uniquely distinguishing it from alternatives — so the model should be read as a principled hypothesis, not a settled description of cortex ([Walsh et al. 2020, as reviewed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12979)).

A second class of deviations concerns scale and dynamics. The model assumes inference settles (or nearly settles) before plasticity, which in cortex would correspond to tens to hundreds of milliseconds of recurrent processing per stimulus; real-time extensions that compute errors prospectively while the network evolves — such as the neuronal least-action principle, which generalises equilibrium propagation and dendritic-error ideas to continuous time — offer a route to relax this assumption ([Senn et al. 2025](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89674v2)). Likewise, the kWTA competition is instantiated as a hard threshold rather than an explicit interneuron circuit; models of dentate gyrus show the same function emerging from mossy-cell recurrent excitation balanced by feedback inhibition, so a circuit-level instantiation exists if needed ([Myers et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2022.826278/full)). These deviations identify the natural directions for elaborating EPC-Net rather than undermining its core claims.

## 6. Relationship to Prior Work

### 6.1 The nearest neighbours

EPC-Net sits at the intersection of five research streams, summarised in the comparison table below. Supervised predictive-coding networks demonstrate the classification backbone but contain no episodic memory ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/), [Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)). Predictive-coding associative memories demonstrate that a PC network can act as a hippocampus-like store with superior pattern completion, but they do not classify and do not model allocation ([Salvatori et al. 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/1fb36c4ccf88f7e67ead155496f02338-Paper.pdf)). Memory-augmented dynamic predictive coding binds content and dynamics representations in an associative store for episodic sequence recall — the closest existing relative — but its memory is a generic associative memory without engram-specific mechanisms (allocation, excitability, linking) and it targets sequence modelling rather than classification ([Rao et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.23.497415v4.full-text)). Brain-inspired replay approaches achieve continual classification with generative replay through feedback, but with backpropagation-trained weights and no allocation mechanism ([Van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2)). Engram-gated artificial networks demonstrate the machine-learning value of engram-style sparse allocation for continual learning, but they are trained with backpropagation and contain no predictive-coding inference ([Aguilar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21436)).

Reading the table column by column shows that EPC-Net's novelty is combinatorial rather than component-wise: every individual mechanism has been demonstrated somewhere, but no existing system holds all six properties simultaneously, and the properties interact rather than merely coexist. Engram allocation without predictive-coding inference (as in engram-gated networks trained by backpropagation) stores whatever features the loss happens to produce, whereas in EPC-Net allocation operates on inference equilibria that have already integrated top-down context; replay without an engram-structured index (as in brain-inspired replay) samples from an unstructured generative prior, whereas in EPC-Net replay cues carry salience and temporal-linking structure that decides *what* is worth consolidating. The closest conceptual relative remains the memory-augmented dynamic predictive-coding model, and the honest statement of EPC-Net's contribution relative to it is the substitution of a biologically detailed allocation system — excitability-biased competition, interneuron-enforced sparsity, decaying linking windows — for a generic associative memory, together with the explicit classification objective that turns the whole system into a learning machine rather than a recall device ([Rao et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.23.497415v4.full-text), [Rashid et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0594)).

### 6.2 Evidence that the components work at scale

The feasibility of the predictive-coding substrate is no longer in doubt: systematic benchmarks show predictive-coding training reaching backprop-comparable accuracy on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, CIFAR-10/100, and Tiny-ImageNet with convolutional architectures, with careful attention to hyperparameters ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)). Figure 4 reproduces representative numbers from the most recent bidirectional predictive-coding study: on VGG-5/CIFAR-10 the bidirectional PC model scores 87.3% against 87.8% for the matched backpropagation model; on VGG-16/Tiny-ImageNet the PC variants actually exceed backpropagation (50.3% vs 47.5%), and the bidirectional model classifies over 60% more accurately than discriminative models when half of the input pixels are missing — direct evidence that the generative pathway EPC-Net relies on for replay also delivers robustness during inference ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/6164b6e5352c139e9ddc1a98c09e4e4a-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

Figure 4. Test accuracy of predictive-coding variants versus matched backpropagation baselines on three image-classification benchmarks (data from the Bidirectional Predictive Coding study, ICLR 2026).

On the memory side, the strongest existence proof is that predictive-coding networks used purely as associative stores already outperform modern Hopfield networks and backpropagation-trained autoencoders on pattern completion, including completing natural images from tiny fractions of visible pixels, and that their top level behaves like a hippocampal index ([Salvatori et al. 2021](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/1fb36c4ccf88f7e67ead155496f02338-Paper.pdf)). EPC-Net's engram ensembles differ from that line by adding the *allocation* layer of realism — competitive, excitability-biased, sparsity-constrained selection — which is what buys the temporal linking structure and the constant-capacity guarantees documented biologically ([Rashid et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0594), [Morrison et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nlm.2016.07.007)). The proposal is thus best understood as taking the hippocampal-memory insight from the predictive-coding-memory literature and re-grounding it in the much richer mechanistic detail of the engram literature, while keeping classification as the task that drives representation learning.

## 7. Predictions and Evaluation Plan

### 7.1 Machine-learning benchmarks and ablations

The empirical programme has four tiers, each isolating a different claim. **Tier 1 — baseline classification**: EPC-Net with the engram subsystem disabled must reproduce standard supervised predictive-coding performance on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and CIFAR-10, confirming the backbone ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)). **Tier 2 — few-shot classification**: with the engram subsystem enabled, the model should classify a novel class from a single labelled example via index completion, and accuracy after k shots should dominate the backbone-only curve for small k, converging as consolidation transfers knowledge into weights. **Tier 3 — class-incremental continual learning**: sequential introduction of classes with no data retention; the predictions are (a) engram-enabled EPC-Net without replay shows modest forgetting (sparse codes limit interference), (b) replay-enabled EPC-Net shows strongly reduced forgetting, and (c) removing precision gating during replay degrades consolidation — each ablation mapping to a specific biological mechanism, paralleling lesion experiments in which blocking specific plasticity components selectively destroys engram maturation or selectivity ([Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28339-z)). **Tier 4 — robustness**: classification with occluded or noisy inputs should degrade gracefully because the generative pathway completes missing evidence, as demonstrated for bidirectional predictive coding ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/6164b6e5352c139e9ddc1a98c09e4e4a-Paper-Conference.pdf)).

| Experiment | Manipulation | Predicted outcome | Biological analogue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Few-shot learning | 1–5 shots per novel class | Immediate above-chance accuracy via index route; dominance fades with consolidation | Hippocampal dependence of recent memory |
| Continual learning | Sequential class introduction, no replay | Sparse codes limit but do not eliminate forgetting | Interference without consolidation |
| Continual learning | + salience-weighted replay | Strong retention of early classes; surprising episodes replayed more | SWS replay and consolidation ([Van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2)) |
| Replay gating | Sensory precision left ON during replay | Reinstated representations corrupted; consolidation fails | Replay protection mechanism ([Tang et al. 2023](https://openreview.net/pdf/53eef03fb906568de161b516705d06871678dc6c.pdf)) |
| Engram lesion | Silence a specific engram ensemble | Selective loss of the associated class memory, sparing others | Optogenetic engram silencing ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325)) |
| Excitability ablation | β = 0 (no excitability bias) | Loss of temporal linking; no graded generalisation between temporally adjacent classes | CREB/excitability manipulation ([Han et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1139438)) |
| Sparsity sweep | Vary ρ | Capacity–interference trade-off curve with an optimum at low ρ | Fixed engram sparsity ([Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/)) |
| Occlusion robustness | 25–50% missing pixels | Graceful degradation via generative completion | Bidirectional PC evidence ([ICLR 2026](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/6164b6e5352c139e9ddc1a98c09e4e4a-Paper-Conference.pdf)) |

Two design principles govern the evaluation plan as a whole. First, every ablation is **paired with a biological counterpart experiment**, so that the benchmark suite doubles as a test of the model's claim to mechanism-level correspondence rather than merely functional imitation: silencing an engram ensemble in silico should reproduce the logic of optogenetic engram silencing, and removing the excitability bias should reproduce the logic of CREB or Kir2.1 manipulations that abolish allocation advantages ([Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaw4325), [Han et al. 2007](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1139438)). Second, the plan is deliberately ordered from cheapest to most expensive claims: backbone replication requires only reproducing published predictive-coding results, few-shot gains require only the index module, and full continual-learning performance additionally requires tuning the replay loop — so partial success at each tier still yields publishable, interpretable evidence about where the integration helps and where it does not. Reporting should follow the benchmarking practices established for predictive-coding networks, with multiple seeds, shared architectures across algorithms, and full hyperparameter disclosure, since the field has learned that predictive-coding results are unusually sensitive to such details ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)).

### 7.2 Neuroscientific predictions

Because the model is cast in neural terms, it also yields predictions for biology, which is where a unified theory earns its keep. First, EPC-Net predicts that **allocation probability in cortical engram experiments should correlate with the animal's surprise**: stimuli that violate learned expectations (high prediction error) should recruit engrams more readily than equally intense but predicted stimuli, an interaction between the predictive-coding and engram literatures that has not, to our knowledge, been directly tested. Second, the model predicts a **dual-signal role for acetylcholine**: the same cholinergic manipulation that increases the gain of prediction-error responses (as in auditory oddball paradigms) should also increase the probability and size of engram allocation for novel stimuli, because both are driven by precision in the model ([Pérez-González et al. 2024](https://elifesciences.org/articles/91475), [Moran et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4745-12.2013)). Third, it predicts that **linking should transfer to classification-like representations**: two object categories learned in the same temporal session should share cortical engram cells and show facilitated cross-generalisation relative to categories learned a day apart, generalising the fear-conditioning linking result to perceptual categorisation ([Rashid et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf0594)).

A fourth, more architectural prediction concerns the locus of the fast store: EPC-Net implies that the engram ensembles observed in cortical regions are not independent memory stores but one end of an index-bound system whose recall signature depends on training age — recently formed categories should be retrievable only via hippocampal engagement, while consolidated categories should survive hippocampal inactivation, reproducing in a categorisation setting the consolidation-dependent shift of engram maturation from hippocampus to cortex demonstrated in network models of hippocampal-thalamic-cortical interaction ([Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28339-z)) and the indexing logic of Teyler and DiScenna ([Teyler & DiScenna 1986](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3008780/)). Each of these predictions is falsifiable with existing optogenetic, pharmacological, and imaging methods, and each failure would localise the error to a specific model component — which is the most a computational proposal of this kind can reasonably offer.

## 8. Limitations and Open Questions

### 8.1 Technical risks

Three risks stand out. The first is **credit-assignment quality in depth**: predictive-coding approximations to backpropagation degrade gracefully but measurably in deeper convolutional architectures, and although variants such as incremental PC and dendritic-kernel hybrids have closed much of the gap ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)), EPC-Net adds state-dependent allocation and replay on top of an already delicate equilibrium computation, and interactions (e.g., engram feedback perturbing inference equilibria) will need careful stabilisation. The second is **replay fidelity**: generative replay quality limits consolidation quality in all replay-based continual learning, and while predictive-coding generative modelling is competitive at small scale, scaling it to natural images remains an open engineering problem ([Van de Ven et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-020-17866-2)). The third is **capacity accounting**: a fixed-sparsity engram ensemble has finite pattern capacity, and the model currently lacks a principled mechanism for engram turnover or forgetting of obsolete traces, something biology handles through ongoing addition and removal of cells from engrams ([Tomé et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/hipo.70032)).

There is also a conceptual risk worth naming: EPC-Net inherits the contested empirical status of both parent frameworks. Predictive coding's uniqueness as an account of cortical circuitry is debated ([Walsh et al. 2020, as reviewed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.12979)), and the laminar mapping of error and prediction signals is not uniformly supported across experiments. The model should therefore be evaluated primarily on its computational returns — few-shot accuracy, continual-learning retention, robustness — with neurobiological correspondence as a strong secondary desideratum rather than the primary success criterion.

### 8.2 Open questions for future work

Several extensions follow naturally. **Temporal structure**: the current model classifies static patterns; incorporating the dynamics-level representations of dynamic predictive coding would extend classification to sequences and videos, with the index module binding content and dynamics jointly ([Rao et al. 2023](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2022.06.23.497415v4.full-text)). **Spiking implementation**: recasting the equilibrium dynamics in spiking form, possibly via equilibrium-propagation-style spiking rules or the neuronal least-action framework, would test real-time operation and neuromorphic deployability ([Senn et al. 2025](https://elifesciences.org/reviewed-preprints/89674v2), [bioRxiv 2026](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.05.19.726261v1.full.pdf)). **Structured excitability**: making the excitability field spatially or feature-structurally correlated (rather than per-unit independent) would allow linking to follow semantic as well as temporal similarity, bringing the model closer to schemas and knowledge organisation — the role allocation is argued to play in structuring knowledge in the brain ([Josselyn & Frankland 2018](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-neuro-080317-061956)).

The broadest open question is whether the brain actually couples its predictive machinery and its memory-allocation machinery through the shared neuromodulatory variables EPC-Net posits. The constituent findings — cholinergic precision gating, excitability-biased allocation, dopamine-gated label-like signals, replay-driven consolidation — are each independently supported ([Moran et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.4745-12.2013), [Rao-Ruiz et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9730430/), [Yagishita et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1255514), [Nature Communications 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-28339-z)), but their conjunction is a hypothesis. If that conjunction fails empirically, the model still stands as an engineering design — a classification network with one-shot memory and continual learning built from local rules — but its claim to biological plausibility would need to be renegotiated.

---

## 9. Conclusion

EPC-Net demonstrates that engram theory and predictive coding are not merely compatible but **complementary in a way that borders on the necessary**: predictive coding supplies the inference and local credit assignment that engram theory presupposes but never specifies, while engrams supply the fast, sparse, one-shot episodic storage and anti-interference machinery that weight-based predictive learners demonstrably lack ([Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5467749/), [Fontaine & Alexandre 2025](https://hal.science/hal-05202850v1/file/main.pdf)). The integration is not a collage but a set of specific functional couplings: prediction error triggers allocation; converged inference states are what engrams store; the generative pathway that predictive coding requires for perception is the same pathway that engram replay uses for consolidation; and the same neuromodulators implement precision, plasticity gating, and allocation bias in one stroke. The result is a single network that classifies through iterative inference, learns from one example, retains old classes while learning new ones, and completes corrupted inputs — using only mechanisms with identified neural correlates.

The proposal is deliberately stated at a level of specificity that invites refutation: its equations can be implemented and benchmarked against the now-standard predictive-coding classification results ([Pinchetti et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01163v1)), and its neuroscientific predictions — surprise-gated allocation, dual-role acetylcholine, temporally linked category representations, consolidation-dependent shifts in recall locus — are testable with existing experimental methods. Whether EPC-Net ultimately describes the brain matters less, in the near term, than whether it sharpens both fields: it gives engram research a computational account of what engrams encode and when they form, and it gives predictive coding a solution to episodic memory and continual learning that does not compromise its claim to biological plausibility.

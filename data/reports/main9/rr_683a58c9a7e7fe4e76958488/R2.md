# Dynamic Synergy of Engram Allocation and Predictive Coding: A Biologically Plausible Cortical Network for Classification

Sensory processing and memory consolidation have traditionally been studied as distinct cortical functions. Hierarchical predictive coding conceptualizes the neocortex as a Bayesian inference engine that continually projects top-down predictions to suppress bottom-up sensory prediction errors[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). Conversely, engram theory posits that specific experiences are instantiated as sparse, physically durable neuronal ensembles formed through activity-dependent synaptic plasticity and variations in intrinsic neuronal excitability[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/).

Integrating these paradigms resolves key computational limitations inherent to each framework when operated independently. Standard predictive coding models suffer from substantial inference latencies because they rely on continuous, multi-layer iterative error minimization even when encountering highly familiar stimuli[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). Classical engram attractor networks achieve rapid pattern completion but lack a dynamic mechanism to modulate memory encoding based on prediction mismatch or sensory noise[[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7843437/).

A unified neurocomputational architecture embeds engram allocation dynamics—specifically intrinsic excitability priming, synaptic tagging and capture (STC), and assembly-specific inhibitory gating—into a canonical predictive coding microcircuit[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). In this architecture, prediction error signals serve as the primary catalyst for intrinsic priming and synaptic tagging, while consolidated engram ensembles function as deep-layer generative priors that immediately suppress prediction errors during classification[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

## Laminar Microcircuit Architecture

The proposed network implements a canonical cortical microcircuit organized across granular, supragranular, and infragranular layers[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). The microcircuit establishes bidirectional message passing between bottom-up sensory feature feeds and top-down generative priors[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

Layer 4 spiny stellate neurons act as the primary receiver for feedforward sensory inputs, relaying feature vectors directly to supragranular Layer 2/3[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). Layer 2/3 houses two distinct populations of pyramidal neurons: positive prediction error units ($e^+$) and negative prediction error units ($e^-$)[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). These error units continuously evaluate mismatch between Layer 4 sensory afferents and top-down predictions projected from infragranular layers[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). The computational precision and dynamic gain of Layer 2/3 error units are modulated by local parvalbumin-positive ($PV^+$) interneurons and intrinsic membrane conductances[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233768010_Canonical_Microcircuits_for_Predictive_Coding).

Layer 5/6 contains deep pyramidal cells organized into sparse, recurrently interconnected ensembles that represent physical engram traces[[5]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9623596/). These deep assemblies store categorical class priors within dense recurrent collateral networks[[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7843437/). Layer 5/6 engram units project feedback predictions to Layer 2/3 to cancel matching bottom-up inputs and send feedforward projections to higher-order association areas or decision readouts[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

Microcircuit stability and pattern separation are enforced by three distinct inhibitory interneuron populations[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/):

- Parvalbumin-positive ($PV^+$) interneurons provide fast perisomatic feedforward and feedback inhibition, establishing global excitation-inhibition balance and regulating precision weighting[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).
- Somatostatin-positive ($SOM^+$) interneurons target the distal dendrites of pyramidal neurons, supplying feature-selective gating and preventing run-away excitation during engram pattern completion[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/).
- Vasoactive intestinal peptide-positive ($VIP^+$) interneurons selectively inhibit $SOM^+$ interneurons, generating a local disinhibitory window during elevated error spikes that permits new engram allocation[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233768010_Canonical_Microcircuits_for_Predictive_Coding).

## Mathematical Formulation of Fast-Scale Dynamics

The fast-scale dynamics of the network dictate perceptual inference and prediction error cancellation[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). The firing rate dynamics of Layer 2/3 error units and Layer 5/6 engram units operate simultaneously to minimize local variational free energy[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

Let $r_i^{(\text{L4})}$ represent the firing rate of feature $i$ in Layer 4, and let $\hat{r}_i^{(\text{L2/3})}$ represent the top-down prediction generated by Layer 5/6 engram ensembles. The positive ($e_i^+$) and negative ($e_i^-$) prediction error units in Layer 2/3 evolve according to the following equations[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/):

$$e_i^+ = \gamma_i \cdot \max \left(0, r_i^{(\text{L4})} - g\left( \sum_j W_{ij}^{\text{pred}} r_j^{(\text{L5/6})} \right) \right)$$

$$e_i^- = \gamma_i \cdot \max \left(0, g\left( \sum_j W_{ij}^{\text{pred}} r_j^{(\text{L5/6})} \right) - r_i^{(\text{L4})} \right)$$

where $g(\cdot)$ is a non-linear activation function, $W_{ij}^{\text{pred}}$ represents the top-down predictive weight matrix from Layer 5/6 unit $j$ to Layer 2/3 unit $i$, and $\gamma_i$ represents the precision parameter (inverse variance or attentional gain)[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). The net prediction error signal for feature $i$ is defined as $e_i = e_i^+ + e_i^-$.

The firing rate vector $\mathbf{r}^{(\text{L5/6})}$ of Layer 5/6 engram neurons integrates bottom-up prediction errors, internal recurrent attractor currents, and inhibitory currents[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/):

$$\tau_r \frac{d r_j^{(\text{L5/6})}}{dt} = -r_j^{(\text{L5/6})} + \sigma \left( \sum_k W_{jk}^{\text{rec}} r_k^{(\text{L5/6})} + \sum_i W_{ji}^{\text{err}} e_i - I_{\text{inh}, j} \right)$$

where $\tau_r$ is the membrane rate constant, $\sigma(\cdot)$ is a thresholded sigmoidal activation function, $W_{jk}^{\text{rec}}$ represents the recurrent engram collateral matrix, $W_{ji}^{\text{err}}$ is the feedforward matrix converting Layer 2/3 error signals into engram excitation, and $I_{\text{inh}, j}$ represents assembly-specific inhibitory current[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/).

Inference corresponds to gradient descent on the network's total precision-weighted prediction error energy $F$[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/):

$$F = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i \gamma_i \left( r_i^{(\text{L4})} - g\left( \sum_j W_{ij}^{\text{pred}} r_j^{(\text{L5/6})} \right) \right)^2 + \mathcal{R}\left(\mathbf{r}^{(\text{L5/6})}\right)$$

where $\mathcal{R}\left(\mathbf{r}^{(\text{L5/6})}\right)$ imposes an L0/L1 norm structural sparsity penalty that restricts simultaneous activation to small, highly specific cell assemblies[[5]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9623596/).

## Dual-Tiered Plasticity Mechanics: Excitability Priming and Synaptic Tagging

While fast-scale dynamics perform inference, slow-scale structural plasticity alters network topology via a dual-tiered mechanism combining Intrinsic Excitability (IE) priming with Synaptic Tagging and Capture (STC)[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/).

### Tier 1: Allocation via Intrinsic Excitability Priming

Memory allocation is non-random; neurons featuring elevated intrinsic excitability during a learning event win the competition for engram recruitment[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). Unpredicted stimuli generate sustained prediction errors ($e_i \gg 0$) that induce intracellular calcium influx, activating transcriptional cascades such as CREB and reducing leak potassium conductance ($g_{\text{leak}}$)[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/).

The dynamics of a neuron's intrinsic excitability state $a_j(t)$ evolve according to[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/):

$$\tau_a \frac{da_j}{dt} = (a_0 - a_j) + \alpha_{\text{IE}} \cdot \bar{e}_j(t) \cdot (1 - a_j)$$

where $a_0$ is baseline excitability, $\alpha_{\text{IE}}$ is the priming rate constant, and $\bar{e}_j(t) = \int_{0}^t e_j(\tau) e^{-(t-\tau)/\tau_e} d\tau$ is the exponentially weighted temporal average of the prediction error delivered to neuron $j$[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). Elevated $a_j$ lowers the action potential firing threshold $\theta_j = \theta_0 - \kappa a_j$, ensuring that excitable neurons are preferentially allocated into nascent engram assemblies[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/).

### Tier 2: Synaptic Tagging and Capture Dynamics

Synaptic modification occurs across two temporal phases: an un-consolidated early phase (e-LTP/e-LTD) and a permanent late phase (l-LTP/l-LTD)[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/). The effective weight between pre-synaptic unit $k$ and post-synaptic unit $j$ is the sum of early-phase ($w_{jk}^{\text{early}}$) and late-phase ($w_{jk}^{\text{late}}$) components[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/):

$$W_{jk} = w_{jk}^{\text{early}} + w_{jk}^{\text{late}}$$

Co-activation driven by persistent prediction errors induces early-phase weight changes and sets a transient biophysical tag $T_{jk}$[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/):

$$\frac{d w_{jk}^{\text{early}}}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\tau_e} w_{jk}^{\text{early}} + \eta_e \cdot e_j^{(\text{L2/3})} \cdot r_k^{(\text{L5/6})}$$

$$\frac{d T_{jk}}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\tau_T} T_{jk} + \lambda_T \cdot H\left( \vert{}w_{jk}^{\text{early}}\vert{} - \theta_T \right)$$

where $\tau_e$ and $\tau_T$ are decay time constants, $\eta_e$ is the early learning rate, $\lambda_T$ is the tag setting rate, $H(\cdot)$ is the Heaviside step function, and $\theta_T$ is the threshold for tag induction[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/).

When network prediction errors remain elevated over extended time windows, indicating novel inputs or persistent misclassifications, somatic translation triggers the synthesis of Plasticity-Related Proteins ($P$) shared across dendritic compartments[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/):

$$\frac{d P}{dt} = -\frac{1}{\tau_P} P + \alpha_P \cdot \Phi \left( \sum_{m,n} \vert{}w_{mn}^{\text{early}}\vert{} - \Theta_P \right)$$

where $\tau_P$ is the protein decay constant, $\alpha_P$ is the synthesis rate, and $\Phi(\cdot)$ activates when aggregate early-phase weight shifts surpass threshold $\Theta_P$[[17]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2596310/). Tagged synapses capture available proteins $P$ to consolidate early weight modifications into permanent structural weights[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/):

$$\frac{d w_{jk}^{\text{late}}}{dt} = k_c \cdot P(t) \cdot T_{jk}(t)$$

where $k_c$ is the capture coefficient[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/). Once consolidated into $w_{jk}^{\text{late}}$, engram traces are permanently embedded into Layer 5/6 recurrent matrices ($W^{\text{rec}}$) and top-down predictive matrices ($W^{\text{pred}}$)[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

### Inhibitory Plasticity and Assembly Separation

To prevent overlap and interference between distinct engrams, excitatory-to-inhibitory (E-I) connections undergo heterosynaptic plasticity[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/). Inhibitory interneurons learn feature-selective firing patterns that actively suppress competing engrams during retrieval[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/):

$$\frac{d W_{j, \text{inh}}^{\text{E-I}}}{dt} = \eta_{\text{inh}} \cdot \left( r_j^{\text{engram\_A}} \cdot r_{\text{inh}}^{\text{engram\_B}} - \rho_0 \right)$$

This assembly-specific lateral inhibition creates distinct attractor basins in Layer 5/6, promoting pattern separation and preventing co-activation of non-relevant memories during classification[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/).

## Operational Workflow: Integrated Classification and Continuous Learning

The computational execution of the unified network transitions dynamically between fast inference and slow consolidation based on prediction error magnitude[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072).

When a sensory stimulus $x$ is presented to Layer 4, feedforward currents activate Layer 2/3 error units[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). Layer 5/6 engram assemblies evaluate feedforward inputs via initial projection weights $W^{\text{err}}$[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). If the stimulus matches a previously learned category, the matching Layer 5/6 engram ensemble undergoes rapid recurrent pattern completion[[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7843437/). The activated engram projects top-down generative predictions ($W^{\text{pred}}$) back to Layer 2/3, instantly canceling bottom-up sensory drive[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). Total prediction error drops below the threshold ($E_{\text{total}} < \Theta_E$), terminating the inference loop[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). The active Layer 5/6 engram index directly provides the classification label[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). Because prediction error collapses rapidly, early plasticity is not sustained, leaving the engram unaltered and protecting it from catastrophic forgetting[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072).

If the stimulus represents a novel class, top-down predictions fail to match Layer 4 inputs, resulting in persistent, high-amplitude prediction errors ($E_{\text{total}} \gg \Theta_E$)[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). Sustained error spikes drive intracellular calcium influx in Layer 5/6, causing local $VIP^+$ disinhibition and upregulating intrinsic excitability $a_j$ via CREB activation[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). Neurons exhibiting the highest excitability win the allocation competition to form a novel assembly[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). Co-activation sets synaptic tags $T_{jk}$ across unassigned recurrent and feedforward synapses[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/). Persistent error signals trigger protein synthesis ($P$), prompting tagged synapses to capture PRPs and consolidate early modifications into permanent late-phase weights $w^{\text{late}}$[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/). Heterosynaptic E-I plasticity simultaneously strengthens targeted lateral inhibition onto competing engrams, establishing a new category representation[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/).

## Comparative Systems Analysis

Combining engram dynamic mechanics with predictive coding addresses several operational trade-offs present in alternative computational models[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

| **Spike Compute Energy** | High: Sustained prediction error spiking across all sensory exposures[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). | Low during Recall: Spiking is concentrated in active attractors. | Efficient: Spiking collapses rapidly as engram predictions cancel sensory errors[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). |

Standard predictive coding architectures optimize continuous internal models via gradient descent but suffer from high computational latencies and catastrophic forgetting during sequential classification tasks[[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6400266/). Because every stimulus updates global weight matrices, old representations are progressively degraded[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072).

Classical attractor networks achieve rapid pattern completion and low recall latency, but they lack adaptive mechanisms to control memory allocation[[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7843437/). They cannot distinguish between persistent structural novelties and transient sensory noise, rendering them vulnerable to spurious attractor states[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/).

The integrated model resolves these trade-offs by utilizing prediction errors as an informational filter[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). When inputs match stored engrams, rapid top-down predictions cancel prediction errors, bypassing expensive iterative optimization and locking weight matrices against overwriting[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). When unpredicted inputs generate sustained errors, the network engages intrinsic excitability priming and STC consolidation, allocating a dedicated engram assembly without altering previously consolidated memories[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/).

## Emergent Computational Insights and Future Directions

Integrating engram allocation dynamics into a predictive coding microcircuit produces emergent computational properties that extend beyond the capabilities of either framework individually[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).

Precision-weighted prediction errors ($\gamma_i \cdot e_i$) act as an adaptive filter for memory formation[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233768010_Canonical_Microcircuits_for_Predictive_Coding). When input variations are unstructured or noisy, local $PV^+$ interneurons increase inhibitory tone, lowering precision $\gamma_i$[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233768010_Canonical_Microcircuits_for_Predictive_Coding). Low-precision errors fail to reach the threshold ($\Theta_P$) required for PRP synthesis and synaptic capture, preventing transient sensory noise from polluting long-term memory[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7977149/). Conversely, structured category novelties generate high-precision errors that exceed $\Theta_P$, driving targeted CREB upregulation, intrinsic excitability priming, and STC consolidation[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). Precision weighting ensures that long-term engrams are allocated exclusively for structurally meaningful information[[2]](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233768010_Canonical_Microcircuits_for_Predictive_Coding).

This dual architecture addresses the stability-plasticity dilemma through error-gated operational modes[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). Familiar inputs activate the "exploit" mode, where Layer 5/6 engrams generate top-down predictions that rapidly reduce prediction errors ($e_i \to 0$)[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). Early-phase weight shifts decay without triggering protein synthesis ($d w^{\text{early}}/dt \approx 0$), preserving consolidated structural weights[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072). Novel inputs activate the "explore" mode, where persistent prediction errors ($e_i \gg 0$) elevate intrinsic excitability in unassigned Layer 5/6 neurons, setting synaptic tags $T_{jk}$ and triggering PRP synthesis to consolidate a new category representation[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). The network autonomously manages continuous learning without requiring external task-switching signals[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072).

Heterosynaptic E-I plasticity enforces sharp pattern separation in Layer 5/6[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/). When Engram A is activated, its associated $SOM^+$ and $PV^+$ interneurons selectively suppress neurons belonging to Engram B[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/). This prevents co-activation of overlapping representations during pattern completion and maintains category precision[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/). Chemogenetic or optogenetic suppression during the excitability window prevents cross-talk and reallocation, ensuring memory context specificity[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/).

The model yields several testable experimental predictions:

- Optogenetic inhibition of Layer 2/3 error units during exposure to a novel visual category will block CREB upregulation and intrinsic excitability priming in Layer 5/6, preventing the formation of a stable engram for that category[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).
- Pharmacological inhibition of protein synthesis in Layer 5/6 will preserve immediate prediction-error cancellation during initial exposure, but will block late-phase STC consolidation, causing the circuit to repeatedly re-process familiar inputs as novel errors in subsequent testing sessions[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/).
- Cell-type-specific knockout of heterosynaptic E-I plasticity in $SOM^+$ interneurons will eliminate assembly-specific lateral inhibition, resulting in multi-engram co-activation in Layer 5/6 and severe classification degradation for visually similar stimulus classes[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244581/).

## Conclusion

The integrated neurocomputational model presented here bridges hierarchical predictive coding and engram theory, establishing a unified framework for perceptual inference and memory formation[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). By employing prediction error signals as the dynamic trigger for intrinsic excitability priming and synaptic tagging, the network restricts structural consolidation to genuinely novel information[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12783673/). Concurrently, utilizing consolidated engrams as deep-layer generative priors enables immediate error cancellation during pattern completion, dramatically reducing computational latency and energy consumption for familiar inputs[[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3777738/). This dual-tier design provides a biologically plausible solution to the stability-plasticity dilemma, offering a functional blueprint for continuous learning architectures and advanced neuro-inspired classification systems[[7]](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202509.1072).

# End-of-Year Banquet Plan — Student Group, Berkeley, CA

**Event:** End-of-year banquet · **Guests:** ~30 · **Budget:** $2,500 target / $3,000 hard cap (transportation excluded)
**Recommended venue:** Tamalpais Room, David Brower Center, 2150 Allston Way, Berkeley, CA 94704
**Recommended caterer:** Berkeley Catering (UC Berkeley / Cal Dining catering) — "Nuevo Latino" buffet with modifications
**Projected total:** **$2,447** — under the $2,500 target, with $553 of headroom to the hard cap

---

## 1. Executive Summary

This plan pairs a **specific venue** — the **Tamalpais Room at the David Brower Center** in Downtown Berkeley — with a **specific catered menu** — the **Nuevo Latino buffet from Berkeley Catering**, modified for your group's dietary restrictions. The Tamalpais Room holds up to 120 guests (comfortably fitting 30 for a banquet), sits one block from the Downtown Berkeley BART station, includes banquet tables and padded chairs at no charge, and every event space in the building is equipped with a projection screen ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)). The university/college half-day rate of **$960** fits the budget, and the building is a LEED Platinum landmark that reads as a genuinely "nice" venue for an end-of-year celebration rather than a generic meeting room.

The menu choice is driven by your allergy list. Berkeley Catering publishes its full menu with **per-dish icons for all Big-9 allergens** — eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, shellfish, and more — which makes it uniquely auditable for a group carrying two egg allergies, two peanut allergies, a combined dairy/tree-nut/shellfish allergy, and one vegetarian ([Berkeley Catering Menu](https://dining.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Berkeley-Catering-Menu.pdf)). The Nuevo Latino buffet ($30.75/person) is naturally free of peanuts and shellfish; its dairy components (cheddar, sour cream) are served as separate toppings; and the spiced tofu fajita option covers the vegetarian guest within the same line. The one hazardous item — Mexican wedding cookies, which contain tree nuts — is swapped for a seasonal fruit dessert. The total projected cost of **$2,447** includes venue, catering for 30, estimated sales tax, the required AV technician, linens, and decor.

Two caveats are flagged throughout: Brower Center requires caterers outside its partner list to obtain prior approval (with possible fees), and the exact AV technician fee must be confirmed at booking — $250 is budgeted as a conservative estimate. Section 7 lists fallback options for both.

## 2. Event Requirements and Planning Assumptions

The requirements stated for this banquet are: a nice venue near Berkeley, CA, with a projector for slides; roughly 30 attendees; catered food accommodating **2 egg allergies, 2 peanut allergies, 1 guest allergic to dairy + nuts + shellfish, and 1 vegetarian**; and a total budget of $2,500 with a hard cap of $3,000, excluding transportation. Because no date, cuisine, format, or venue-style preference was given, this plan assumes a **weekday-evening dinner banquet of about four hours** (setup plus a 2–2.5 hour event with a slide presentation), a **buffet format**, and a menu chosen primarily for allergen safety and broad appeal. Buffet service is the recommended format for mixed-restriction groups because clearly labeled dishes let each guest self-navigate, whereas a single fixed-protein plated menu forces a custom plate for every restricted guest and raises service complexity and cost ([Dining Rated](https://diningrated.com/guides/food-allergy-catering-guide/)).

Two assumptions materially affect pricing and are worth stating explicitly. First, the group is treated as a **university-affiliated student group** for venue pricing purposes — the Brower Center publishes a dedicated "Colleges & Universities" rate tier, and the figures below use it ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)). If your group is a UC Berkeley Registered Student Organization (RSO), an on-campus alternative (Section 3.3) may cut the venue line to near zero. Second, the group is treated as a **non-university paying client** for catering purposes, meaning Berkeley city sales tax (~10.25%, estimated) applies to food; UC departments paying by chartstring are exempt from sales tax ([Berkeley Catering Menu](https://dining.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Berkeley-Catering-Menu.pdf)). If your student organization can pay through a campus chartstring, the tax line disappears and roughly $95 returns to the budget.

## 3. Venue: Tamalpais Room, David Brower Center

### 3.1 The space and why it fits

The David Brower Center at 2150 Allston Way is a LEED Platinum-rated events building in Downtown Berkeley, one block from the Downtown Berkeley BART station and multiple bus lines, with the City of Berkeley's Oxford Garage directly underneath the building (entrance at 2161 Kittredge) ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/rentals/)). The **Tamalpais Room** on the second floor — served by an ADA-compliant elevator — seats up to 120 and is sized well for a 30-person banquet: large enough for a presentation screen, buffet line, and rounds of 8–10, without the cavernous feel of a ballroom built for hundreds ([David Brower Center](https://www.eventective.com/berkeley-ca/david-brower-center-627798.html)). The room also adjoins the Terrace, which works as a pre-dinner reception space in good weather.

On the projector requirement specifically: **projection screens are installed in all Brower Center event spaces**, and the building provides sound reinforcement by Meyer Sound. Two categories of AV support are relevant — access to the sound/projection/lighting systems requires a **house technician** for setup and breakdown (and, for some rentals, operation during the event), while furniture and basic supplies come at no additional charge: 29 rectangular banquet tables, 15 sixty-inch round banquet tables, 5 cocktail tables, 225 padded chairs, lecterns, coat racks, and whiteboards ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)). Note that the Brower Center **does not provide linens**, so a small linen line is included in the budget.

### 3.2 Rates, policies, and booking logistics

Published rates for the Tamalpais Room are: **Colleges & Universities — Quarter Day $590 / Half Day $960 / Full Day $1,460**; nonprofit rates are $580/$955/$1,430 respectively ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)). This plan budgets the **half-day ($960)**, which covers an evening banquet with setup and breakdown. The Center's staff handle room set-up and take-down of tables and chairs, and a House Manager is on site throughout the event ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/event-room/tamalpais-room/)). Payment terms: a 50% booking deposit is due at contract signing and the balance one month before the event; cancellations more than 90 days out are fully refunded, 30–90 days forfeit the deposit, and under 30 days forfeit the full rental fee ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/rentals/)).

One policy matters for the catering plan: the Brower Center maintains a list of approved **Catering & Event Partners**, and caterers not on the list "are subject to our prior approval and additional fees may apply" ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/catering-event-partners/)). Berkeley Catering — this plan's first-choice caterer for allergen-labeling reasons — should be cleared with the Brower Center events team (events@browercenter.org, 510-809-0900) before signing; if approval carries a fee or is declined, Section 4.4 provides an in-house fallback (Gather, the restaurant located inside the Brower Center) and other listed partners. Alcohol note: the Brower Center permits alcohol service through partner caterers with bartenders; this plan assumes a **no-alcohol** student event, which also avoids permit and staffing costs — consistent with city facility rules that prohibit alcohol at many public venues in Berkeley ([City of Berkeley](https://berkeleyca.gov/city-services/event-permits-rentals/event-and-facility-rentals)).

If you prefer staffed full service rather than drop-off, request a quote when booking and expect the total to rise accordingly; the budget in Section 5 assumes drop-off with light self-service, which suits a casual student banquet.

### 4.2 Dietary restriction coverage map

The reason this menu wins is structural: Mexican-style build-your-own fajita service keeps allergenic ingredients physically separated rather than baked or mixed in. Every dish on Berkeley Catering's menu carries Big-9 allergen icons (eggs, peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, shellfish, fish, wheat/gluten, soy, sesame), the same labeling system used across UC Berkeley dining locations ([Berkeley Dining](https://dining.berkeley.edu/nutrition/allergies/)). The table below maps each restriction to the menu design; items marked "confirm" must be verified in writing with the caterer at booking, since recipes can change.

| Guest restriction | How the menu covers it | Action required at booking |
|---|---|---|
| **Egg allergy ×2** | No egg-based entrée or side; fajitas, beans, rice, tortillas, guacamole, pico de gallo are egg-free by ingredient list | Request chipotle dressing on the side (or swap to vinaigrette); confirm no egg in tortilla strips; dessert swapped to fruit |
| **Peanut allergy ×2** | The Nuevo Latino menu contains **no peanut items at all** — a major reason it was chosen over, e.g., Thai or Burmese menus | State both peanut allergies in the written order so the kitchen flags cross-contact |
| **Dairy + tree nuts + shellfish ×1** | Dairy is confined to side toppings (cheese, sour cream, dressing) — the guest simply skips them; **no shellfish anywhere on the menu**; the only tree-nut item (wedding cookies) is removed | Confirm vegetable tagine/baklava from other menus are not being substituted in; confirm pepitas (seeds, not tree nuts) are acceptable to this guest or omit them from a plated portion |
| **Vegetarian ×1** | Spiced local tofu fajita protein, beans, rice, all toppings, salad, fruit dessert | Ask that tofu be prepared separately from meat on the grill line |
| Everyone else | Full menu | — |

One honest limitation: like virtually all commercial kitchens, Berkeley Catering is not a dedicated allergen-free facility, and UC Berkeley Dining's own allergen page notes that manufacturer precautionary labels ("may contain") are not used to build menu allergen labels ([Berkeley Dining](https://dining.berkeley.edu/nutrition/allergies/)). For guests with severe or anaphylactic allergies, the protection comes from the written protocol in the next subsection, not from the menu alone.

### 4.3 Allergy safety protocol (do these, in writing)

Allergy communication must be **written into the catering contract or an addendum**, listing each allergy by name — verbal mention is not enough, and without written documentation you have no recourse if a restricted guest is served the wrong dish ([Dining Rated](https://diningrated.com/guides/food-allergy-catering-guide/)). Berkeley Catering's terms require a signed Banquet Event Order (BEO) at least 10 days before the event and a final guaranteed guest count by 12 p.m. seven days out — put the allergy list, the dessert substitution, the dressing-on-the-side instruction, and the separate-tofu-prep instruction **directly into the BEO** ([Berkeley Catering Menu](https://dining.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Berkeley-Catering-Menu.pdf)).

On the day of the event, three low-cost practices close the remaining risk. First, ask the caterer to deliver with **per-dish allergen labels** (their icon system makes this easy to print) and place them on the buffet line. Second, arrange the line so dairy toppings sit at the far end in their own vessels with dedicated utensils, reducing cross-contact into shared dishes. Third, seat the six restricted guests through the line first or have the organizer plate their food directly from the fresh delivery containers before general service. If any of the six guests has anaphylaxis-level sensitivity, confirm with the caterer whether a separately plated, kitchen-sealed meal can be provided for that guest — caterers typically accommodate this, sometimes with a small surcharge in the $5–$15 range per affected guest ([Dining Rated](https://diningrated.com/guides/food-allergy-catering-guide/)).

## 7. Risks and Contingencies

**Caterer approval risk.** Berkeley Catering is not published on the Brower Center's partner list, and off-list caterers require prior approval with possible fees ([David Brower Center](https://browercenter.org/catering-event-partners/)). If approval fails or the fee is material, pivot to **Gather**, the in-house partner located inside the Brower Center, which offers catering platters with marked vegan/vegetarian options and full-service capability — the menu would need to be built with Gather directly around the same allergy rules ([Gather](https://gatherberkeley.com/takeout-catering-menu)).

**AV cost risk.** The house technician fee is not published; this plan holds $250 as an allowance. If the quote comes in materially higher, ask whether your event qualifies for setup/breakdown-only technician service (rather than full-event operation), or move the slides to a large monitor. If the quote is lower, the savings flow straight to the contingency line.

**Allergy incident risk.** The residual risk after menu design is cross-contact in a shared kitchen. The mitigations — written BEO allergy list, dessert substitution, separated toppings, labeled buffet, first-pass plating for restricted guests — follow standard guidance for hosting with food allergies ([Dining Rated](https://diningrated.com/guides/food-allergy-catering-guide/)). The organizer should also confirm day-of that any guest carrying an epinephrine auto-injector has it on hand.

**Headcount risk.** The budget scales linearly at about $34 per added or removed guest ($30.75 plus tax). Berkeley Catering charges for the guaranteed number or actual attendance, whichever is greater, and additional meals above the guarantee are billed at menu price plus 15% — so lock the count at 30 and treat RSVPs as final at the seven-day mark ([Berkeley Catering Menu](https://dining.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/Berkeley-Catering-Menu.pdf)).

# End-of-Year Banquet Plan for a 30-Person Student Group Near Berkeley, California

**Assumed planning date:** October 10, 2026  
**Recommended event window:** early–mid December 2026, preferably a weeknight or Sunday evening to control venue cost  
**Target budget:** $2,500  
**Hard cap:** $3,000  
**Recommended plan:** Book **The Hillside Club in Berkeley** as the private banquet venue, use a **rented projector/screen setup**, and order **individually boxed, allergen-managed meals from Curry Up Now’s East Bay catering program**.

---

## 1. Executive Recommendation

For a student-group banquet of approximately 30 people, the best overall plan is to use **The Hillside Club** as the venue and **Curry Up Now** as the caterer. The Hillside Club is a strong venue choice because it is a dedicated event space in Berkeley, can handle seated dining well above 30 guests, accepts reservations up to 24 months in advance, and has clear deposit and cancellation policies. Its seated-dining capacity is listed at up to 120 people, so a 30-person banquet will fit comfortably without feeling logistically constrained ([The Hillside Club, n.d.](https://www.hillsideclub.org/faqs)).

The main caveat is that the provided Hillside Club information does **not** explicitly list an in-house projector. Therefore, the plan should treat the projector as an event requirement to be added through a venue-approved AV rental or student-group equipment source. This is still preferable to using UC Berkeley classrooms because several attractive classroom-style options either restrict reservations to department affiliates or prohibit food. For example, UC Berkeley Math’s Evans 736 has the exact 30-person capacity and an LCD projector, but it is limited to Department of Mathematics affiliates and food is not permitted in Math department spaces ([UC Berkeley Department of Mathematics, n.d.-a](https://math.berkeley.edu/resources/rooms/math-evans-seminar-736); [UC Berkeley Department of Mathematics, n.d.-b](https://math.berkeley.edu/resources/rooms)). Rhetoric/Film & Media rooms in Dwinelle also prohibit food and drink, making them poor choices for a banquet even if their capacities are suitable ([UC Berkeley Department of Rhetoric, n.d.](https://rhetoric.berkeley.edu/room-reservations)).

For catering, the safest and most budget-efficient recommendation is to order **individually boxed meals** from Curry Up Now rather than a shared buffet. Curry Up Now supports individual boxed meals for approximately 10–500 guests, serves Berkeley and the East Bay, offers vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, halal, keto-friendly, and nut-free-available options, and states that boxed meals start around $15 per person for 15–50 guests ([Curry Up Now, n.d.](https://www.curryupnow.com/catering-locations/oakland)). Because the allergy list includes egg, peanut, dairy/milk, tree nuts, and shellfish, individually labeled boxes are safer than a buffet and reduce the risk of serving-utensil cross-contact.

## 2. Event Requirements and Planning Assumptions

### 2.1 Core Requirements

| Requirement | Planning Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Attendance | Approximately 30 people |
| Location | Nice venue near Berkeley, California |
| Presentation need | Projector or equivalent slide-display setup |
| Food | Catered meal |
| Dietary restrictions | 2 egg allergies; 2 peanut allergies; 1 dairy + nuts + shellfish allergy; 1 vegetarian |
| Budget | $2,500 target; $3,000 hard cap |
| Transportation | Excluded from budget |
| Student-group context | Must consider RSO responsibilities, campus rules if on campus, and third-party liability |

The dietary restrictions should be treated as serious allergen constraints, not simple preferences. Food Allergy Research & Education identifies milk, egg, peanut, tree nuts, fish, crustacean shellfish, wheat, soy, and sesame as major U.S. allergens, with sesame added as the ninth major allergen in 2021 ([FARE, n.d.-a](https://www.foodallergy.org/resources/facts-and-statistics)). The banquet’s known restrictions include several of these major allergens: egg, peanut, milk/dairy, tree nuts, and shellfish.

### 2.2 Recommended Food Strategy

The cleanest strategy is to make the **entire ordered meal egg-free, peanut-free, tree-nut-free, dairy-free, and shellfish-free**, while also including vegetarian/vegan meals as the default. This avoids the risk of accidentally giving one guest the wrong meal and reduces cross-contact at the event. A fully vegan, nut-free, dairy-free, egg-free, shellfish-free boxed-meal approach also automatically accommodates the vegetarian attendee.

This does not guarantee a medically allergen-free event, because most caterers operate shared kitchens. Curry Up Now itself notes that cross-contamination cannot be fully guaranteed, even though nut-free options are available and allergen details are available ([Curry Up Now, n.d.](https://www.curryupnow.com/catering-locations/oakland)). Therefore, the group should obtain written confirmation of ingredients and cross-contact practices before finalizing the order.

## 3. Venue Analysis

### 3.1 Why Not a UC Berkeley Classroom?

UC Berkeley classroom and department-room options are attractive because they often include AV, are close to students, and may be inexpensive or accessible to Registered Student Organizations. However, they are generally not ideal for a banquet.

| Venue Option | Advantages | Problems for This Event | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Math Evans 736 | Exact capacity of 30; LCD projector; chalkboards; HDMI/VGA | Reservations limited to Department of Mathematics affiliates; Math spaces prohibit food | Do not use unless the group is Math-affiliated and food is held elsewhere |
| Rhetoric/Film & Media Dwinelle rooms | Capacities include 20, 22, 49, and 84; student groups can reserve for a fee | Food/drink prohibited; tech support generally not provided | Not suitable for banquet |
| General UC Berkeley Event Services spaces | RSOs can reserve campus spaces; Event Services can help with catering/equipment | Campus policies, deadlines, possible event approvals; specific capacity/AV not established in supplied data | Possible fallback, not primary |
| Cal Performances venues | Professional venues; strong AV environment | Too large for 30 people; likely cost-inefficient | Not suitable |

Math Evans 736 is the only cited room with an exact 30-person capacity and built-in LCD projector, but its reservation eligibility and food prohibition make it a poor fit for a banquet ([UC Berkeley Department of Mathematics, n.d.-a](https://math.berkeley.edu/resources/rooms/math-evans-seminar-736); [UC Berkeley Department of Mathematics, n.d.-b](https://math.berkeley.edu/resources/rooms)). Dwinelle rooms also fail the food requirement because food and drink are prohibited ([UC Berkeley Department of Rhetoric, n.d.](https://rhetoric.berkeley.edu/room-reservations)).

### 3.2 Recommended Venue: The Hillside Club

**Recommended venue:** The Hillside Club, Berkeley, CA  
**Event format:** seated dinner banquet with short slide presentation  
**Projected attendance:** 30  
**AV plan:** rent or bring projector, screen, HDMI adapter, extension cords, and speaker if needed  
**Booking feasibility:** available to book up to 24 months in advance, so a December 2026 event is within the booking window as of October 10, 2026 ([The Hillside Club, n.d.](https://www.hillsideclub.org/faqs)).

The Hillside Club is recommended because it is a private event venue rather than a classroom or restaurant corner. It has enough capacity for the group to dine, socialize, and view slides without crowding. The venue’s FAQ states that reservations are confirmed after a signed contract and a deposit equal to 50% of total rental fees; the balance and a refundable $400 security deposit are due at least 30 days before the event ([The Hillside Club, n.d.](https://www.hillsideclub.org/faqs)). Cancellations must be made in writing at least 30 days before the event; cancellations under 30 days forfeit the full deposit, while cancellations more than 30 days out receive 75% of the deposit refunded ([The Hillside Club, n.d.](https://www.hillsideclub.org/faqs)).

### 3.3 Venue Cost Expectation

Because the provided Hillside Club source gives deposit and policy details but not a rental rate, the cost estimate should be benchmarked against Berkeley event-space market data. Berkeley party venue rentals vary widely, with Giggster reporting typical party-venue rentals around $500–$5,000 and Berkeley listings averaging around $170 per hour ([Giggster, n.d.](https://giggster.com/find/berkeley--ca/party)). Tagvenue reports a similar Berkeley average of about $166 per hour, with October 2026 hire-fee ranges around $150–$400 per hour depending on venue and amenities ([Tagvenue, n.d.](https://www.tagvenue.com/us/hire/party-venues/berkeley)).

For budgeting, assume a **5-hour venue block**: 1 hour setup, 3 hours event, 1 hour cleanup.

| Cost Scenario | Hourly Estimate | 5-Hour Venue Cost |
|---|---:|---:|
| Low market estimate | $150/hour | $750 |
| Average market estimate | $166–$170/hour | $830–$850 |
| Conservative high estimate | $300/hour | $1,500 |
| Hard-cap stress test | $400/hour | $2,000 |

The plan remains viable under the target budget if the rental is near the local average and remains under the $3,000 hard cap even under a high-but-plausible $400/hour scenario, assuming catering is kept controlled.

## 4. Catering Analysis and Recommended Menu

### 4.1 Caterer Selection

**Recommended caterer:** Curry Up Now, East Bay/Oakland catering  
**Recommended format:** individually boxed meals  
**Reasoning:** Curry Up Now serves Berkeley and the East Bay from its San Francisco Valencia Street kitchen, offers catering for 10–500 guests, supports individual boxes, party packages, and à la carte trays, and provides dietary-friendly options including vegan, vegetarian, gluten-free, and nut-free-available items ([Curry Up Now, n.d.](https://www.curryupnow.com/catering-locations/oakland)).

Curry Up Now’s individual boxed meals are more appropriate than a buffet for this event because the group has multiple allergies. Buffet service can be safe only if dishes are clearly labeled and utensils remain assigned to specific dishes; allergen-planning guidance emphasizes clear labels for allergens and dietary categories such as vegetarian, vegan, gluten-free, dairy-free, and nut-free ([Catering by Design, n.d.](https://www.catering-by-design.com/blog/how-to-plan-catering-for-dietary-requirements/)). Culinary Eye also emphasizes that serious allergies require questions about cross-contact through separate prep areas, utensils, cookware, storage, fryers, work surfaces, and serving pieces ([Culinary Eye, n.d.](https://www.culinaryeye.com/blog/catering-for-dietary-restrictions)).

### 4.2 Specific Recommended Menu

Because the known allergies overlap heavily, the safest menu is a **single unified vegan, nut-free, dairy-free, egg-free, and shellfish-free boxed-meal order**. The group should avoid peanuts, tree nuts, dairy, eggs, and shellfish across the entire order rather than isolating “special meals” for only some guests.

#### Recommended Curry Up Now Order

| Item | Quantity | Dietary Logic |
|---|---:|---|
| **Vegan, gluten-free, nut-free rice bowl boxes** | 30 | Default meal for all attendees; vegetarian-compatible; avoids egg, dairy, peanuts, tree nuts, and shellfish if confirmed by caterer |
| **Individually labeled boxes** | 30 labels | Prevents mix-ups and reduces buffet cross-contact |
| **No naan unless allergen-reviewed** | 0 by default | Naan often contains wheat and may contain dairy; unnecessary for stated needs |
| **No dessert unless allergen-reviewed** | 0 by default | Desserts often contain egg, dairy, wheat, or nuts |
| **Sealed bottled or canned nonalcoholic drinks** | 30–36 | Avoids alcohol-policy complications and keeps service simple |
| **Disposable plates, napkins, utensils** | included/confirmed | Curry Up Now states buffet orders include setup items; boxed orders should be confirmed |

Curry Up Now states that individual boxed meals start around $15 per person for 15–50 guests, while buffet pricing depends on menu and count ([Curry Up Now, n.d.](https://www.curryupnow.com/catering-locations/oakland)). For budgeting, use a conservative estimate of **$22–$25 per person** to include dietary handling, delivery, taxes, service, and beverages.

### 4.3 Allergen-Control Instructions to Send the Caterer

The student group should send the following written requirement to Curry Up Now before paying the deposit:

> “We need 30 individually boxed meals. The entire order must be vegetarian/vegan and must avoid egg, peanuts, tree nuts, dairy/milk, and shellfish. One guest has a combined dairy + nuts + shellfish allergy, two guests have egg allergies, and two guests have peanut allergies. Please confirm ingredients in writing and explain whether any cross-contact risk exists through shared prep surfaces, utensils, fryers, storage, or serving tools.”

This is important because ingredient lists alone are not enough for serious allergies. Event-catering best practice is to ask caterers how they prevent cross-contact through separate prep areas, utensils, cookware, storage, fryers, work surfaces, and serving pieces ([Culinary Eye, n.d.](https://www.culinaryeye.com/blog/catering-for-dietary-restrictions)).

## 6. Student-Group Compliance Considerations

If the banquet is held off campus at The Hillside Club, the UC Berkeley Major Event Policy is less likely to control the event. However, student-group officers should still follow RSO financial and conduct responsibilities. UC Berkeley states that RSO signatories are responsible for ensuring the organization follows university policies, uses spaces responsibly, pays university or third-party charges, obtains required permits, and meets deadlines ([UC Berkeley Student Leadership & Engagement, n.d.-a](https://lead.berkeley.edu/student-orgs/manage-your-org/)).

If the group considers moving the event onto campus, it should consult ASUC Student Union Event Services early. ASUC Event Services can help RSOs with venues, catering, decorations, equipment, and event-planning guidance, with some services free for student organizations ([UC Berkeley Student Leadership & Engagement, n.d.-b](https://lead.berkeley.edu/student-orgs/manage-your-org/plan-events-for-your-org/)). A 30-person banquet without alcohol, outdoor amplified sound, or a dance format is unlikely to meet the 300-person Major Event threshold, but alcohol would trigger additional requirements under campus policy ([UC Berkeley Event Services, n.d.-a](https://eventservices.berkeley.edu/campus-major-events-policy-update/)).

For this event, the objective recommendation is: **do not serve alcohol**. Alcohol introduces policy complexity, possible insurance issues, and extra cost. It is not needed for an end-of-year student-group banquet.

1. Select the event date.
2. Sign venue contract only if the venue cost keeps the event below the hard cap.
3. Pay the 50% venue deposit.
4. Place a tentative catering order with allergen requirements in writing.
5. Assign one student officer as food-allergy lead.

### Three Weeks Before Event

1. Confirm final headcount.
2. Reconfirm allergy list with attendees.
3. Send final dietary count to Curry Up Now.
4. Request written ingredient/allergen confirmation.
5. Create printed labels: “Vegan,” “No egg,” “No dairy,” “No peanuts/tree nuts,” “No shellfish,” and “Prepared in a shared kitchen—cross-contact cannot be guaranteed.”

### One Week Before Event

1. Confirm AV delivery/pickup.
2. Confirm catering delivery time.
3. Prepare slide deck and test HDMI connection.
4. Print seating chart or meal-label list.
5. Confirm venue setup and cleanup responsibilities.

### Day of Event

1. Arrive one hour early.
2. Set up projector and test slides.
3. Create a clearly marked food pickup area.
4. Keep boxed meals closed until distribution.
5. Do not transfer food to shared platters.
6. Keep ingredient/allergen notes available for guests.

## 8. Final Judgment

The best concrete plan is to hold the banquet at **The Hillside Club**, add a **rented projector/screen package**, and serve **Curry Up Now individually boxed vegan, nut-free, dairy-free, egg-free, and shellfish-free rice bowl meals**. This plan is stronger than a UC Berkeley classroom plan because it permits a real banquet environment, avoids classroom food prohibitions, and gives the group more control over catering and setup. It is also stronger than a restaurant-private-room plan because restaurant rooms may restrict outside catering and may not provide reliable allergen control for a customized student-group menu.

The main operational risks are venue rental price, outside-catering permission, and allergen cross-contact. Those risks are manageable if the group follows the decision rule not to sign a venue contract above $1,250 without rebalancing the budget, obtains written allergen confirmation from Curry Up Now, and uses individually boxed meals instead of buffet service. Under the working budget, the event should cost approximately **$2,375 after the refundable deposit is returned**, with a temporary cash-flow need around **$2,775**, keeping the plan within the $3,000 hard cap.

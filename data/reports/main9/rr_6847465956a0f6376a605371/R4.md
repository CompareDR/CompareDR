# Relocating from San Francisco on $120k: Top 3 Cities

**Bottom line.** My ranked list is:

1. **Salt Lake City, Utah** — the strongest verified cost position, the closest healthcare spending to your 30% target, and the best mountain access. It is the only finalist that clears a rent-*excluded* cost-of-living index, but its citywide Walk Score is 59, so neighborhood choice is essential.
2. **Atlanta, Georgia** — the best airport, the deepest walkable-neighborhood stock, and the strongest diversity and amenity base. Cost is 30.6% below San Francisco on a rent-inclusive basis, but healthcare is only 15.0% below California.
3. **Charlotte, North Carolina** — the lowest income tax, the strongest median internet speeds, and an American Airlines hub minutes from downtown. Healthcare is the weakest of the three at 13.4% below California.

**The healthcare caveat that shapes everything.** A literal “30% cheaper healthcare than California” gate is not achievable under the best official state-level data. The KFF/CMS state health-expenditure table puts California at **$10,299 per capita** in the 2020 vintage, so the 30% threshold is **$7,209**; no state is at or below it ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/state-health-policy-data/state-indicator/health-spending-per-capita/)). Utah is closest at **$7,522**, or **27.0% below California**, followed by Idaho ($8,148), Nevada ($8,348), Texas ($8,406), Colorado ($8,583), Arizona ($8,756), Georgia ($8,758), and North Carolina ($8,917). On KFF’s private-insurance spending metric, only Utah clears 30% below California ([KFF private insurance](https://www.kff.org/private-insurance/state-indicator/private-health-insurance-spending-per-capita-by-state/)). Numbeo’s healthcare cost index shows the same pattern: San Francisco scores 120.2, the 30% threshold is 84.1, and the cheapest major city in the data, Las Vegas, is 95.9 ([Numbeo healthcare](https://www.numbeo.com/health-care/country_result.jsp?country=United+States)).

That does **not** mean your personal premium will be 30% higher in Atlanta or Charlotte. Employer-sponsored premiums are priced largely nationally, and your actual exposure depends on plan type, deductible, network, and any California subsidy. Treat healthcare as a **plan/network question first** and a **state-ranking question second**. Because the literal gate is infeasible, I treat healthcare as a strong soft factor and rank the cities that clear the other gates while coming closest on healthcare.

## The San Francisco baseline and the 30% test

San Francisco is an unusually high bar. Numbeo’s February 2025 estimates put a single person’s monthly cost at **$1,556.60 excluding rent** and **$4,779.18 including a city-center one-bedroom**; the median home price is about **$1.36 million**, and healthcare runs **24% above the national average** ([CNBC/Numbeo](https://www.cnbc.com/amp/make-it/2025/02/17/this-is-how-much-it-can-cost-someone-to-live-across-12-major-cities.html); [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/cost-of-living-calculator/California-San-Francisco)). Numbeo’s SF page later lists a city-center one-bedroom at **$3,496.50/month** and an outside-center one-bedroom at **$2,772/month** ([Numbeo SF](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/in/San-Francisco)). Payscale’s median rent across all bedroom counts is **$4,073/month**.

A straight 30% reduction therefore implies roughly:

- **All-in monthly cost ≤ $3,345**
- **City-center one-bedroom ≤ $2,448**
- **Healthcare ≤ $7,209/year** on the KFF per-capita basis

Rent is not the binding constraint. The finalists’ city-center one-bedroom rents are **$1,620–$1,641 in Salt Lake City**, **$1,889 in Charlotte**, and **$2,069 in Atlanta**, all well below $2,448 ([SLC comparison](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/compare_cities.jsp?country1=United+States&city1=San+Francisco%2C+CA&country2=United+States&city2=Salt+Lake+City%2C+UT); [Charlotte comparison](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/compare_cities.jsp?country1=United+States&city1=San+Francisco%2C+CA&country2=United+States&city2=Charlotte%2C+NC); [Atlanta comparison](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/compare_cities.jsp?country1=United+States&city1=San+Francisco%2C+CA&country2=United+States&city2=Atlanta%2C+GA)).

Overall cost of living is metric-sensitive. On Numbeo’s **rent-inclusive** comparison, SLC is **40.6% below SF**, Charlotte **36.5% below**, and Atlanta **30.6% below**. On the **rent-excluded** index, only SLC clears 30% — about **30–31% below** — while Charlotte is **25.6% below** and Atlanta **17.8% below**. Because you are a renter and named rent specifically, I use the rent-inclusive measure as the primary test and treat the rent-excluded measure as a sensitivity.

A simplified cash-residual check — federal tax and FICA, rent, and national-average healthcare, excluding state income tax — gives about **$5,833/month in SLC**, **$5,565 in Charlotte**, and **$5,385 in Atlanta**, versus **$3,958 in San Francisco**. State income tax then improves the advantage further in all three states.

## How the ranking was built

I applied the hard gates first:

- **Cost:** rent-inclusive COL at least 30% below SF; one-bedroom rent at least 30% below SF.
- **Internet:** at least 100/20 Mbps, with a 300/30 Mbps target, fiber or high-tier cable, and at least two viable ISPs.
- **Walkability:** a named neighborhood with Walk Score above 60.
- **Nature:** green space or hiking within roughly 60 minutes.
- **Airport:** a major commercial airport within about 90 minutes’ typical drive from the walkable neighborhood.

Cities that passed were scored on **cost** (COL + rent + healthcare) **30%**, **internet 15%**, **walkability 15%**, **nature 10%**, **airport 10%**, **tax 10%**, **diversity 5%**, and **weather 5%**. Tie-breakers were residual income, internet speed, Walk Score, tax burden, and airport breadth.

## Ranked profiles

### 1. Salt Lake City, Utah

**Cost and healthcare.** SLC has the strongest cost position of the three: rent-inclusive COL is **40.6% below SF**, and a city-center one-bedroom is about **57% below SF** ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/compare_cities.jsp?country1=United+States&city1=San+Francisco%2C+CA&country2=United+States&city2=Salt+Lake+City%2C+UT)). Utah’s per-capita health spending is **$7,522**, or **27.0% below California** — the closest any state gets to your 30% target ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/state-health-policy-data/state-indicator/health-spending-per-capita/)). That combination is the main reason it ranks first.

**Internet.** SLC’s citywide median fixed connection is about **220 Mbps down / 72 Mbps up** with 10 ms latency; Google Fiber reaches about 43% of the city, Xfinity about 98%, UTOPIA Fiber about 20%, and Quantum about 39% ([Speedtest SLC](https://www.speedtest.net/performance/united-states/utah/salt-lake-city); [BroadbandNow 2026](https://broadbandnow.com/research/best-cities-with-internet-coverage-and-speed)). It clears the FCC’s 100/20 floor and has fiber, but the citywide median download is below the 300 Mbps target. Confirm 300/30 at the exact address before leasing.

**Walkability and nature.** The citywide Walk Score is **59**, just below your threshold, so the neighborhood matters. Downtown addresses score in the low-80s to low-90s, and Sugar House, 9th & 9th, and Liberty Wells are the most walkable residential cores ([Walk Score](https://www.walkscore.com/score/downtown-salt-lake-city-ut)). Nature access is the best of the three: the Wasatch-Cache National Forest covers 1.6 million acres, with Millcreek Canyon, Big Cottonwood Canyon, and Park City reachable in roughly 30–60 minutes ([USFS](https://www.fs.usda.gov/uwcnf/)).

**Airport.** Salt Lake City International is a Delta hub with about **28.2 million passengers in 2025** and a roughly 15-minute drive from downtown; TRAX light rail serves the airport ([SLC airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City_International_Airport); [FAA](https://www.faa.gov/airports/planning_capacity/passenger_allcargo_stats/passenger/arp-cy2024-commercial-service-enplanements.pdf)).

**Taxes.** Utah’s flat individual income tax is about **4.55%**, versus California’s **9.3% marginal rate** on a $120,000 salary ([Paycor](https://www.paycor.com/resource-center/articles/state-income-tax-rates/); [FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-540-tax-rate-schedules.pdf)). Utah’s total state-local burden is **9.46%**, lower than California’s **11.00%**, though not the lowest of the three finalists ([WalletHub](https://wallethub.com/edu/states-with-highest-lowest-tax-burden/20494)).

**Weather, diversity, and amenities.** Winters are cold and snowy — January averages **38/26°F**, with about 47 inches of snow annually — while summers are dry and sunny, July averaging **90/69°F** ([US Climate Data](https://usclimatedata.com/climate/salt-lake-city/utah/united-states/usut0225)). The bigger outdoor-lifestyle caveat is air quality: the American Lung Association’s 2024 rankings placed the SLC metro **9th worst for ozone** and **19th worst for short-term particle pollution**, with roughly 18 days per year above federal standards; winter inversions trap PM2.5 in the valley ([Air quality in Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_quality_in_Utah)). Diversity is lower than Atlanta or Charlotte: about **63.9% white, 1.6% Black, 21.1% Hispanic, 5.7% Asian**, and **16.6% foreign-born** ([City-Data](https://www.city-data.com/races/races-Salt-Lake-City-Utah.html)). Amenities include the University of Utah, the “Silicon Slopes” tech corridor, Intermountain Medical Center, and world-class ski access at Park City, Alta, Snowbird, and Deer Valley.

**Verdict.** Best cost, closest healthcare, and best hiking. The weaknesses are the citywide Walk Score of 59, winter cold and inversion-related air pollution, lower diversity, and a smaller airport network than Atlanta or Charlotte.

### 2. Atlanta, Georgia

**Cost and healthcare.** Atlanta’s rent-inclusive COL is **30.6% below SF**, and a city-center one-bedroom is **46.1% below SF** ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/compare_cities.jsp?country1=United+States&city1=San+Francisco%2C+CA&country2=United+States&city2=Atlanta%2C+GA)). The margin is thin: on a rent-excluded index, Atlanta is only **17.8% below SF**. Healthcare is the bigger weakness — Georgia’s per-capita spending is **$8,758**, or **15.0% below California** ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/state-health-policy-data/state-indicator/health-spending-per-capita/)).

**Internet.** Atlanta’s citywide median fixed connection is about **226 Mbps down / 80 Mbps up** with 10 ms latency. AT&T Fiber reaches about 80% of the city, Xfinity about 98.2%, and Spectrum about 10.4%, with Google Fiber and T-Mobile 5G also present ([Speedtest Atlanta](https://www.speedtest.net/performance/united-states/georgia/atlanta); [BroadbandNow 2026](https://broadbandnow.com/research/best-cities-with-internet-coverage-and-speed)). The median download is below 300 Mbps, but fiber is widely available; verify the exact address.

**Walkability and nature.** The citywide Walk Score is **47.7**, but the walkable core is strong: South Downtown scores **83**, and Midtown, Old Fourth Ward, Inman Park, Virginia Highland, and Decatur are the most walkable neighborhoods ([Walk Score Atlanta](https://www.walkscore.com/score/atlanta-ga)). Nature access includes the Chattahoochee River National Recreation Area, Stone Mountain, Kennesaw Mountain, the Atlanta Beltline, and what is described as the densest urban tree canopy of any major U.S. city ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/chat/index.htm); [Wikipedia Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta)).

**Airport.** Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International is the world’s busiest airport, with about **108.1 million passengers in 2024** and 225 destinations; MARTA rail runs to the terminal ([ATL airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartsfield%E2%80%93Jackson_Atlanta_International_Airport)). This is the clearest airport advantage among the three.

**Taxes.** Georgia’s flat income tax is **5.19% for 2025**, scheduled to drop to **4.99% for 2026**, with a standard deduction of **$12,000 for single filers in 2025** and **$15,000 in 2026** ([Georgia DOR](https://dor.georgia.gov/taxes/important-tax-updates)). Georgia has no broad local income tax. Its total state-local burden is **8.47%**, versus California’s **11.00%** ([WalletHub](https://wallethub.com/edu/states-with-highest-lowest-tax-burden/20494)).

**Weather, diversity, and amenities.** Atlanta has a humid subtropical climate: January averages **53/35°F**, July averages **88/71°F**, annual precipitation is about **43 inches**, and snow is rare ([US Climate Data](https://www.usclimatedata.com/climate/atlanta/georgia/united-states); [WeatherSpark](https://weatherspark.com/y/15598/Average-Weather-in-Atlanta-Georgia-United-States-Year-Round)). The hot season runs about 3.8 months with average highs above 81°F, rarely exceeding 95°F. Racial/ethnic percentages were not retrievable in this research, but Atlanta is a historic civil-rights center and major Black cultural hub; its HRC Municipal Equality Index score is **100** ([HRC](https://hrc-prod-requests.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/files/documents/MEI-Scorecard-Assets/MEI-25-Scorecards/MEI-2025-Atlanta-Georgia.pdf)). Amenities include the Georgia Aquarium, World of Coca-Cola, Fox Theatre, Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Centennial Olympic Park, major universities, and a large film/television sector; Emory University Hospital is ranked #1 in Georgia and metro Atlanta ([Emory](https://www.emoryhealthcare.org/newsroom/2025/07/us-news-and-world-report-ranks-three-emory-hospitals-as-best-in-georgia-and-atlanta)).

**Verdict.** Best airport, walkable-neighborhood stock, diversity, and amenities. The weaknesses are the thin COL margin, healthcare only 15% below California, hot humid summers, and a higher income tax than North Carolina or Utah. If you weight walkability and airport access more heavily than cost and healthcare, Atlanta is the better #1.

### 3. Charlotte, North Carolina

**Cost and healthcare.** Charlotte’s rent-inclusive COL is **36.5% below SF**, and a city-center one-bedroom is **49.6% below SF** ([Numbeo](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/compare_cities.jsp?country1=United+States&city1=San+Francisco%2C+CA&country2=United+States&city2=Charlotte%2C+NC)). On a rent-excluded basis, it is only **25.6% below SF**, so the overall-COL pass depends on including rent. Healthcare is the weakest of the three: North Carolina’s per-capita spending is **$8,917**, or **13.4% below California** ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/state-health-policy-data/state-indicator/health-spending-per-capita/)).

**Internet.** Charlotte has the strongest median internet of the three: about **310.6 Mbps down / 47.4 Mbps up** with 17 ms latency. Google Fiber reaches about 14% of the city, AT&T Fiber about 83%, and Ripple Fiber about 1%, with Spectrum cable as another option ([Speedtest Charlotte](https://www.speedtest.net/performance/united-states/north-carolina/charlotte)). It meets the 300/30 target at the median and has three viable wired providers, though address-level verification is still required.

**Walkability and nature.** Charlotte is a car-oriented city: the citywide Walk Score is **26.4**. The walkable core is South End, Uptown, NoDa, Plaza Midwood, and Dilworth; South End scores about **74**, First Ward about **85**, and Fourth Ward about **89** ([Walk Score Charlotte](https://web.archive.org/web/2025/https://www.walkscore.com/score/charlotte-nc)). About **66% of the city’s area is green space**, with Lake Norman, Crowders Mountain State Park about 32 miles west, Uwharrie National Forest, and the U.S. National Whitewater Center nearby ([Wikipedia Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte,_North_Carolina); [Crowders Mountain](https://www.ncparks.gov/state-parks/crowders-mountain-state-park)). One caution: Charlotte-Mecklenburg’s 2026 ParkScore rank was **90th of 100**, with a score of **37.1/100**, so the park system itself is weaker than the nature access suggests ([Mecklenburg County](https://news.mecknc.gov/news/mecklenburg-county-and-2026-trust-public-land-parkscore)).

**Airport.** Charlotte Douglas International is an American Airlines fortress hub with about **58.8 million passengers in 2024**, located about six miles west of the central business district ([CLT airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Douglas_International_Airport)). It is the easiest airport commute of the three.

**Taxes.** North Carolina’s flat income tax is **4.25% for 2025**, scheduled to drop to **3.99% for 2026**; there is no broad local income tax ([NCDOR](https://www.ncdor.gov/taxes-forms/individual-income-tax/tax-rate-schedules)). The total state-local burden is **8.18%**, the lowest of the three finalists, versus California’s **11.00%** ([WalletHub](https://wallethub.com/edu/states-with-highest-lowest-tax-burden/20494)).

**Weather, diversity, and amenities.** Charlotte is humid subtropical, with January lows around **34°F**, July highs around **89°F**, annual precipitation of about **41.6 inches**, and only about four inches of snow ([WeatherSpark](https://weatherspark.com/y/18893/Average-Weather-in-Charlotte-North-Carolina-United-States-Year-Round); [US Climate Data](https://usclimatedata.com/climate/charlotte/north-carolina/united-states/usnc0121)). Diversity is relatively strong: about **37.3% white, 32.3% Black, 18.4% Hispanic, and 6.7% Asian**; the foreign-born share was not verified ([City-Data](https://www.city-data.com/city/Charlotte-North-Carolina.html)). Its HRC Municipal Equality Index score is **80** ([HRC](https://hrc-prod-requests.s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/files/documents/MEI-Scorecard-Assets/MEI-25-Scorecards/MEI-2025-Charlotte-North-Carolina.pdf)). Amenities include the Panthers, Hornets, and Charlotte FC, the NASCAR Hall of Fame, the Mint/Bechtler/Gantt museums, Opera Carolina, the Charlotte Symphony, and the country’s second-largest banking center; Atrium Health is the major hospital system.

**Verdict.** Balanced cost, tax, internet, and airport. The weaknesses are the weakest healthcare position of the three and serious citywide car dependence.

### The California remote-work tax rule that matters most

California taxes wages where services are performed, not where the employer is located. If you relocate and perform **all** services outside California, the wages are generally **not** California-source income. If you periodically return to California to work, you file Form 540NR and pay California tax on the ratio of **California workdays ÷ total workdays** ([FTB](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/file/personal/residency-status/part-year-and-nonresident.html)). Deferred or equity compensation can retain California source even after you leave ([FTB Publication 1031](https://www.ftb.ca.gov/forms/2025/2025-1031-publication.pdf)).

To establish nonresidency, build a paper trail: new driver’s license, voter registration, vehicle registration, IRS Form 8822, employer withholding/work-state change, bank and insurance address updates, and a contemporaneous workday log ([IRS Form 8822](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f8822.pdf)). Several states apply a “convenience of the employer” rule to remote work; because your employer is California-based, that is usually not a direct problem, but confirm the employer’s entity locations and nexus ([Connecticut OLR](https://www.cga.ct.gov/2025/rpt/pdf/2025-R-0067.pdf)).

## Evidence limits

- **Numbeo is crowdsourced.** Its city pages and comparison pages are useful for relative price levels, but the rent-inclusive and rent-excluded measures can flip a city’s pass/fail. Cross-check rents against Zillow, Redfin, Apartments.com, or current listings before leasing ([Numbeo methodology](https://www.numbeo.com/common/motivation_and_methodology.jsp)).
- **KFF per-capita health spending is a 2020 vintage**, the latest state-level series retrieved. State-level premiums and deductibles were not retrievable, so the healthcare comparison is a relative screen, not your personal premium quote.
- **Walk Score city averages are population-weighted grid averages.** A city can average below 60 while containing neighborhoods above 80. The gate should be applied to the exact address ([Walk Score methodology](https://web.archive.org/web/2024/https://www.walkscore.com/methodology.shtml)).
- **Internet city medians do not guarantee service at a specific address.** Ookla and BroadbandNow use different methodologies and can disagree materially; verify the exact unit with both, plus the FCC National Broadband Map ([Ookla methodology](https://www.ookla.com/resources/guides/speedtest-methodology); [FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/broadband)).
- **ParkScore was unavailable for most cities** in this research; Charlotte-Mecklenburg’s 2026 rank is the only verified ParkScore result.
- **Airport drive times are approximate.** Test the drive at peak, not midday, from the specific neighborhood.

## Before you sign a lease

1. **Get the employer’s remote-work and pay policy in writing.** Confirm the official work state, withholding state, and whether a location-based pay cut applies. The $120,000 salary is assumed unchanged here, but that must be verified.
2. **Establish domicile.** Get a new driver’s license, register to vote, register your vehicle, file IRS Form 8822, change your employer withholding, and move bank/insurance addresses. Keep a workday log.
3. **Check the health plan type.** If you are on a California HMO such as Kaiser, routine care outside California is generally not covered; urgent and emergency care is covered while traveling, but post-stabilization and routine follow-up usually require approval ([Kaiser](https://healthy.kaiserpermanente.org/get-care/traveling/care-outside-KP-area)). Ask for the plan’s service area, out-of-network benefits, and whether a national PPO or out-of-area plan is available. If you are on Covered California, moving out of state ends eligibility; you generally have a 60-day special-enrollment window from the move ([Covered California](https://www.coveredca.com/marketing-blog/special-enrollment-when-is-it-and-how-it-works)).
4. **Verify the exact address, not the city.** Run the unit through BroadbandNow and the FCC map; check cell coverage as a backup; look up the FEMA flood zone; check crime data; confirm Walk Score and park access; and test the peak-hour airport drive ([FEMA](https://www.fema.gov/flood-maps)).
5. **Do a 30–60 day trial rental first.** Visit in the worst weather month, not the best, before signing a 12-month lease.
6. **Budget the move.** Long-distance moves generally run **$800–$12,000**, with DIY/container moves typically **$1,500–$3,500** and full-service moves higher; get three binding quotes and move off-peak if possible ([Extra Space Storage](https://www.extraspace.com/moving/tools/moving-cost-calculator/)).

**Final recommendation.** Choose **Salt Lake City** if cost and healthcare proximity matter most and you are comfortable with cold winters, inversion-season air quality, and lower diversity. Choose **Atlanta** if airport access, walkable neighborhoods, diversity, and amenities dominate. Choose **Charlotte** if you want the lowest tax rate, the strongest median internet, and a major hub with an easy airport commute. In all three cases, the first concrete step is to verify address-level internet and health-plan portability before signing anything.

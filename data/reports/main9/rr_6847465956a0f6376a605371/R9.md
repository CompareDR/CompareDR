# Relocation Research Report: Top 3 U.S. Cities for a Remote Software Engineer Leaving San Francisco

**Prepared for:** Remote software engineer earning **$120,000/year** in San Francisco  
**Date assumed:** October 10, 2026  
**Decision objective:** Identify the best U.S. relocation targets that are materially cheaper than San Francisco while preserving reliable remote-work infrastructure, walkability, green-space access, and airport connectivity.

---

## Executive Summary

Based on the available cost, walkability, housing, tax, climate, and digital-nomad infrastructure data, my ranked recommendation is:

1. **Austin, Texas — Best overall fit**
2. **Salt Lake City, Utah — Best nature-and-airport balance**
3. **Philadelphia, Pennsylvania — Best big-city walkability value**

Austin ranks first because it is the only candidate in the provided data with an explicit San Francisco-to-Austin cost-of-living comparison showing roughly **31% lower overall cost of living**, no state income tax, several highly walkable neighborhoods, a major airport, and strong urban amenities. Salt Lake City ranks second because its metro-level rent is roughly **50% below San Francisco’s**, it has the strongest walkability/transit coverage among several western and Sun Belt metros reviewed, and it offers unusually good access to hiking and outdoor recreation. Philadelphia ranks third because it is substantially cheaper than San Francisco on housing, has a very high citywide Walk Score, a major airport, and the most complete “urban without SF prices” profile.

The biggest caveat is healthcare: the research base indicates that **healthcare costs usually vary only a few percent nationally**, while housing varies dramatically. That means it is realistic to get **30%+ lower total cost of living and rent**, but it is not realistic to promise healthcare alone will be 30% cheaper without employer-plan-specific data ([Costoflivingusa, n.d.](https://costoflivingusa.com/)).

## Methodology and Decision Criteria

I weighted the cities against your stated non-negotiables:

| Criterion | Why it matters | Evidence used |
|---|---:|---|
| **At least 30% cheaper than San Francisco** | Your $120k salary has much more value if rent and taxes fall | Numbeo San Francisco benchmarks, metro rent/home values, COL comparison tools |
| **Rent materially lower** | Housing is the dominant driver of cross-city differences | SafeStreets rent/home data; Costoflivingusa housing analysis |
| **Reliable high-speed internet** | Remote software work depends on uptime and speed | Novoresume digital-nomad internet rankings; coworking backup availability |
| **Walkable neighborhood over 60 Walk Score** | You do not want car dependency | Walk Score summaries, neighborhood-level Austin data, SafeStreets walkable/transit coverage |
| **Green spaces / hiking** | Quality of life and recreation | City/neighborhood descriptions and known metro profiles, with evidence where available |
| **Major airport within ~90 minutes** | Travel flexibility | Candidate screening against city airport access |
| **Tax implications** | Moving from California can materially raise take-home pay | Tax Foundation and cost-of-living tax modeling sources |
| **Climate / insurance / risk** | Lower rent can be offset by insurance, utilities, or climate exposure | Realtor.com 2025 climate-risk report, PreventionWeb summary, insurance data |

A core research finding is that generic cost-of-living indexes can mislead because household budgets vary. A useful comparison should weight housing, groceries, transportation, healthcare, utilities, and miscellaneous expenses to match the mover’s actual lifestyle rather than assuming fixed national weights ([US Finance Calculators, 2026](https://usfinancecalculators.com/personal-finance/cost-of-living-comparison-calculator/)). For your case, housing, taxes, internet reliability, and neighborhood walkability should receive unusually high weights.

## San Francisco Baseline

San Francisco remains expensive even when compared with other high-cost U.S. cities. Numbeo’s current Americas ranking lists San Francisco with a **Cost of Living Index of 89.0**, **Rent Index of 75.0**, **Cost of Living Plus Rent Index of 82.5**, **Groceries Index of 89.7**, **Restaurant Price Index of 98.7**, and **Local Purchasing Power Index of 151.8** ([Numbeo, 2026](https://www.numbeo.com/cost-of-living/region_rankings_current.jsp?region=019)).

Using those Numbeo values as a benchmark, a city would need an overall index of **62.3 or lower** to be at least 30% cheaper overall, and a rent index of **52.5 or lower** to be at least 30% cheaper on rent. However, the most important practical point is that housing dominates the difference between cities: rent and home prices can vary by multiples, while groceries, utilities, and healthcare usually vary much less ([Costoflivingusa, n.d.](https://costoflivingusa.com/)).

For a remote worker, the financial upside is “geographic arbitrage”: keeping the same salary while moving to a lower-cost city. The cited calculator explicitly models this by holding salary constant and estimating monthly and annual savings from lower expenses ([US Finance Calculators, 2026](https://usfinancecalculators.com/personal-finance/cost-of-living-comparison-calculator/)).

# 1. Austin, Texas — Best Overall Fit

## Why Austin ranks #1

Austin is the strongest recommendation because it directly matches the relocation pattern in the research: a remote software worker leaving a very high-cost coastal market for a cheaper tech-oriented city while keeping salary constant. The cost-of-living calculator’s example specifically notes that moving from San Francisco to Austin while keeping a San Francisco salary can produce geographic-arbitrage savings, with SF shown at **COL 178** and Austin at **COL 123**—a reduction of roughly **30.9%** ([US Finance Calculators, 2026](https://usfinancecalculators.com/personal-finance/cost-of-living-comparison-calculator/)).

Austin also has a strong rent case. SafeStreets reports Austin metro rent around **$1,635/month**, compared with **$3,258/month** for San Francisco, implying Austin rent is roughly **50% lower** than San Francisco’s metro figure in that dataset ([SafeStreets, 2026](https://safestreets.streetsandcommons.com/most-walkable-cities)).

## Walkable neighborhoods

Austin is not uniformly walkable, so the neighborhood matters. The best fit would be **Downtown, East Austin, West University, North University, or Bouldin Creek**. Moreland reports these Austin neighborhood Walk Scores:

| Austin neighborhood | Walk Score |
|---|---:|
| Downtown | 98 |
| West University | 93 |
| East Austin | 91 |
| North University | 84 |
| Bouldin Creek | 81 |

Downtown is described as the top area for public transit, while East Austin offers a live/work mix near downtown. Bouldin Creek gives access to South Austin plus Zilker Park and Auditorium Shores, though it has less walkable shopping than more central neighborhoods and is strongest for restaurants and food trucks ([Moreland Properties, 2026](https://moreland.com/blog/most-walkable-neighborhoods)).

## Internet and remote-work infrastructure

Austin is not listed among Novoresume’s five fastest-internet cities, which are Chattanooga, Tampa, St. Petersburg, El Paso, and Orlando. However, those fastest-internet cities often fall short on walkability, climate risk, or urban-lifestyle criteria. The broader research finding is that no single source directly ranks cities by the full bundle of high-speed internet, Walk Score over 60, parks/hiking, and airport access ([Novoresume, 2026](https://novoresume.com/career-blog/best-us-cities-digital-nomads)).

For backup work infrastructure, Austin has Switchyards locations in **East Austin** and **Hyde Park**, and Switchyards advertises 24/7 access, phone booths, meeting rooms, and “dangerously fast internet” ([Switchyards, 2026](https://www.switchyards.com/)). For a remote engineer, that does not replace checking fiber availability at a specific apartment, but it reduces operational risk.

## Taxes

This is a major advantage. Texas has no state income tax and ranks #1 for individual taxes in the Tax Foundation index. California ranks near the bottom at #49 for individual taxes ([Tax Foundation, 2026](https://taxfoundation.org/statetaxindex/)). Another cited tax source notes that 2026 top marginal state income tax rates range from **0% in nine states** to **13.3% in California** ([US Finance Calculators, 2026](https://usfinancecalculators.com/personal-finance/cost-of-living-comparison-calculator/)).

For a $120,000 remote salary, this is likely one of the largest take-home-pay improvements among the finalists.

## Trade-offs

Austin’s biggest downsides are heat, sprawl, and climate exposure. Realtor.com’s 2024 climate-risk work identified Austin as one of the metros where **100% of homes face severe or extreme heat risk** ([Realtor.com, 2024](https://www.realtor.com/research/climate-risk-2024/)). Austin’s new construction market is favorable, and Realtor.com reports that Austin new builds are priced below existing homes and have lower flood risk than existing metro homes, but new builds are slightly more fire-prone than existing homes ([Realtor.com, 2025](https://www.realtor.com/research/top-new-construction-metros-2025/)).

**Bottom line:** Choose Austin if your top priorities are maximizing after-tax income, staying in a tech-friendly environment, and living in a specific walkable neighborhood.

# 2. Salt Lake City, Utah — Best Nature + Airport Balance

## Why Salt Lake City ranks #2

Salt Lake City is the best fit if access to hiking and nature is nearly as important as cost savings. It has a strong housing-cost profile relative to San Francisco. SafeStreets reports Salt Lake City metro rent around **$1,641/month**, compared with San Francisco’s **$3,258/month**, or roughly **50% lower**. Typical home value is listed around **$453,168**, compared with San Francisco’s **$1,065,932**, a much lower ownership-cost base ([SafeStreets, 2026](https://safestreets.streetsandcommons.com/most-walkable-cities)).

## Walkability and transit

Salt Lake City performs surprisingly well in the SafeStreets walkability/transit dataset. Among the queried metros, it appears strongest on broad walkability/transit coverage: **47.6% of residents live in neighborhoods that are both walkable and in the top 40% for transit access**. That is higher than Austin at 41.5%, Phoenix at 36.4%, Denver at 29.5%, and Tampa at 17.6% ([SafeStreets, 2026](https://safestreets.streetsandcommons.com/most-walkable-cities)).

This makes Salt Lake City a better “not stuck in a car” candidate than many western metros. The right neighborhood still matters, but the metro-level data is favorable.

## Internet and airport considerations

Salt Lake City is not listed among the fastest-internet cities in Novoresume’s dataset, so you should verify fiber or high-speed cable availability at the address level before signing a lease. That said, the overall package is stronger than many faster-internet cities because it combines cost savings, walkability/transit coverage, airport access, and outdoor access. Novoresume’s own framework confirms that internet speed and airport connectivity are important digital-nomad variables, but also notes that the fastest-internet cities are not necessarily the usual tech hubs or best lifestyle fits ([Novoresume, 2026](https://novoresume.com/career-blog/best-us-cities-digital-nomads)).

## Taxes

Utah is not as tax-advantaged as Texas, but it is much better than California. Tax Foundation notes that Utah has continued incremental reductions in individual and corporate income tax rates, with the rate reduced from **4.55% to 4.5%** for both taxes in 2025 ([Tax Foundation, 2026](https://taxfoundation.org/research/all/state/2026-state-tax-competitiveness-index/)). Compared with California’s high marginal-rate structure, Utah should materially improve take-home pay for a $120,000 earner, though not as dramatically as Texas.

## Trade-offs

Salt Lake City has fewer big-city amenities than San Francisco or Philadelphia. It may also feel culturally different from coastal tech hubs. If diversity, nightlife density, and large-city cultural institutions are central to your lifestyle, Philadelphia may outperform it. But if the priority is hiking, airport convenience, lower rent, and a cleaner day-to-day budget, Salt Lake City is a strong second-place choice.

**Bottom line:** Choose Salt Lake City if you want the strongest nature access and a very large housing-cost reduction while retaining decent walkability/transit coverage.

# 3. Philadelphia, Pennsylvania — Best Big-City Walkability Value

## Why Philadelphia ranks #3

Philadelphia is the best “real city” alternative to San Francisco in this ranking. It has strong walkability, major-city amenities, a large airport, historic neighborhoods, universities, hospitals, restaurants, and cultural institutions—while housing costs are far below San Francisco’s.

SafeStreets reports Philadelphia metro rent around **$1,914/month**, compared with San Francisco’s **$3,258/month**, about **41% lower**. Typical home value is around **$337,890**, compared with San Francisco’s **$1,065,932**, making ownership far more accessible ([SafeStreets, 2026](https://safestreets.streetsandcommons.com/most-walkable-cities)).

## Walkability

Philadelphia is one of the strongest walkability candidates in the research. It is reported around **Walk Score 74.8/75**, well above the 60 threshold and within the “Very Walkable” range. A Walk Score above 60 is meaningfully above the average for large U.S. cities, as the 130 U.S. cities with populations over 200,000 average 48, while 70–89 is categorized as “Very Walkable” ([Radical Storage, 2026](https://radicalstorage.com/travel/most-walkable-cities-in-the-us/)).

SafeStreets also ranks Philadelphia well on walkable and transit-served neighborhood coverage, with **50.8%** of residents living in neighborhoods that are both walkable and in the top 40% for transit access ([SafeStreets, 2026](https://safestreets.streetsandcommons.com/most-walkable-cities)).

## Taxes

Pennsylvania is not a no-income-tax state, but it is far less punishing than California. The broader tax research emphasizes that state income taxes materially affect take-home pay, and California sits near the bottom of the Tax Foundation individual-tax ranking at #49 ([Tax Foundation, 2026](https://taxfoundation.org/statetaxindex/)). The main Philadelphia-specific caveat is that city-level wage or local taxes should be modeled before committing; city taxes can reduce the advantage relative to Austin or Salt Lake City.

## Trade-offs

Philadelphia has colder winters and more humidity than San Francisco. It also has substantial neighborhood-by-neighborhood variation in safety, schools, housing quality, and amenities. For your profile, the best fit would likely be a highly walkable neighborhood with transit access and parks nearby rather than an outer car-oriented area.

Philadelphia’s internet profile is less clearly documented in the provided dataset than Austin’s coworking backup or Chattanooga’s fiber leadership. As with Salt Lake City, you should verify building-level fiber/cable service before signing a lease.

**Bottom line:** Choose Philadelphia if you want the most urban, walkable, culturally rich alternative to San Francisco at a much lower housing cost.

---

## Why I Did Not Rank Chattanooga, Tampa, or Denver in the Top 3

### Chattanooga, Tennessee

Chattanooga is the standout broadband value city. Novoresume reports **186.4 Mbps average speeds**, the fastest in its index, supported by EPB’s municipal **10-gig fiber network**, plus below-average living costs and roughly **$950/month rent** ([Novoresume, 2026](https://novoresume.com/career-blog/best-us-cities-digital-nomads)). Tennessee also has no state income tax, which is excellent.

However, Chattanooga was not placed in the top 3 because the research explicitly flags that separate validation is needed for citywide Walk Score and airport/hiking fit. The airport issue is especially important if “major airport within 90 minutes” is strict.

### Tampa / St. Petersburg, Florida

Tampa and St. Petersburg appear in the fastest-internet group, and Florida has no state income tax. But Tampa performs poorly on broad walkability/transit coverage, with only **17.6%** of residents in walkable and transit-served neighborhoods ([SafeStreets, 2026](https://safestreets.streetsandcommons.com/most-walkable-cities)). Florida also has major insurance and climate-risk issues. Miami has the highest HO-3 premium-to-market-value ratio among the top 100 metros at **3.7%**, and Tampa is also in the top 10 at **1.7%**, with an estimated typical annual premium around **$6,645** ([PreventionWeb, 2025](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/2025-realtorcom-housing-and-climate-risk-report)).

### Denver, Colorado

Denver has excellent outdoor access and several highly walkable neighborhoods, but climate and insurance concerns are significant. Realtor.com’s 2025 climate-risk data shows Denver-Aurora-Centennial has **30.7%** of home value at severe or extreme wildfire risk ([PreventionWeb, 2025](https://www.preventionweb.net/news/2025-realtorcom-housing-and-climate-risk-report)). NerdWallet also lists Denver homeowners insurance at about **$6,315/year**, much higher than several alternatives ([NerdWallet, 2026](https://www.nerdwallet.com/insurance/homeowners/learn/average-homeowners-insurance-cost)).

## Final Recommendation

If I were making the decision for a remote software engineer earning **$120,000** in San Francisco, I would prioritize:

1. **Austin, TX** if maximizing after-tax income and staying in a tech-forward city is the top priority.
2. **Salt Lake City, UT** if hiking, mountains, and airport access matter almost as much as cost.
3. **Philadelphia, PA** if you want the most walkable big-city lifestyle at a much lower housing cost than San Francisco.

The most financially powerful move is probably **Austin**, because it combines the clearest 30%+ cost-of-living reduction evidence with no state income tax. The most lifestyle-balanced move is probably **Salt Lake City**, especially if hiking is central to your life. The best urbanist move is **Philadelphia**, because it offers genuine city walkability rather than isolated walkable pockets.

Before signing a lease in any city, verify three things at the address level: **fiber or high-speed cable availability, renter’s insurance and utility costs, and the neighborhood’s actual Walk Score / transit access**. Citywide averages are useful for screening, but your lived experience will be determined by the specific block and building.

# Gut Microbiota and Colorectal Cancer Treatment: Interaction Matrix and Future Directions

## Core answer

Colorectal cancer (CRC) is reproducibly associated with **dysbiosis** — a shift away from the healthy, high-diversity gut community toward mucin-degrading, genotoxic and pro-inflammatory **pathobionts** (normally commensal organisms that can promote disease under dysbiotic conditions). The best-reproduced pathobionts are *Fusobacterium nucleatum* (Fn), enterotoxigenic *Bacteroides fragilis* (ETBF) and pks+ *Escherichia coli*; the best-reproduced depleted commensals are *Faecalibacterium prausnitzii*, *Bifidobacterium* and *Lactobacillus* ([Ranjbar et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12935-021-01886-z); [Lichtenstern et al. 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/bacteriology/articles/10.3389/fbrio.2023.1229077/full); [Cancer Cell International 2022 review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12672-022-00517-x)). Microbes influence treatment through five general routes: immune modulation, metabolite signalling, barrier integrity/translocation, genotoxins/virulence factors, and microbial drug metabolism.

Three conclusions organize the evidence available to 30 September 2024. First, the strongest mechanistic interactions are **Fn-driven resistance to 5-fluorouracil (5-FU)/capecitabine and oxaliplatin**, and **bacterial β-glucuronidase (GUS)-mediated irinotecan toxicity**. Second, **probiotics modestly reduce chemotherapy- and radiation-induced diarrhoea**, but effects are strain-, dose- and population-specific. Third, **microbiome modulation of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs)** is biologically compelling but rests mainly on melanoma and other non-CRC cohorts plus early-phase faecal microbiota transplantation (FMT) trials; the CRC-specific evidence is not practice-changing. Nearly all human data are associative; causal evidence comes mainly from gnotobiotic and FMT animal models. No microbiome-guided intervention is incorporated into major CRC guidelines.

| Surgery | Colorectal resection with anastomosis (anastomotic leak, AL) | • Diverse, short-chain-fatty-acid (SCFA)-producing communities support collagen stability and epithelial repair; SCFA/butyrate deficiency is implicated in impaired healing ([Lianos et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/22/6634); [Hajjar et al. 2024](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/30/3/616/733547/) via [Helliwell et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11399839/)).<br>• In mice, inulin (fermented to butyrate) plus 5-ASA reinforced the gut barrier and reduced AL and anastomotic tumour implantation/dissemination ([Hajjar et al. 2024](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/30/3/616/733547/)). | • Collagenase/gelatinase-producing *Enterococcus faecalis* degrades healing anastomotic tissue; associated with AL in a rodent model and human surgical cohort ([Anderson et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8851212/)).<br>• AL is associated with enrichment of *F. nucleatum*, *Prevotella*, *Bacteroides*, *Klebsiella*, *Pseudomonas* and *Streptococcus*, and with low-diversity/dysbiotic communities ([Hajjar et al. 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002961019301977); [Lianos et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/22/6634)).<br>• Poor healing permits implantation/dissemination of exfoliated CRC cells; AL is associated with higher local recurrence and lower disease-free survival ([Helliwell et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11399839/); [Hajjar et al. 2024](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/30/3/616/733547/)).<br>• Counter-evidence: AL occurred independently of bowel-preparation-induced microbiota shifts in a wild-type murine model (mechanical preparation/oral antibiotics/combination/water: 49%/23%/44%/31%; χ² P=0.09) ([Boatman et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11408509/)). |

## Surgery

Anastomotic leak is best understood as a host–microbiome interaction superimposed on technical, nutritional and ischaemic factors: the microbiome modifies risk but is not a necessary cause. The candidate mechanism with the most direct experimental support is collagenolysis — collagenase/gelatinase-producing *E. faecalis* degrading extracellular matrix at the healing interface — supported by a rodent model and a human surgical cohort ([Anderson et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8851212/)). Reviews consistently associate AL with enrichment of *F. nucleatum*, *Prevotella*, *Bacteroides*, *Klebsiella*, *Pseudomonas* and *Streptococcus* and with low-diversity communities ([Hajjar et al. 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0002961019301977); [Lianos et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/22/6634)), while the protective side involves SCFA-mediated collagen stability and epithelial repair ([Hajjar et al. 2024](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/30/3/616/733547/) via [Helliwell et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11399839/)). Poor healing is also oncologically relevant: AL is associated with local recurrence and reduced disease-free survival, and in a murine caecal-colotomy model poor healing permitted implantation of exfoliated CRC cells ([Helliwell et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11399839/); [Hajjar et al. 2024](https://aacrjournals.org/clincancerres/article/30/3/616/733547/)).

The causal claim is tempered by a 2024 wild-type murine study in which AL occurred even when mechanical bowel preparation and oral antibiotics did not significantly shift the microbiota, indicating that bowel preparation alters the microbiome without uniformly changing leak risk ([Boatman et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11408509/)). Perioperative pro-/synbiotics reduced infectious complications in a 13-RCT meta-analysis ([Chen et al. 2016](https://e-century.us/files/ijcem/9/6/ijcem0024099.pdf)), and the non-randomised MIRACLe protocol (probiotics, oral antibiotics, low-volume MBP) reported a considerably lower AL rate ([Carlini et al. 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35739383/); [Marcellinaro et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37452926/)), but these are hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing.

The sharpest perioperative trade-off is bowel preparation. Adding oral antibiotics to MBP lowers SSI in roughly half of trials, yet the combination produces marked, sometimes month-long dysbiosis (loss of alpha diversity, enrichment of *Enterococcus*/*Lactobacillus*/*Streptococcus*) that MBP alone largely avoids ([WHO Appendix 6](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/integrated-health-services-%28ihs%29/ssi/evidence/appendix6.pdf); [Nalluri-Butz et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14819-1)). Because IV antibiotics, age and comorbidity co-vary in surgical cohorts, the independent contribution of MBP plus oral antibiotics cannot be isolated ([Nalluri-Butz et al. 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14819-1)). Clinically this is a trade-off rather than a simple substitution: MBP plus oral antibiotics remains guideline-supported for SSI prevention despite its microbiome cost.

No verified study directly compares microbiome composition between minimally invasive and open resection, or characterises the microbiome around CRS/HIPEC, liver metastasectomy or lung metastasectomy. The only adverse signal is the epidemiologic infection burden after CRS/HIPEC ([Yang et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12885-023-11404-1)); the gut–liver axis provides a plausible portal-translocation mechanism for liver metastasis ([Mignini et al. 2024](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-0383/13/2/420)). These should be carried as explicit evidence gaps, not negative findings.

## Chemotherapy

The best-supported fluoropyrimidine interaction is Fn-driven resistance. Fn activates TLR4/MyD88/NF-κB→BIRC3 anti-apoptotic signalling and miR-18a*/ULK1/ATG7/Beclin-1 autophagy, and suppresses chemotherapy-induced pyroptosis through Hippo/YAP/BCL2 ([Mandip et al. 2018](https://tcr.amegroups.org/article/view/18249/html); [Zhang et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6327560/); [Wang et al. 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19490976.2024.2333790)). Two human cohorts corroborate the direction: high intratumoral Fn correlated with chemoresistance and recurrence after 5-FU-based adjuvant therapy, and an Fn-based recurrence model outperformed AJCC stage ([Zhang et al. 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6327560/); [Wang et al. 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/19490976.2024.2333790)). No randomised trial has tested Fn depletion to restore drug sensitivity, so this remains mechanistic plus associative, not actionable.

Oxaliplatin resistance converges on overlapping scaffolds. Fn overexpresses GPX4 and inhibits ferroptosis through the E-cadherin/β-catenin/TCF4 axis, and separately upregulates ANO1 to block apoptosis ([Li et al. 2024](https://www-ovid-com.ezproxy.uky.edu/journals/frbm/abstract/10.1016/j.freeradbiomed.2024.04.226~fusobacterium-nucleatum-induces-oxaliplatin-resistance-by?redirectionsource=fulltextview); [Lu et al. 2019](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/8ed8/4c83a089e69967d5e3a2f6024ccd02896c22.pdf)). Oxaliplatin also reshapes the murine microbiota and engages HMGB1/TLR4 signalling (Stojanovska et al. 2018). In the opposite direction, daily FMT reduced FOLFOX-induced diarrhoea and mucositis in tumour-bearing mice without bacteraemia (Chang et al. 2020), although a provisional 2024 abstract reports that FMT from oxaliplatin-exposed mice can transfer harm (He et al. 2024). The human evidence remains associative and tissue-based.

Irinotecan provides the clearest example of a microbial enzyme causing toxicity: bacterial GUS deconjugates the inactive hepatic metabolite SN-38G back to toxic SN-38 in the intestinal lumen, producing delayed-onset diarrhoea that limits dosing ([Yue et al. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2021.710945/full); [Gao et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11725075/)). Approximately 43% of microbial species encode Gus or candidate structures, with higher hydrolytic activity in *Bacteroides uniformis*, *Clostridium paraputrificum*, *Clostridium clostridioforme* and *E. faecalis* ([Gao et al. 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11725075/)). GUS inhibitors prevented diarrhoea and preserved antitumour efficacy in animal models and permitted dose intensification ([Cheng et al. 2019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30391354/); [Bhatt et al. 2020](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1918095117)), but no inhibitor is approved and none has been tested in a published CRC randomised trial ([Hillege et al. 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00432-024-06028-2)).

Microbial products can also be exploited therapeutically. Urolithin A potentiated 5-FU cytotoxicity in colon-cancer cell lines ([González-Sarrías et al. 2015](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25857357/)), and *L. casei* var. *rhamnosus* reduced FOLFOX intestinal toxicity in mice without compromising antitumour effect ([Chang et al. 2018](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/microbiology/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2018.00983/full)). Later-line and targeted agents are summarised in the supplementary table below; their microbiome evidence is sparse, single-centre and associative.

## Immunotherapy

ICIs are effective mainly in MSI-H/dMMR CRC (~10–15% of non-metastatic and ~5% of metastatic cases), where high tumour mutational burden underpins responsiveness; MSS/pMMR tumours are largely refractory to ICI monotherapy ([Chalabi et al. 2024](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/208879584/Beets-Tan-2024-Neoadjuvant-Immunotherapy-in-Locally-Advanced_.pdf); [ASCO Post, KEYNOTE-177](https://ascopost.com/issues/june-10-2020/pembrolizumab-doubles-progression-free-survival-in-msi-hdmmr-metastatic-colorectal-cancer/)). Standard-of-care anchors include pembrolizumab (median PFS 16.5 vs 8.2 months; ORR 43.8%) and nivolumab plus low-dose ipilimumab (ORR 69%; 13% complete responses) in MSI-H/dMMR mCRC, and neoadjuvant nivolumab plus ipilimumab (95% major pathological response in dMMR colon cancer) ([ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/issues/june-10-2020/pembrolizumab-doubles-progression-free-survival-in-msi-hdmmr-metastatic-colorectal-cancer/); [Lenz et al. 2022](https://mdanderson.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/first-line-nivolumab-plus-low-dose-ipilimumab-for-microsatellite-); [Chalabi et al. 2024](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/208879584/Beets-Tan-2024-Neoadjuvant-Immunotherapy-in-Locally-Advanced_.pdf)). The inverse relationship with chemotherapy is instructive: dMMR tumours are relatively chemo-refractory (FOxTROT pathological response ~7%) but ICI-sensitive ([Chalabi et al. 2024](https://cris.maastrichtuniversity.nl/ws/files/208879584/Beets-Tan-2024-Neoadjuvant-Immunotherapy-in-Locally-Advanced_.pdf)), so microbial determinants of ICI response cannot be inferred from chemotherapy–microbiota interactions.

At the mechanism level, the most reproducible pattern is that mucin-degrading and SCFA-producing commensals tend to support response. *Akkermansia muciniphila* is the most-cited beneficial taxon and has been proposed as an adjunct that may increase checkpoint-inhibitor effectiveness while decreasing side effects ([Faghfuri et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38471272/); [Fan et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11797418/)); *Bifidobacterium pseudolongum* enhances checkpoint response through inosine and the adenosine A2A receptor in mice ([Mager et al. 2020](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abc3421)); and polysaccharide-A-producing *B. fragilis* supports CTLA-4 blockade immunology in gnotobiotic mice ([Vétizou et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721659/)). Human cohort associations for higher alpha diversity and *Faecalibacterium*/Ruminococcaceae enrichment come mainly from melanoma, NSCLC and RCC, not CRC ([Hwang et al. 2023](http://irjournal.org/upload/pdf/ir-2023-00019.pdf)). Antibiotic exposure is the most consistent negative modifier: it inhibited the clinical benefit of PD-1 blockade in a prospective cohort ([Routy et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29097494/)).

Fn is genuinely context-dependent. In one line of work, Fn-derived butyrate sensitises MSS CRC to anti-PD-1 by inhibiting HDAC3/8, increasing H3K27 acetylation at the *Tbx21* promoter and raising TBX21, a repressor of PD-1, thereby lowering PD-1 on CD8⁺ TILs ([Wang et al. 2024](https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/fusobacterium-nucleatum-facilitates-anti-pd-1-therapy-in-microsat-2/)). In another, Fn-derived succinic acid suppresses the cGAS–interferon-β pathway and limits CD8⁺ T-cell responses, and FMT from low-Fn responders — but not Fn-high non-responders — restored anti-PD-1 sensitivity in mice ([Jiang et al. 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37130518/)). The discrepancy likely reflects strain, compartment (intratumoral vs luminal), burden and tumour context, but it is unresolved; Fn abundance should not be treated as a simple predictive biomarker.

The clinical translation effort focuses on MSS/pMMR disease, where the goal is to convert “cold” tumours into an inflamed phenotype. Responder-derived FMT plus anti-PD-1 produced objective responses in a minority of PD-1-refractory melanoma patients (ORR 20% and 3/10 responses in two phase 1 trials) and a 7.7% ORR in a mixed GI-cancer cohort ([Davar et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf3363); [Baruch et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abb5920); [Kim et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39059396/)). These are early-phase, non-randomised signals; the CRC-specific fraction is small and no randomised CRC trial exists. Defined consortia are earlier still: VE303 (an 8-strain product) reduced recurrent *C. difficile* infection versus placebo in a phase 2 study but has no CRC efficacy data ([Vedanta Bio](https://www.vedantabio.com/pipeline-programs/ve303/)). Immune-related toxicity also has a microbiome dimension: CTLA-4 blockade caused microbiota-dependent intestinal pathology in gnotobiotic mice ([Vétizou et al. 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4721659/)), and non-CRC cohorts associate Bacteroidetes enrichment with absence of colitis and Firmicutes enrichment with colitis ([Hwang et al. 2023](http://irjournal.org/upload/pdf/ir-2023-00019.pdf)).

## Radiotherapy

Radiation and the microbiome form a bidirectional loop. Abdominal/pelvic radiotherapy reduces diversity and depletes SCFA-producing commensals while enriching pro-inflammatory/pathobiont taxa ([Jian et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8358303/)); transfer of radiation-altered microbiota rendered germ-free mice susceptible to dextran-sodium-sulfate-induced colitis and radiation injury, and germ-free mice were resistant to lethal radiation enteritis, establishing a causal contribution of the dysbiotic community ([Sokol et al. 2018](https://gut.bmj.com/content/67/1/1)). Downstream mechanisms include tight-junction redistribution and LPS/TLR4–NF-κB activation, IL-1β-driven inflammation (ameliorated by the IL-1 receptor antagonist anakinra in mice), and TGF-β-mediated fibrosis in chronic injury ([Jian et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8358303/); [Sokol et al. 2018](https://gut.bmj.com/content/67/1/1); [StatPearls, Radiation Enteritis](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526032/)).

Human evidence for radiotherapy efficacy modification is limited to a 22-patient pilot in which baseline *Shuttleworthia* and predicted fatty-acid metabolism pathways were associated with tumour response, and *Bifidobacterium*/*Clostridia*/*Bacteroides* with no or mild diarrhoea ([Shi et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2020.562463/full)). A model-development study of neoadjuvant immunotherapy plus chemoradiotherapy reports predictive microbiome information, but its full text and performance metrics were not retrievable, so it cannot be treated as established ([Yang et al. 2024](https://www.cell.com/med/fulltext/S2666-6340(24)00261-7)). Concurrent fluoropyrimidine chemotherapy confounds attribution to radiation alone ([Shi et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/cellular-and-infection-microbiology/articles/10.3389/fcimb.2020.562463/full)).

Probiotics are the best-studied intervention for radiation-induced diarrhoea: pooled estimates show roughly a 40–45% relative reduction (RR 0.55–0.62), though individual trials conflict and the largest negative trial (Bifilact®) found no significant benefit ([Liu et al. 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0178870); [Wulandari et al. 2023](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/95b2/fbfe3ce6d047056268c92c75789ace12194a.pdf); [Demers et al. 2014](https://mdedge.com/b-cell-lymphoma-icymi/article/88508/oncology/probiotics-radiation-caused-diarrhea)). Mechanistically, *L. rhamnosus* GG protected intestinal epithelium from radiation injury in a TLR-2/COX-2-dependent manner ([Ciorba et al. 2012](https://gut.bmj.com/content/61/6/829)), and the same strain reduced severe diarrhoea and abdominal discomfort in a CRC population receiving 5-FU-based chemotherapy rather than pure pelvic radiotherapy ([ONS/OncohemaKey summary of Österlund et al. 2007](https://oncohemakey.com/bacteria-in-patients-treated-with-chemotherapy-and-radiation-therapy/)). No pre-cutoff human evidence compares microbiome interactions across short-course radiotherapy, long-course nCRT and total neoadjuvant therapy; these remain explicit gaps.

## Future Outlook: Probiotics, Prebiotics, and FMT

Probiotics have the largest intervention evidence base. Pooled analyses show modest reductions in all-grade chemotherapy-induced diarrhoea (RR ~0.64–0.70) and radiation-induced diarrhoea (RR ~0.55–0.62), but benefit is not uniform: it was significant only in Asian populations in one meta-analysis, multi-species formulations outperformed single strains, and several trials were negative ([Feng et al. 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/nutrition/articles/10.3389/fnut.2022.823288/full); Danis et al. 2022; Hassan et al. 2018; [Liu et al. 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0178870); [Wulandari et al. 2023](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/95b2/fbfe3ce6d047056268c92c75789ace12194a.pdf)). Effects are strain-, dose-, product- and timing-specific, and over-the-counter products vary in potency and purity ([ONS clinical resource](https://www.ons.org/clinical-tools/symptom-management-resources/chemotherapy-induced-diarrhea/probiotics); [AGA 2020](https://gastro.org/press-releases/aga-does-not-recommend-the-use-of-probiotics-for-most-digestive-conditions/)). Safety is the principal constraint: probiotic-associated bacteraemia and fungaemia have been reported in neutropenic and perioperative hosts, and routine use is not recommended in prolonged neutropenia ([Lu et al. 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8006328/)). Most consequentially, over-the-counter probiotic use was associated with lower anti-PD-1 response in melanoma, so probiotics should not be co-administered with ICIs outside a trial until this interaction is clarified ([Spencer et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaz7015)). Prebiotics and synbiotics have thinner evidence, no CRC-specific hard-endpoint RCT, and an unresolved tumour-protection concern during radiotherapy (Delemans et al. 2021; Miarons et al. 2021; [Liu et al. 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13014-020-01735-9)).

FMT has the strongest mechanistic rationale but the weakest CRC-specific clinical data. Responder-derived FMT plus anti-PD-1 overcame resistance in melanoma and produced modest signals in mixed GI cancers ([Davar et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf3363); [Baruch et al. 2021](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abb5920); [Kim et al. 2024](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39059396/)), and FMT reduced FOLFOX intestinal toxicity in mice (Chang et al. 2020), but no phase 3 CRC trial exists and engraftment remains donor-, route- and preparation-dependent. Safety is anchored in recurrent *C. difficile* infection: FDA alerts document EPEC/STEC transmission and deaths, and multidrug-resistant-organism transmission is a particular concern in immunocompromised cancer patients ([FDA, March 2020](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/safety-availability-biologics/update-march-12-2020-safety-alert-regarding-use-fecal-microbiota-transplantation-and-risk-serious); [FDA, June 2019](https://www.fda.gov/safety/medical-product-safety-information/fecal-microbiota-transplantation-safety-communication-risk-serious-adverse-reactions-due)). Approved microbiota products are limited to recurrent CDI ([FDA, April 2023](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-orally-administered-fecal-microbiota-product-prevention-recurrence-clostridioides); [Ferring, November 2022](https://www.ferring.com/ferring-receives-u-s-fda-approval-for-rebyota-fecal-microbiota-live-jslm-a-novel-first-in-class-microbiota-based-live-biotherapeutic/)). Next-generation live biotherapeutic products — defined, GMP-manufactured consortia such as VE303 — offer standardisation advantages over donor FMT but have no CRC efficacy data ([Vedanta Bio](https://www.vedantabio.com/pipeline-programs/ve303/)).

As of 30 September 2024, no microbiome-based biomarker or modulation strategy is part of standard CRC care, and major guidelines do not contain microbiome-based treatment recommendations ([NCCN](https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/guidelines-detail?id=1428); [ESMO](https://www.esmo.org/guidelines/esmo-clinical-practice-guideline-metastatic-colorectal-cancer)). The most defensible current positions are: antibiotic stewardship around ICI initiation; strain-specific probiotic discussion for chemotherapy- or radiotherapy-induced diarrhoea in non-neutropenic patients; avoidance of empiric probiotics during prolonged neutropenia, perioperative instability or ICI therapy outside trials; and referral to registered trials for FMT and defined consortia.

### What would change practice

The translational pipeline is concentrated in five priorities: (1) prospective validation of Fn abundance and alpha diversity as predictive biomarkers using standardized assays; (2) adequately powered RCTs of strain-specific probiotics for chemotherapy- and radiotherapy-induced diarrhoea with embedded microbiome endpoints; (3) completion of FMT and defined-consortium trials in MSS/pMMR CRC with immune and clinical endpoints; (4) prospective testing of antibiotic-stewardship protocols around ICI initiation; and (5) development of clinically deployable microbiome assays and aligned regulatory pathways. Until these are completed, clinicians should confine probiotic recommendations to evidence-supported indications and direct patients toward registered trials for FMT and live biotherapeutics.

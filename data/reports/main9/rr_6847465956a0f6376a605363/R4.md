# Turo vs. Getaround: Two Car-Sharing Startups, Two Very Different Fates, 2020–2025

## First, the vocabulary

This report uses a handful of financial terms that are easy to confuse. They matter because the two companies' stories are mostly stories about them.

- **Valuation** is an estimated price tag on a company. For a **private** company (one whose shares are not traded on a stock exchange), the tag is set only when investors actually buy shares in a **funding round** (Series A, B, C…). It is not money the company has in the bank. A **public** company's **market capitalization** is its share price multiplied by its shares outstanding, set daily by the stock market ([CFI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/resources/valuation/market-capitalization/)).
- A **unicorn** is a private startup valued above $1 billion. **Post-money valuation** is a company's value after a new cash investment is added ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/p/postmoneyvaluation.asp)). Each new round **dilutes** earlier owners — they own a smaller slice of the same pie.
- **Cash runway** is how long a company's money lasts; **burn** is its spending rate. A **secondary-market sale** trades existing shares between investors without raising new money for the company ([Clay.com](https://www.clay.com/dossier/turo-funding)).
- A **SPAC** is a listed shell company that raises money from public investors and merges with a private business to give it a faster route to the stock market than a traditional **IPO** (initial public offering, a first public share sale) ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/terms/s/spac.asp)).
- In car-sharing, **gross booking value (GBV)** is the total price of trips booked, including the car owner's share; **revenue** is what the platform keeps. The **take rate** is revenue as a share of GBV. **Gross margin** is revenue left after the direct costs of bookings, chiefly insurance and protection programs.

The central fact for readers unfamiliar with venture finance: **valuation is not cash.** Paper wealth does not pay payroll, and a company can be profitable while its headline value stays flat.

## The two companies

Both operate **peer-to-peer (P2P)** marketplaces where ordinary people list their own cars for short trips; the platform handles booking, payment, insurance and screening for a commission. Turo, founded in 2010 and backed by IAC, became the larger of the two. Getaround, founded in 2009 and backed by SoftBank's Vision Fund, was its closest rival. Both entered 2020 valued around $1 billion or more — and both were forced to cut staff that year when COVID-19 stopped travel. From there, their paths diverged sharply: Turo stayed private, grew into profitability, and withdrew its IPO; Getaround went public through a SPAC at the peak of the market, watched its stock collapse, and wound down its entire U.S. business.

## Turo, 2020–2025

### 2020: a unicorn that still had to fire people

Turo entered 2020 as a private unicorn. In July 2019, IAC led a $250 million **Series E** (a late-stage venture round) that pushed Turo past $1 billion and made IAC its largest shareholder at roughly 31%. In February 2020, Turo added $30 million to that round at a reported **~$1.24 billion post-money valuation**, broadly flat with the 2019 mark ([Clay.com](https://www.clay.com/dossier/turo-funding); [Forge Global](https://forgeglobal.com/turo_ipo/)).

When COVID-19 shut down travel in March 2020, demand collapsed. Turo laid off **108 employees — about 30% of staff** — across San Francisco and Phoenix, saying consumers were no longer renting cars because of shelter-in-place orders ([Layoffs.fyi](https://layoffs.fyi/2020/04/21/turo-laid-off-108-employees/)). The cut was a cash-preservation move: revenue had stalled, and payroll was one of the largest costs.

Per Turo's IPO filing, 2020 net revenue was $149.9 million, up just 6% from $141.7 million in 2019, and the net loss was $97.1 million ([TechCrunch, Jan. 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/turos-s-1-filing-shows-that-unicorns-can-slingshot-out-of-the-pandemic/); [SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000119312522005696/d145731ds1.htm)). A widely cited ~$98.6 million figure is actually the 2019 loss from the same filing, not 2020's.

### 2021–2022: the travel boom, first profits, and expansion

The rebound came in 2021. During lockdown some hosts parked or exited their fleets; as vaccines rolled out and traditional rental chains ran short of cars, hosts returned and many expanded to meet surging demand ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2021/08/10/an-auto-rental-shortage-sparked-a-boom-in-peer-to-peer-car-sharing-.html)). Turo's 2021 net revenue jumped 213% to $469.0 million and the net loss narrowed to $40.4 million ([TechCrunch, Mar. 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/24/turo-just-dropped-its-2022-financials-as-its-ipo-hunt-continues/)). In the first nine months of 2021, days booked rose 163% and gross booking value per day rose 40% ([TechCrunch, Jan. 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/01/11/turos-s-1-filing-shows-that-unicorns-can-slingshot-out-of-the-pandemic/)).

On January 10, 2022, Turo filed its **S-1** — the SEC form for an IPO — to list on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol "TURO," with Morgan Stanley, J.P. Morgan, Allen & Company and Citi as bookrunners, on an estimated $300 million deal ([SEC S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000119312522005696/d145731ds1.htm); [Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/109393/car-sharing-platform-turo-withdraws-estimated-300-million-ipo)). That June it acquired French competitor **OuiCar** for undisclosed terms and launched in **New York** on June 27, becoming the first peer-to-peer car-sharing marketplace in all 50 states ([Auto Futures](https://www.autofutures.tv/topics/peer-to-peer-car-sharing-marketplace-turo-expands-into-france-by-acquiring-ouicar/s/4e2cffbc-14f8-4507-b01b-0f994382bad5)). New York had been closed since 2013 by insurance disputes and, before that exit, had represented about 20% of Turo's business ([Auto Rental News](https://www.autorentalnews.com/news/turo-returns-to-new-york-v2)).

Financially, 2022 brought revenue of $746.6 million (up 59%) and reported net income of $154.7 million — though that profit was flattered by a non-cash gain from revaluing a **warrant liability** (a right to buy shares at a set price, marked to market each quarter), while 2021's loss included the opposite charge ([Turo S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024024022/turoinc-sx1a10.htm)). In other words, the swing owed more to accounting than to operations.

Throughout this period, no new funding round reset Turo's headline valuation; it stayed pinned near the ~$1.24 billion 2019/2020 mark. Because no exchange sets a daily price for a private company, its paper value only changes when investors actually buy shares. Forge records an August 29, 2023 **secondary-market round** — a sale of existing private shares, not new company money — but no disclosed price ([Forge Global](https://forgeglobal.com/turo_ipo/)).

### 2023–2024: growth cools

Turo's growth decelerated sharply. Revenue rose from $469 million in 2021 to $746.6 million in 2022 (+59%), then to $879.7 million in 2023 (+18%), and to about $958 million in 2024 (+9%) ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/turo-at-958m-revenue/)). Reported net income was $14.7 million in 2023 and roughly $26 million in 2024. Gross margin was 51% in 2023, then fell to 46% in 2024 as marketing and operating costs rose ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/turo-at-958m-revenue/)). Gross booking value was about $2.41 billion in 2023 and $2.5 billion in 2024, of which Turo paid hosts about $1.5 billion — gross bookings dwarf revenue because most of each rental's value goes to the car owner ([DWU Consulting](https://dwuconsulting.com/dwu-ai/turo-and-peer-to-peer-car-sharing); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/turo-at-958m-revenue/)).

Private-market trackers continued to estimate Turo well above the 2019/2020 baseline — Forge data implied roughly $1.7 billion post-money at a September 2024 round, and Caplight data cited by AccessIPOs put the private valuation near $2.2 billion in early 2025 ([Forge Global](https://forgeglobal.com/turo_ipo/); [AccessIPOs](https://accessipos.com/turo-ipo-stock/)). These are indicative estimates, not transactions. The best-supported primary-round mark remained about $1.24 billion.

### February 2025: the IPO that never happened

After three years of amendments and no pricing, Turo **withdrew its IPO registration on February 13, 2025**, forgoing the planned cash raise and the public-market exit for employees and early investors. The company said only that it "does not wish to conduct a public offering of securities at this time"; CEO Andre Haddad said the board had decided "now is not the right time" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/13/turo-scraps-plans-for-an-ipo/); [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/public/shrinking-ipo-pipeline-unicorn-turo-hits-pause/)). The trigger was decelerating growth, in a frozen IPO market: from 2022 the Federal Reserve lifted interest rates to 5%–5.25%, and there were 18 months without a notable venture-backed tech IPO through mid-2023 ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/06/29/tech-ipo-drought-reaches-18-months-despite-nasdaq-first-half-rally.html)).

Two months later, in April 2025, Turo cut **about 150 employees — roughly 15% of a workforce it said was nearly 1,000**, implying about 850 remained. Haddad tied the cuts to "ongoing economic uncertainty" and strengthening the company for long-term growth, while saying Turo "remains in a strong financial position" ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/tech/article/turo-lays-offs-150-workers-economic-uncertainty-20281905.php)). Its S-1/A had disclosed 931 full-time employees at the end of 2023 ([SEC S-1/A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1514587/000162828024008965/turoinc-sx1a9.htm)). The cut was a cost-and-timing decision, not a response to a falling valuation: because Turo stayed private, no exchange repriced its stock.

## Getaround, 2020–2025

### 2020–2021: layoffs, a funding round, and a regulatory fine

Getaround entered 2020 as a heavily funded private company, with tracker estimates placing its 2019 valuation between $1.5 billion and $1.7 billion ([TechCrunch, Sept. 2019](https://techcrunch.com/2019/09/05/softbank-backed-getaround-is-raising-200m-at-a-1-5b-valuation/)). That January it laid off roughly **150 employees — about a quarter of staff** — to cut costs, with CEO Sam Zaid citing SoftBank's portfolio-wide push for cash-flow discipline ([TechCrunch, Jan. 2020](https://techcrunch.com/2020/01/07/getaround-is-latest-softbank-portfolio-company-to-announce-layoffs/)). A further ~25% layoff round followed in March 2020 ([San Francisco Business Times](https://www.bizjournals.com/sanfrancisco/news/2020/03/27/getaround-issues-another-layoff-round-as-it-seeks.html)). COVID-19 then drove usage down as much as 75%, before revenue recovered to more than double the pre-COVID baseline by October 2020 ([Getaround press release, Oct. 2020](https://www.braemarenergy.com/news/pr/2020/10/getaround-series_e-201014.html); [Reuters, Aug. 2021](https://wtbx.com/2021/08/25/exclusive-softbank-backed-getaround-in-spac-merger-talks-sources/)).

In October 2020 Getaround raised a **$140 million Series E** led by PeopleFund, bringing total raised to "nearly $600 million"; the announcement did not disclose a valuation ([Getaround press release, Oct. 2020](https://www.braemarenergy.com/news/pr/2020/10/getaround-series_e-201014.html)). In July 2021 the D.C. Attorney General announced a **$950,000 settlement**, alleging Getaround misrepresented its safety features, used "phony owner profiles" for fleet cars, and failed to pay sales tax ([OAG DC](https://oag.dc.gov/release/ag-racine-announces-car-sharing-company-getaround)). In August 2021, Reuters reported exclusive talks to merge with a SPAC, Altitude Acquisition Corp., at a reported ~$1.7 billion valuation — which the company did not confirm ([Reuters, Aug. 2021](https://wtbx.com/2021/08/25/exclusive-softbank-backed-getaround-in-spac-merger-talks-sources/)).

### 2022: the $1.2 billion SPAC and the first-day collapse

Getaround's 2022 flotation shows how fast a headline valuation can be repriced. On May 11, 2022, it agreed to merge with **InterPrivate II Acquisition Corp.**, whose trust held $259 million. The deal valued the combined company at about **$1.2 billion** "assuming no redemptions" — no SPAC investors pulling their cash out — and projected gross proceeds of at least $225 million and up to $434 million ([SEC merger release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839608/000119312522147020/d289503dex991.htm); [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/News/2022/05/Orrick-Advises-Getaround-on-Billion-SPAC-Merger)).

The December 9, 2022 closing was far weaker. SPAC investors holding about **69% of InterPrivate II's shares redeemed** — cashed out — leaving only about $80 million from the trust. Getaround raised about **$228 million before expenses**, mostly convertible notes and short-term bridge debt, well below the $434 million maximum ([Silicon Valley Business Journal](https://sg.headtopics.com/news/getaround-s-value-drops-76-after-completing-spac-merger-silicon-valley-business-journal-32841876)). The stock then collapsed: GETR closed its first day **66% below** InterPrivate II's final pre-merger close and fell another 32% the next Monday — a two-day drop of about 76% — leaving a market capitalization of roughly **$241 million**, about one-fifth of the $1.2 billion deal value ([Silicon Valley Business Journal](https://sg.headtopics.com/news/getaround-s-value-drops-76-after-completing-spac-merger-silicon-valley-business-journal-32841876)). The 2022 tech selloff made SPAC investors eager to exit; 13 of 15 Bay Area companies that listed via SPAC that year lost over half their value.

The fall reflected heavy losses. In 2022 Getaround booked $59.5 million of revenue against a net loss of $136.1 million, burned $132.5 million of operating cash, and ended the year with $64.6 million of cash and $80.9 million of debt ([StockAnalysis, aggregating SEC filings](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/GETR/financials/)).

### 2023–2024: falling share price, delisting, and repeated layoffs

Unlike private Turo, Getaround's value was continuously marked by the stock market — and the market marked it down, triggering a self-reinforcing decline. On **February 2, 2023**, Getaround laid off about **10% of staff — roughly 42 of 421 employees**, all in North American teams, to save $25–30 million annually; the cut came one day after the NYSE warned the stock had traded too low, with shares closing at $0.64 ([TechCrunch, Feb. 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/02/car-sharing-spac-getaround-lays-off-10-of-staff/)). In May 2023 it bought substantially all of **HyreCar's assets** for a reported $9.45 million after HyreCar entered bankruptcy, adding gig-carsharing supply ([Businesswire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230516005889/en/Getaround-Closes-Acquisition-of-HyreCar-Assets)).

FY2023 revenue was $72.7 million and the net loss before taxes narrowed to $113.9 million; third-quarter 2023 revenue grew 42% year over year. Headcount was about 290 at the end of 2023 ([Getaround FY2023 results via Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/getaround-reports-full-year-2023-results-2024-03-28); [Getaround 2023 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839608/000095017024038301/getr-20231231.htm)). In January 2024, Getaround took a $20 million rescue investment from Mudrick Capital, which also gained a board chair ([Clay.com](https://www.clay.com/dossier/getaround-funding)). On **February 7, 2024**, it cut roughly **30% of its North American workforce**, saying it needed to reach profitability ([MarketWatch](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/getaround-will-lay-off-30-of-its-workforce-to-further-reduce-costs-112c5c71); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/08/car-sharing-company-getaround-cuts-one-third-of-u-s-workforce/)).

The decisive break came in July 2024. On July 9, the NYSE told Getaround it would begin delisting proceedings because the company failed **Rule 802.01B**, the exchange's requirement to keep an average global market capitalization of at least **$15 million over 30 consecutive trading days**. Trading was suspended July 10, and Getaround said its shares would move to **OTC Markets**, where disclosure and liquidity are weaker ([Getaround Q3 2024 Form 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839608/000095017024126782/getr-20240930.htm); [NYSE press release](https://ir.theice.com/press/news-details/2024/NYSE-to-Commence-Delisting-Proceedings-Against-Getaround-Inc.-GETR/default.aspx)). The formal delisting took effect August 16, and the stock later traded on the OTCQB market as GETR. Reported market capitalization was roughly $24 million during 2024 and about $12 million at the delisting ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getaround); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/getaround-to-appeal-nyse-delisting-trades-on-otc-markets-93CH-3513784)). An April 2024 proxy sought approval for a **reverse stock split** (a move that reduces the number of shares and lifts the share price without changing the company's value), but none was implemented before delisting ([Getaround DEF 14A](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1839608/000114036124032420/ny20031467x2_def14a.htm)).

By Q3 2024, revenue of $22.4 million was slightly below the prior year, mainly because Getaround suspended its New York State operations; the net loss narrowed to $15.5 million, and cash rose to $30.8 million. AJ Lee became interim CEO ([Getaround Q3 2024 results via StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/GETR/getaround-reports-third-quarter-2024-financial-wvedc64aodrd.html)). The chain was clear: a low share price and sub-$15 million market cap led to delisting, delisting reduced access to public capital, and reduced capital access forced deeper cuts.

### February 2025: the U.S. business shuts down

Getaround's U.S. business ended on **February 11, 2025**, when the company announced an "orderly wind-down" of all U.S. operations — its peer-to-peer marketplace and the HyreCar fleet business — while keeping the European unit running. New U.S. rentals stopped immediately. Interim CEO AJ Lee said that "despite significant improvements in overall profitability and extensive restructuring efforts, the Company has faced an ongoing lack of liquidity which has made U.S. operations no longer viable" ([Getaround press release, Feb. 11, 2025](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/getaround-inc-announces-wind-down-us-operations-2025-02-11)). **Liquidity** means cash on hand to pay immediate bills. Getaround had burned through its SPAC cash; its stock had collapsed to **$0.036725 per share**, making new equity raises heavily dilutive, and it owed substantial debt to Mudrick Capital ([8-K / Prospectus Supplement No. 8](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001839608/da25a9bb-e702-4ea7-86ed-42651a2c17d0.pdf)).

The 8-K says Getaround cut "**substantially all**" of its U.S. employees, most separated on February 14, 2025, with $1.5–$2.0 million in expected severance charges; the exact U.S. headcount was not disclosed ([8-K](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001839608/da25a9bb-e702-4ea7-86ed-42651a2c17d0.pdf)). European employees were explicitly unaffected, and the European business stayed headquartered in Paris. At the February 2025 price and roughly 127.5 million shares registered that month, the equity was worth on the order of **$5 million** — about 99.6% below the $1.2 billion SPAC valuation ([Getaround press release](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/getaround-inc-announces-wind-down-us-operations-2025-02-11)).

## Why the outcomes diverged

The divergence was driven less by the two apps than by **when each reached public markets** and **how much profit each booking generated**.

Turo stayed private through 2022–2023, so its reported $1.24 billion valuation was set only in negotiated funding and secondary sales, not by a stock exchange ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/turo/); [Clay.com](https://www.clay.com/dossier/turo-funding)). With no exchange repricing its shares daily, Turo could wait out the frozen IPO window and withdraw its registration in February 2025, spared the delisting spiral that drove Getaround's repeated layoffs. The trade-off: private valuations are sticky and illiquid, so a $1.2 billion paper valuation can coexist with employees unable to sell their shares.

Getaround, backed by SoftBank's Vision Fund, merged with a SPAC at a $1.2 billion equity value in December 2022, at the peak of the SPAC boom. The public market repriced the stock within days. The 2022 rate-hike and tech selloff left newly public companies nowhere to hide ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/09/23/stock-market-ipos-went-from-boom-to-bust-in-2022.html)), and Getaround's $228 million of SPAC proceeds were absorbed by losses, forcing a rescue from Mudrick Capital and eventually the U.S. wind-down ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/GETR/getaround-reports-full-year-2023-jpbc5nn2cqi8.html); [Clay.com](https://www.clay.com/dossier/getaround-funding)).

Unit economics explain the rest. Turo's 2024 revenue (~$958 million, ~46% gross margin, ~$26 million net profit) was roughly thirteen times Getaround's, and profitable ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/turo-at-958m-revenue/)). Turo also scaled supply faster: its U.S. share tripled from 2% to 6% in 2019–2021 ([Bloomberg Second Measure](https://secondmeasure.com/datapoints/top-car-rental-companies-competitors-turo-getaround-hertz-avis-enterprise/)), aided by the 2022 OuiCar acquisition and New York launch.

## What happened to employees

**Turo's cuts were smaller and tied to cash needs and IPO timing.** It cut 108 employees (30%) in March 2020 and about 150 (15%) in April 2025 — roughly 258 documented cuts — despite being profitable from 2022 onward ([SFGate](https://www.sfgate.com/tech/article/turo-lays-offs-150-workers-economic-uncertainty-20281905.php)). Payroll is one of a young company's largest costs, so even a healthy company trims staff when growth slows. Because Turo stayed private, its IPO withdrawal removed the public-market event that would have let employees sell their shares, leaving their equity priced only in private secondary markets.

**Getaround's cuts escalated into a full U.S. shutdown.** After the January and March 2020 layoffs, it cut about 10% of staff in February 2023, 30% of its North American workforce in February 2024, and substantially all U.S. employees in February 2025 — its largest cut — winding down its U.S. and HyreCar operations while keeping Europe ([Getaround, Feb. 11, 2025](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/getaround-inc-announces-wind-down-us-operations-2025-02-11)). Its collapse destroyed public-market equity value, and the 2025 wind-down left that equity close to worthless.

The lesson for newcomers to venture finance: valuation is not cash; private companies can hide distress longer than public ones; SPACs can accelerate a collapse; and employees' equity is often worth far less than the headline number suggests.
